# Synthesis bench — design notes (2026-10-19)

The next app bench. It groups most of the gesture techniques and lets Ian
compose with them himself, down to the substructure. Ian, 2026-10-19: "a full
on bench (surpassing any other we have created in complexity), that allows me
to partake in the process of composition, change stuff in the substructure,
maybe add a picture to serve as an unseen base."

Read with: the [inventory](inventory-2026-10-19.md) (122 techniques, 11
families, 48 relations), the [study bench v3 spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-19-study-bench-v3.md)
(the interaction this bench inherits), the
[round 15 review](../../tools/gesture/results/round15-review-2026-10-19.md)
(awareness is the axis). Decisions Ian made on the open questions are in §11.

---

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

Today a composer is a program that draws a whole sheet and, at best, writes
down afterwards what it did (the round-15 score). The synthesis bench turns
that around: **the score is the program.** A score is a list of layers and
the relations between them. An executor plays it. Composers become writers of
scores, Ian edits the score they wrote (or starts one himself), and every
relation that fires is logged by the executor, the same way for every
technique. The score Ian edits and the record the devices read become one
object in two states: *written* and *played*.

---

## 2. What exists, and the two problems

The kit has 32 voices, 27 modulators, 24 renderers, 26 arrangers and 13
devices. The inventory groups them into 11 families (F1–F11) and finds 48
relations already implemented, most of them buried inside composers.

**Problem 1: relations are recorded in only 5 files** (arch, listen,
lcorp lens, lcorp relay, meas_boxes), and what they record is observation:
dicts appended after the fact, not operations that can be re-run. Arch
collapses carry and reverse into ride; standalone sympathy discards its log.
Ian can't edit what isn't there.

**Problem 2: composers own the whole sheet.** mutual, the four round-14
composers, arch, listen, series and stages each decide the frame, the order
and the blank. They take the whole paper and return finished marks, so two of
them can't share a sheet, and none can be one part of a larger piece. listen,
for example, keeps its own private `Layer` objects and raw arrays and only
turns them into kit `Mark`s at the very end.

Both problems have one fix: move the layer list, the order and the relation
bookkeeping out of the composers and into a shared executor.

## 3. The score, version 2 (executable)

```json
{"version": 2, "frame": [250, 175], "seed": 16001,
 "base": null,
 "layers": [
   {"id": "L0", "role": "found", "pen": "blue",
    "part": {"voice": {"id": "oehlen_found_river"}}, "region": null},
   {"id": "L1", "role": "speaker", "pen": "black",
    "part": {"voice": {"id": "hand", "hand": "dance_thrusting"},
             "modulators": [], "renderer": {"id": "wire"}}},
   {"id": "L2", "role": "listener", "pen": "red",
    "part": {"voice": {"id": "hand", "hand": "trail_miner"}}},
   {"id": "L3", "role": "sealed", "pen": "purple", "region": [150, 20, 90, 70],
    "composer": {"id": "ccolour", "density": 0.5}}
 ],
 "relations": [
   {"id": "R0", "kind": "imitate", "src": "L1", "dst": "L2", "p": 1.0, "params": {"len_mm": [55, 120]}},
   {"id": "R1", "kind": "sympathy", "src": "L0", "dst": "L2", "params": {"reach": 4.0, "gain": 1.0}},
   {"id": "R2", "kind": "crossing", "src": "*", "dst": "*",
    "params": {"weights": {"gap_under": 0.3, "gap_over": 0.3, "cross": 0.25, "stop": 0.15}, "streak": 0.3}}
 ],
 "devices": [{"id": "meas_arcs", "place": "beside"}],
 "decisions": {"density": 0.5, "order": ["L0", "L1", "L2", "L3"]}}
```

- **Layers** are drawn in list order, which is also plot order. Each is a
  kit part (voice + modulators + renderer, unchanged), a role, a pen and an
  optional region. A `sealed` layer is a whole composer confined to a region
  (§6).
- **Relations** name a src and a dst layer (`*` = every pair), a kind, a
  probability `p` and params. Their kinds come from a registry (§5).
- **The played log** is written by the executor, never by the technique:
  every firing as `{relation, kind, src, dst, at, s, outcome}`. Devices read
  it; the arc diagram draws it. The v1 `Score` keeps working as a reader of
  old sheets.
- **Determinism:** each relation draws from `rng(seed, relation id, event
  index)`, events ordered by arc length, never by set or hash order. Editing
  one relation re-rolls only that relation.

**The executor**, per layer in order: grow the layer (its part, with its
growth relations reading earlier layers); apply contact relations between it
and the layers already on the sheet; log. Then devices, last. Every layer's
result is cached by a hash of its spec plus the hashes of what it listens to,
so an edit to a late layer re-runs only that layer and the ones after it.
Measured today: listen draws a whole sheet in 0.6–0.7 s, arch in 2.2–2.5 s,
so live preview is in reach without the cache; the cache keeps it there as
scores grow.

## 4. Families and their shared controls

The bench shows techniques by what they do in a composition, not by kit kind.
Six groups, built from the inventory's families. Each group has a few shared
controls that every member maps onto; a technique's own params sit under
"more". The mapping is one lead-owned table (`kit/_families.py`), not edits
to each technique.

| # | Group | Inventory families | Shared controls | Keepers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lines (what draws) | F1 grammar lines, F2 found paths, F3 structured voices | source (hand / route / found), length, scale, seed | drift, morph_handover, revisit_glyph, river_zoom, found river, series travel, dance |
| 2 | Ink (how it inks) | F4 regime channels, F5 renderers | pen (colour + width), weight (wire → nib → mass budget), roundness, stress threshold | nib, swell, trail_gallery, arch_ink, revisit_nib, danced timing |
| 3 | Relations (how layers know each other) | F7 conversation/awareness, F8 ground negotiation, the listen vocabulary | probability, reach, gain, delay, outcome weights | sympathy, weave/yield, imitate, exaggerate, translate, echo, mask, ride/catch, answer |
| 4 | Composers (who proposes) | F9 | density, blank, relation budget, palette, frame, source pool | listen, arch, ccolour; oehlen_found, rules |
| 5 | Placement | F6 | region, common scale, margin | poly_page, series_row |
| 6 | Measure (optional layers) | F10 analytic companions, F11 general devices | place, side and size, strength, labels | ledger, arcs, inventory, air, ruler, isoline, rose, misfit grid |

What goes in: keepers and liked techniques are in the palette; meh ones sit
behind "more"; duds (the drivers, the intuition replay, solid script) stay in
the kit but not on the bench. Ian's verdicts in the inventory decide which is
which.

The inventory's repeated mechanisms (§4.1 there) are not merged. The four
ways a line changes hands (belt, gmorph, morph_handover, passage, accrue)
stay distinct, because "distinct grammars kept whole, never averaged" applies
to methods too. The bench groups them so they are found together.

## 5. Relations, and awareness as a first-class relation

A relation is a registered function, like the kit's four kinds:

```python
@relation("sympathy", phase="contact", params=[P("reach", "float", 4.0, "mm", min=0.5, max=20.0),
                                              P("gain", "float", 1.0, "", min=0.0, max=3.0)])
def sympathy(rctx, src: list[Mark], dst: list[Mark], *, reach, gain):
    ...
    return new_dst, events        # events → the played log
```

Two phases:

- **grow** relations shape how the dst layer is drawn from the src: imitate,
  exaggerate, translate, echo, refuse (detour), ride, catch, answer, nest,
  bind. They run when the dst is drawn and read only earlier layers.
- **contact** relations resolve where two layers meet after both exist:
  crossing, sympathy, mask, depth. They can change either side (a mask cuts
  layers drawn before it, as in listen).

**Awareness is the default contact relation, not an effect.** Every new
score carries one `crossing` relation over `* → *`, so every pair of layers is
aware unless Ian removes it. Ian's notes ask for two things the current code
doesn't do:

1. **Crossings that vary unpredictably** ("not always make gaps … have them
   go above and below unpredictably"). Today weave alternates strictly and
   yield always breaks the later line. The new crossing draws each crossing's
   outcome separately from weights: `gap_under` (this line breaks), `gap_over`
   (the other breaks), `cross` (both run through), `stop` (one ends at the
   other), `sympathy` (the near pass bends with the far one). A `streak`
   param lets an outcome repeat a few times before it changes, so it reads as
   behaviour, not noise.
2. **Sympathy between any layers** ("this is the type of awareness I want").
   It exists only inside lcorp, between passes of one zooming line, and only
   from farther to nearer zoom. As a relation it takes any src and dst: the dst,
   where it runs over or along the src, takes on the src's twist, faded by
   distance. Depth (who is "in front") becomes a param instead of the zoom
   channel.

Porting order (round 16 thread A does the first three): crossing,
sympathy, mask/depth; then listen's eight grow kinds; then ride/catch/release
from oehlen_found and rules; answer from arch and the chorus. Each ported
relation must redraw a sheet its old composer made, close enough to judge
side by side. Bit parity isn't needed.

## 6. Composers as score writers (problem 2)

A composer stops drawing and starts writing. It receives:

- a **region** (the whole frame, or a box or polygon), and
- the **ground**: the layers already on the sheet, as marks plus an
  occupancy raster,

and returns a score fragment: layers and relations inside its region, plus
its decisions. The executor draws it. So two composers can share a sheet
(listen in one region, arch in another, crossings between them handled by
the same `crossing` relation), and a composer can write onto a ground Ian
started.

Porting is gradual. listen goes first: it already thinks in layers and
relations. Then arch, then ccolour. A composer that isn't ported yet is still
usable as a **sealed layer**: it draws its own marks inside its region, exactly
as now, and the executor treats the result as one layer that others can
listen to and cross. Its own private v1 score is kept as that layer's
annotation. Nothing is lost by waiting.

**Composer proposes, Ian edits.** "Compose" asks the chosen composer for a
whole score within the locks; Ian edits layers and relations; "Recompose"
asks again around what he locked. Ian chose this (§11); a next-move mode
is left for later (S5).

## 7. The unseen base

Something that shapes the drawing but is never drawn. Ian, answering §11:
"import a pic and somehow extract lines, or import a layer or the sum of
layers in the canvas." So the base is **lines**, from one of three sources:

- **a picture**: an image asset, run through the research extractor
  (`extract_hybrid`, the bake-off winner) into strokes with width;
- **one canvas layer**: its resolved geometry;
- **the whole canvas**: the sum of the project's layers, flattened.

Canvas sources are **captured once** (snapshot input, the pattern the A/B
capture already uses and the ROADMAP measured as cheap), with a visible
"recapture" when the canvas has changed. Never a live link: a live link would
make the generator read the project it belongs to, which is the cycle the
single resolve path forbids.

In the score the base is the layer **`base`**, role `unseen`: it sits in the layer
list, it can be any relation's src, it is never inked or plotted. Lines treat
it like any other layer: ride its lines, stop at them, refuse them, take on
their twist (sympathy), cross them with the varied outcomes of §5. Because
its lines are only heard, the drawing carries the base's structure without
showing it. Its tone (from a picture) is also available as a field for
density or regime. A device can document it beside the drawing (its
extracted lines, faint, in the margin), which keeps it out of the drawing
while still measuring it.

Policy applies to the base as to any source: behaviours, never forms; a
picture of devotional script stays measure-only.

## 8. Measuring devices as optional layers

Already contracted in `kit/_devices.py`. In the synthesis bench:

- devices sit in their own section of the score, always after the drawing,
  off by default, each on its own pen (the bench v3 Measure layer);
- process devices read the **played log**, so the arc diagram shows exactly
  the relations that fired, and the ledger lists which layer each line came
  from;
- the round-15 notes on placement carry over: one or two devices per drawing,
  numbers that don't collide, crosshairs only with a margin, the misfit grid
  light or partial over dense sheets.

## 9. Interaction: what carries over from study bench v3

| v3 move | In the synthesis bench |
|---|---|
| Alive controls (`when`, dimmed with a reason) | the same, per layer and per relation; a relation that can't fire (no crossing between its layers) says so |
| Dice | per control, per layer (re-roll its seed or hand), per relation (re-roll its outcomes), per sheet |
| Locks | three levels: a control; a **layer** (pins its geometry, so edits upstream don't move it); a **relation** |
| Shuffle | varies every unlocked control; **Recompose** varies the structure (the composer rewrites unlocked layers and relations) |
| History strip | whole-score snapshots with their previews; any one can be branched from |
| Gallery | starting points: the 31 studies (listen and arch open as their written scores, the others as one sealed layer) and saved syntheses |
| Pen-colour previews, caches | the same; the per-layer cache (§3) makes late edits fast |

**Editing the substructure** has two editors over the same score (Ian chose
both):

- a **layer stack** beside the canvas: one row per layer (role, technique,
  pen, lock, dice); under each row, chips for the relations into it
  ("imitates L1", "crosses all: gap / over / cross"). Clicking a chip opens
  its params; clicking a layer highlights its lines on the canvas.
- the **arc diagram** as an editor: the layers along a line in drawing
  order, relations as arcs (the meas_arcs figure he called "spectacular").
  Drag from one layer to another to add a relation, click an arc to edit it,
  drag a layer along the line to change the order.

Selecting in one selects in the other. The played log can be overlaid on the
arcs, so a relation that never fired shows as a dashed arc.

Possible later, not first: drawing a layer by hand with the app's draw tool,
so Ian's own line is a layer the others listen to.

## 10. Where it lives in the app

- **Research first.** The executor (`kit/_synth.py`), the relation registry
  and the ported relations land in the kit, with tests and images, like any
  technique. The bench is built only when that works.
- **One generator.** The app gets one source, frozen from HEAD like Gesture
  Studies, whose main param is the score (bounded JSON). The bench is its
  editor.
- **Keep as a group.** Keep makes one app layer per score layer, each on its
  pen, all resolving the same score and showing one layer (the chorus
  pattern in `gesture_studies_session.py`), plus a Measure layer when
  devices are on. One undo step. A kept group can be reopened in the bench.
- **The resolve path doesn't change.** Relations live inside the score, inside
  one generator's params. App layers still resolve one by one through the
  single path. This deliberately **does not** settle the app's open node
  question (ROADMAP "Far / undecided"): the score is a small graph inside
  one generator, not a graph of project layers. If Ian later wants relations
  between arbitrary app layers (a gesture layer listening to an imported
  SVG), that is the node question's first concrete workflow and should be
  decided there.

## 11. Open questions (for Ian) and decisions

Asked through the questions UI with the lead's recommendation first.
Answers recorded here.

1. **Where the score lives.** Recommended: inside one synthesis generator,
   kept as a group (§10). Alternative: relations between real app layers
   (forces the node question).
2. **Editing surface.** Recommended: layer stack with relation chips, the arc
   diagram as a view (§9). Alternatives: the arc diagram as the editor; a
   node graph.
3. **The base.** Recommended: an unseen layer that relations can name (§7).
   Alternatives: a field only (density, regime, zoom); left out of the first
   version.
4. **Who starts.** Recommended: the composer proposes, Ian edits and locks
   (§6). Alternatives: Ian builds layer by layer and the composer suggests a
   next move; both from the start.

**Decisions (Ian, 2026-10-19):**

1. Inside one generator, kept as a group.
2. Both: the layer stack and the arc diagram, as two editors of one score.
3. The base is lines: extracted from an imported picture, or captured from
   one canvas layer or the whole canvas (§7).
4. The composer proposes; Ian edits, locks and recomposes.
