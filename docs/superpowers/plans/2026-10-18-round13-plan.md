## The two axes

**Axis I: interlocking regimes.** Several regimes share one surface and are
aware of one another. A line can be captured by another regime, borrow it,
become it, ignore it, or refuse it, and then go its own way somewhere else.
What matters is the relation, never the shape. Indifference counts as a
relation too.

**Axis II: gradual regime and hand change along a line.** One line morphs its
gesture type as it goes: the hairline that widens into a mine, the belt whose
bends tighten, the wire that becomes a nib. The change has to be readable as
a process: it is gradual, it has direction, and it can be seen on the paper.

The two axes meet when a change of regime along a line (II) is caused by what
the line meets (I). That is the found-ground river ride.

## Phase 1: experiments (parallel, one approach per agent)

Run 3–4 approaches per axis, each pushed deep. Each agent delivers one finished
piece, at most 4 supporting images, and a short write-up with its read. Every
agent gets the round-12 common brief (`results/round12-brief-2026-10-17.md`,
updated to 13) plus these Ian quotes.

Candidate approaches. These are starting points to choose from or replace:

Axis I:
- **I-a. Rules from the sheets.** Study the 29 Oehlen works on paper
  (`.local/gesture/corpus/artists/albert_oehlen/`, with research A1–A7). Write
  candidate unnamed rules as falsifiable hypotheses: when a layer is ridden or
  ignored, when something is covered, how a line enters a blank, what follows
  what. Implement them as breakable rules. Breaking a rule is an event too.
- **I-b. Awareness fields.** Each regime projects an influence (capture radius,
  alignment, appetite, fatigue). The moving line decides event by event. One
  regime's state changes another's appetite, so awareness runs both ways.
- **I-c. Contagion.** Touching a regime infects the line with that regime's
  grammar for a while: it becomes the river, then the graph. The infection
  decays or mutates, and the line may carry it to a place where it doesn't
  belong.
- **I-d. Call and response across regimes over time.** Each layer's events
  trigger changes in the others. This extends the conductor beyond one source
  and one performer.

Axis II:
- **II-a. A grammar morphed continuously.** The parameters of one learned hand
  are interpolated along the line (belt gain, corner budget, curvature
  statistics), so its gesture type changes while it stays one hand.
- **II-b. Handover with visible morph.** Hand A becomes hand B in one stroke
  through intermediate grammars you can read, not a blend. Read Hand Drift's
  history first, so the result doesn't collapse into a style.
- **II-c. Morph by accumulation.** Change compounds along the line: spin,
  fatigue, memory of its own past. This develops drive dud 3.
- **II-d. Morph across a series.** Sheets as stages, building on Metamorphosis
  1–4, with the paper edge as one event among several.

Gate: build the review page (≤ 30 images, notes per image), then read Ian's notes
