## What changes

Round 13's studies were mechanisms with a seed: a fixed layout, and only the
line varies. Ian asks for a different unit: **a full composition that is
different every time and more dynamic**. The composition itself has to vary
and make decisions. That covers:
- the frame;
- which techniques and regimes enter, and how many;
- where the found matter sits and what it is;
- scale contrasts;
- where the blank is;
- the order of layers;
- where the heavy event falls.

The test is a page of compositions from one procedure that do not look like
one template.

## (a) First: how Oehlen builds complex compositions

Use it "vaguely and abstractly", to inform the experiments, not to copy him.
Round 13's `rules` thread read rules off the sheets literally (R1–R5). This is
a different question: how a whole composition is built, at the level of the
sheet.
- How many layers, and in what order?
- How does he set scale against scale, and density against blank?
- Where does the eye enter, and where does the heaviest event sit?
- How does a sheet stay unresolved yet hold together?
- What differs between two sheets of the same series, and what stays the same?

**Approaches, one per agent, run in parallel.** These are starting points;
replace them if the Oehlen note suggests better ones.
- **b1, composer as procedure.** An arranger that decides the composition
  itself from the Oehlen principles: frame, layer order, regimes, scales,
  blank. Then it plays the techniques into it.
- **b2, composer as improviser.** It looks at the sheet so far (a rendered
  image or density map) and decides the next move. It is the round-12
  intuition session, made into a procedure that runs every time.
- **b3, compositions as a grammar.** A small space of compositional moves
  (paste, cover, cross, answer, zoom, morph, leave blank), sampled widely.
  Sample, don't optimise: §7 says breeding toward a score collapses variety.
- **b4, colour as structure.** Developed from his note on the map: the pens
  carry what each line is doing (riding, carrying, answering) as a
  compositional layer, not as a diagnostic.

**Each agent delivers.** Images only, at most 5 per thread:
- one population sheet of 8+ compositions from one procedure, to show
  "different every time";
- its 2–3 strongest, full size;
- a short write-up with its read.

**"More dynamic" is the bar.** Contrast of scale, speed, density and weight
inside one sheet, and between sheets. Density is a quality (§7); do not thin
it out for legibility.

## (c) The Amazon river, zooming in and out along itself

Ian clarified, verbatim: "i was meaning specifically the amazon one, to make
it more interesting". So (c) is **for the Amazon river line itself**, the
frozen far-view GRWL stem (`river_stems/grwl_far_sb19`; the `oehlen_found_river`
voice). He loves it, but at one fixed far zoom it is "starting to be
overused". The way to make it fresh: along that same line, "the small dense
twists, and gradually they get larger, and smaller."

- It is the real river's own bends throughout. Only the zoom changes along
  the line, as if a camera moved in and out while the river was drawn.
- It is not a regenerated river (contagion's statistical river), and not
  gmorph's `gain` (tighter bends at the same scale).

Mechanism ideas, for one or two agents:
- **Variable magnification along the stem.** Integrate the stem's own
  heading θ(u) with a paper speed ds = k(u)·du. Where k is small, the far
  view's dense twists stay dense. Where k grows, the same bends open into
  large ones. Heading stays continuous; curvature scales by 1/k. The zoom
  schedule k(u) can be gradual, asymmetric or recurring.
- **Real zooms.** The river looks different up close: round 11 learned
  rivers at three zooms (`river_meander`, `river_oxbow`, `river_threads`, plus
  the belt). Hand the line over between the far stem and the closer-zoom
  hands as the zoom grows, whole features at a time (`passage`'s lesson),
  so zooming in shows detail the far view cannot.
- **Camera window.** A window sliding along the full 9684-point stem whose
  width widens and narrows. This is "zooming in and out" literally, and the
  frame decides how much river is on the sheet.

Risks:
- Magnifying the far stem shows its sampling. At a large zoom the polyline's
  vertices and the GRWL centre-line steps become visible; smooth or resample
  in a way that keeps the real bends.
- A bend at high zoom can run off the sheet; the schedule has to know the
  frame.
- A symmetric small → large → small reads as a swelling ornament. Asymmetric
  zooms keep it a river.

(c) can feed (b): the zooming river is found matter or a regime a composition can place.
