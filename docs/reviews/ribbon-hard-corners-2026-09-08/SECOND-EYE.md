# Hard-corner ribbon — second eye

Image-first reading: B handles the hard corners more convincingly. Its strands stay nested and evenly paced through the lower zigzag and the left-hand elbow; the pointed creases remain sharp enough to carry the drawn contour. A's diagonals, detached-looking spikes and cross-hatched knots in the lower half read as construction failures because they escape the ribbon's local flow and obscure its width. I would not protect those disruptions.

B also improves the relation between the large lower fold and the long right edge: the eye can follow one continuous bundle through both, whereas A repeatedly loses the strand identities at their corners. The strongest evidence is the lowest point and the inward notch immediately above it, where B keeps both a tight cusp and legible separation.

The weak case for B is the small upper-left elbow of the middle ribbon. A has a compact loop or snag there that briefly complicates the otherwise fluent contour; B rounds it into a tidier stacked turn. That awkward eye has some character, although A's adjacent overlap makes it difficult to separate useful snag from failure. If any smoothing were relaxed, this is the passage worth testing—not the lower spikes.

The top arc and long right descent appear materially unchanged, so this comparison does not suggest a general loss of contour character. The judgement is screen-only: thin-line aliasing, actual pen joins, ink accumulation at cusps and paper-scale strand separation remain untested. After the blind comparison, the individual filenames exposed A as “before” and B as “after”; that context did not change the reading.
