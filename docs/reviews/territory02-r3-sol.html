Scope and limits

This is a blinded visual reading of the eight round-three M/R/K/P sparse and full population sheets (six seeds per sheet) and every supplied 100 × 70 mm close view for seeds 13, 18, 47 and 82. At the point of the initial reading below I had not read mechanisms, manifests, source, maker notes, earlier-round images, prior reviews or prior verdicts. Labels remain neutral. Evidence is from screen renders at a nominal 0.1 mm line width; physical weight, registration and scale on paper remain unknown.

The calibration sheet was read first. A held attention through its articulated pinch and internal cavity; C preserved a more useful quiet separation and oddly particular gap; B made the structure brittle but imposed one angular voice; D replaced hierarchy with an almost uniform decorative curl. That reading made sparse air and unequal authority important evidence here, without treating either as compulsory.

Initial round-three image-only reading

Across the populations

The sparse populations contain the clearest evidence of lines changing character along a course. Long almost-uninflected reaches tighten into doubled seams, small hooks, pinches and short parallel echoes. The transformations are usually concentrated at junctions rather than spread evenly. The most consequential instances change how a large empty region is held: M-sparse-13, M-sparse-82, R-sparse-47, K-sparse-47 and K-sparse-82. They make a viewer retrace a line to determine whether a mass is attached, folded behind, or only nearly meeting another passage.

The four sparse labels do not merely reshuffle the same silhouette, although their differences are uneven. M and P are often very close at whole-sheet scale; their distinctions become visible in the closes as the presence, removal or multiplication of short hooks and internal parallels. R tends toward larger continuous lobes and long hooked reaches. K is the most skeletal and discontinuous: it can expose unusually shaped air, but it also produces the thinnest failures when too much connective material disappears.

The full populations introduce a second visual language: nested parallel fields, clipped bundles and hard rays laid against the sparse contour scaffold. The strongest full cells make those languages exert pressure on each other. M-full-13 leaves a large calm center between an organic hinge and a dense curled field; M-full-18 and K-full-18 force soft contour bands through rigid diagonal beams; P-full-24 lets one low dense tide dominate a much frailer upper structure. In weaker cells, the parallel fields become detachable “hair” or fingerprint-like islands. Their repetition can make different seeds answer with the same surface event even when the underlying scaffolds differ.

M

M-sparse ranges from the stretched hinge of M-sparse-5 through the articulated, almost anatomical M-sparse-13 to the much larger, open constructions of M-sparse-18 and M-sparse-82. M-sparse-13 has a useful change in authority: a remote descending line kinks into a tiny hook and pinch, then releases into the large right enclosure. The close confirms that the small hook is not preview aliasing. M-sparse-82 is more forceful overall: loops, long diagonals and doubled turns make one course successively read as boundary, knot and open channel. Its crowding is productive in the close, though the whole cell risks turning into a generic gestural tangle. M-sparse-47 is quieter and more conventional in its stacked rounded masses; the isolated fragments around them do little visible work.

M-full has considerable range in distribution. M-full-13 protects a broad field of air around a concentrated curl; the mismatch of scales is awkward in a useful way. M-full-18 has the most explicit collision between fan-like fields and long straight cuts, but it approaches all-over activity and loses a place to rest. M-full-47 is a weak/uncertain case: separate repeated islands surround the scaffold without strongly changing its reading, so the additions can feel placed beside rather than grown through it. M-full-24 is also under-resolved at whole-sheet scale, yet its lopsided scarcity may be useful material rather than a defect.

R

R-sparse enlarges and simplifies several relations. R-sparse-47’s two stacked enclosures are held by a narrow waist and unequal parallel seams; the middle almost fails structurally, which makes the upper and lower air depend on each other. R-sparse-82 has long hooks and overlapping open contours that change identity along their course, but its whole view is busier and less decisive than its close. R-sparse-13 keeps an especially clear central interval between the upper junction and lower enclosure, though the enormous right loop is close to a generic lobe. R-sparse-18 is plainer: its broad enclosing arcs and diagonal divisions create scale but little local consequence.

R-full often distributes the dense fields more widely than M. R-full-47 gives a large bent band enough isolation to read as a specific mass rather than texture; its abrupt clipped ends are worth protecting. R-full-18 produces a harsher, less fluent crossing of parallel swells and rigid lines, with real differences in authority. R-full-13 is an ambiguous quiet case: the center is unusually open and the few arcing bands bridge remote regions, but the full treatment can feel withheld rather than deliberate. R-full-82 is lively yet close to a familiar turbulence of nested waves and rays.

K

K-sparse most clearly tests how little structure can remain. K-sparse-47 is a consequential oddity: a central nested arch, a shallow hooked canopy and an unrelated lower prong produce a large, tense blank that is not merely background. K-sparse-82 combines bulb, hook and doubled pocket without settling into one figure. K-sparse-13 opens large compartments but its long smooth contours dominate the few character changes. K-sparse-18 is the clearest failure in the sparse set: at whole and close scale, so little remains that the large blank feels unshaped rather than charged.

K-full can rescue that austerity when the second language is selective. K-full-13 keeps the sparse compartments while dense bands press in from the top and lower right; this unequal authority is stronger than a uniform fill. K-full-18 is a potent collision of an S-like bundled course, straight beams and open ground. K-full-47 remains weak: isolated bars and peripheral fragments do not establish enough relation to the central sparse scaffold. K-full-82 has a substantial layered body, but the repeated parallel swells begin to regularize the stranger hooked scaffold underneath.

P

P-sparse is the least differentiated from M in the population view. The closes show real but modest changes: P-sparse-13 removes the little return hook at the upper pinch; P-sparse-18 multiplies an inner side into a narrow channel; P-sparse-47 is effectively indistinguishable to my eye in the supplied window; P-sparse-82 preserves the dense looped course. The P-sparse-13 simplification loses a small but consequential hesitation. P-sparse-18 gains a stronger sense that a single boundary becomes a band. These are local gains and losses, not yet a clearly different population role.

P-full is more distinct. It often builds broad, coherent parallel masses around a comparatively legible scaffold. P-full-24’s low-left field has unusual authority and makes the frail upper anatomy feel suspended. P-full-47 creates a large circular void amid surrounding fields; the relation is compelling, though more polished and easily resolved than the awkward gaps in M-full-13 or K-full-13. P-full-13 uses a wide right-hand band to turn the open middle into a shaped corridor. P-full-18 lets a dense bend terminate beside a sparse ray field, a useful change of register. P-full-82 has substantial breadth but risks letting smooth wave bands become the default answer everywhere.

What I would protect and what I would test

Protect the tiny, apparently unnecessary returns and doubled seams at structural transitions: the hook in M-sparse-13, the narrow waist of R-sparse-47, and the nested central arch in K-sparse-47. Removing them would make the large surrounding contours easier to parse but would also remove the local event that makes a line become something else.

The clearest population-level concern is convergence in full mode. The parallel fields are persuasive when they bend around, interrupt or contradict a specific contour. When they sit as repeated islands, they make distinct seeds look like instances of one ornamental texture. A focused next visual experiment would hold one sparse seed and overall field count constant while changing only whether a single dense field crosses a structural junction, stops just before it, or sits in unrelated air. That would test whether the fields can cause a consequential rereading rather than merely increase density.

I cannot yet tell from these renders whether close-view clipping is revealing meaningful scale shifts or incidental framing. Nor can I infer which fine parallel runs will merge into physical weight at 0.1 mm. The whole-sheet and close-view judgements above should therefore remain provisional until a plotted sample is seen.

Later evidence: common course windows

After saving the initial reading, I inspected the additional common-window sparse course views for seeds 18 and 47. These use the same larger window for all four labels. They do not replace the population sheets, but they show that the original 100 × 70 mm crops excluded some of the most displaced turns.

Seed 18 changes most in this evidence. M contains a very large bulb course, a small segmented inner curve and a low hook. R carries larger unequal lobes with stacked turns. K has a long diagonal boundary, a fragmented hooked passage and a lower lobe; it remains austere at whole-sheet scale, but it is not the empty event suggested by the first close. P makes the largest revision to my initial reading: the narrow local channel belongs to a much larger displaced course with a long loop, an angular return and doubled sides. That course is materially stranger than the original crop implied.

Seed 47 also clarifies distinct roles. M forms a large C-like enclosure with a restrained turn. R is a thin, interrupted vertical coil whose fragility is more apparent than in the close. K now reads as a single course changing repeatedly: tight top loops become a descent, then a bulb and a return. P makes a broader bulb loop with a doubled turn, between M's restraint and K's compressed sequence of changes.

This evidence weakens my initial designation of K-sparse-18 as the clearest sparse failure. Its whole cell is still under-readable because the active passage is thin and displaced, but the common window reveals a deliberately broken change of course. It also strengthens K-sparse-47 and substantially strengthens P-sparse-18. I retain the initial observations above as the blinded first reading rather than silently rewriting them around the better framing.

Matched comparison with rounds one and two

I then compared the same labels and seeds across the two earlier round populations and matched close views. The comparisons below describe visible changes only; they do not attribute those changes to a mechanism.

Stable references

M appears unchanged across all three rounds in both sparse and full sheets, including the matched seed closes. R-sparse likewise appears stable. These are useful controls: any round-three gain assigned to those images would be a framing or attention effect rather than a visible revision. They also show that several strong properties in round three—the M-sparse-13 pinch, M-sparse-82's changing course, and the dense/sparse tension in M-full—were already present.

Correction after a targeted evidence recheck: the claim that R-sparse is visually stable is not supported by the round-one and round-three whole sheets. I had over-weighted the matched close regions and missed course changes outside them. Seed 5 replaces the earlier bent central ribbon with a thinner horizontal doubled passage, crossings and a more angular lower route. Seed 18 retains the large lobe family but adds a doubled upper approach and a long diagonal departure, changing the course even though the dominant enclosure remains familiar. Seed 24 changes most clearly: the earlier compact vertical cluster becomes a longer top reach with a stretched lower relation, producing a stranger, less self-contained silhouette. Smaller local differences are also visible elsewhere in the population. These are changes of identity, so R-sparse cannot serve as a stability control. Aesthetically, seed 24 gains eccentric scale and tension; seed 5 gains brittleness but risks diagrammatic joins; seed 18 gains a local character change without fully escaping its generic large lobe. M remains the supported stable reference. This correction qualifies the comparison above without altering the saved initial round-three reading.

Sparse changes

K changes substantially from round to round. At seed 13, round one has a large double arch, bay and pointed internal cavity; round two strips this to broad compartments; round three makes a quieter open wall with long diagonals and a low arch. Round three gains separated air but loses the first round's oddly specific internal notch and stronger relation between parts. At seed 18, round three gains displacement, scale and a broken hooked passage over the plainer earlier diagonals, while losing immediate readability at the population scale. At seed 47, the earlier rounds offer more local layers and interlocking turns. Round three trades that density for one legible but strange trajectory—coil, descent, bulb, return—and a larger tense blank. At seed 82, round three separates bulb, hook and doubled pocket more clearly, but the earlier versions have more interlocking and less regular overall shapes.

P's sparse changes are less even. At seed 13, round one adds a diagonal intrusion and round two adds a large vertical loop; round three removes those and even drops M's small return hook. The result is calmer but loses useful hesitation. At seed 18, the common course window establishes a real round-three gain: the large displaced loop and angular return are more consequential than the earlier local additions. At seed 47, round two's central hinge knot is locally stronger, while round three relocates the event into a much larger doubled loop. At seed 82, I cannot find a consequential difference in the supplied matched window.

The sparse revision therefore has a real but mixed effect. Its strongest move is to let a course travel farther before changing character, especially P-18 and K-47. Its cost is that some of the earlier strange internal relations become so displaced or reduced that the whole cell reads as simply vacant or regular before the close view repairs it.

Full changes

R-full changes mainly by redistributing density. At seed 13, round three opens the center and gives one arching band unequal authority; this gains air but loses the earlier fields' sustained pressure. Seed 47 gains a broad bent mass where round one had almost no field and round two had a narrower splay. Seed 18 continues to stage a forceful band/ray collision, though round three's crossing can read as harsh rather than articulated. Seed 82 remains mixed: fields move and widen, but the population still leans toward familiar nested turbulence.

K-full shows the clearest gains where a field now presses on a sparse compartment. Seed 13 advances from scattered bars and peripheral fragments to a strong top/lower-right pressure. Seed 18 gains a continuous S-like bundled course and a more forceful collision with straight beams. Seed 82 gains mass and a clear field/scaffold tension, while losing some of the earlier open interlocking oddity and approaching a standard smooth wave. Seed 47 is an unresolved weak case across rounds: additions change position and quantity, but still feel detachable from the central scaffold.

P-full more often shifts fields from patches into larger spatial roles. At seed 13, the dense event moves from a central island through an upper/right wave to a broad right-hand corridor; round three shapes the empty center more clearly, though smoothly. At seed 18, round three separates a dense bend, sparse rays and open center more clearly than round two's forceful all-over collision, gaining hierarchy and losing some pressure. At seed 47, round two concentrates one plume while round three distributes fields around a circular void; the latter changes the whole composition more, but resolves rather easily. Seed 82 broadens the field presence while retaining the risk that repeated wave bands become a default surface answer.

Consequential balance and unresolved points

The round-three evidence supports two gains: longer-range sparse transformations that alter the silhouette, and full fields that more often shape corridors, voids or structural pressure. Both gains are conditional. Displacement can make the active turn disappear from the whole view, and broad fields can replace one detachable patch with a more polished but equally reusable wave vocabulary.

I would protect the round-three course-level changes in P-sparse-18 and K-sparse-47, the unequal density in K-full-13 and K-full-18, and the shaped central air in P-full-13 and P-full-47. I would keep testing K-sparse-18 rather than discard it, because the whole and course views disagree usefully. I would also keep the disagreement around K-sparse-13 and K-sparse-82 explicit: round three is clearer and quieter, while the earlier rounds are stranger through interlocking internal relations. That is a real trade rather than a sequence of unqualified improvement.

The next comparison should use a common course window by default and keep the whole population as the primary reading. The most informative controlled test remains one fixed sparse scaffold with one dense field placed to cross a structural change, stop just before it, or occupy unrelated air. That separates a field that changes the drawing's logic from one that only supplies texture.
