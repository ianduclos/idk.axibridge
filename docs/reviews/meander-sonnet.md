# Meander round 1: blind review (Sonnet)

Reviewer: Sonnet 5.5, replacing Sol on this track by Ian's ruling. Screen only; nothing plotted. Images inspected: Ian's sketch, sheets A-D, close-a, close-b, sheet-X. Sheets viewed at about 1744x2000 px, so hairline detail on the sheets is reduced; the close views are the only evidence for line-level claims.

## Meander round 1

### 1. Image-only reading (recorded before recipes)

Ian's sketch, first: a handful of closed-ish lobes joined by a loose spine, with a few passages where 4-6 strokes bunch into a swelling band (upper-left tuft, the lower run under the central form), and long single lines elsewhere. Weight comes from wandering restatement, not from parallel spacing.

**(a) One line that swells and narrows.**
Observations: K01/K07/K10 lower-left (close K01, K10): 5-8 strokes run along the belly of the big form; they converge at both ends and are widest mid-arc, so the band tapers like a brush stroke. K03 close: the two bands lower right lens open in the middle and pinch shut at the ends, and they undulate together, which is the closest thing here to a line with volume. K02/K17 diagonal band upper-left tapers to a point at the top. K19 lower-left curl and right-hand lobe: bands that follow a curl and thin toward the spiral end.
Where it fails: K22 close and K13 close, where the strokes are near-evenly spaced, parallel, with dashed outer members: this reads as railway/ruled doubles, or a tree-ring edge. K10 close is borderline: the spacing is graded but the strokes are nested concentric arcs, so it reads as a contour set rather than one line. K05 close left arc likewise. Interpretation: the effect works when strokes cross, drift and drop out (K03, K02); it fails when they stay equidistant and continuous (K22, K13).

**(b) Registers.**
Two, clearly: (1) a single clean thin line, long, calm, closed-loop or curl-ended; (2) the bunched bands. A faint third: broken/dashed lines (K01 right lobe, K18 top right, K16 lower arcs) and tiny hooks at stroke ends. They do not mix; the bands are bolted on at a few arc apexes and the rest is one handwriting. Ian's "different registers mixing into each other" is barely met. The X sheet, by contrast, has a real second register: a scratchy, hesitant, multiply-overdrawn one.

**(c) Where the eye rests.**
K01/K10: the heavy lower belly band, then the curl by the upper loop. K04/K13/K22: the top arc, because it is the only large closed arc and the heaviest. K20: the upper-right spiral. K19: the lower-right lobe. In most cells the rest point is simply the densest band; it wins by contrast, not by a relationship.

**(d) Unprompted associations.**
Calligraphic flourish / swash lettering (K02, K11, K19, K20 - curled terminals everywhere). Noodles/ribbons (K02, K17, K19). Crescents (K13, K14, K22 - the bands are literally crescent moons). Cartoon elephants/ears (K04/K13/K22 with the "face" arc; K05/K14 the "head" mound). River meanders and tree rings: not from the sheet-level view; tree rings arrive only at close view of K22 and K13. Maps: no, K16 comes closest via the enclosed lobes. So the named river/map risk is not by eye the problem; the flourish/ornament reading is.

**(e) Unity vs separate treatments.**
Individual cells are unified by one line character (smooth, tapered, hooked ends), maybe too unified: it is one handwriting everywhere, which Ian rejected. Inside a cell, the bands look applied to the curves after the fact rather than arising from them. Hooks at stroke ends recur identically in every cell.

**(f) Density/weight contrast and negative space.**
Weight contrast is real but confined to small patches; large fields are one-thin-line-wide. Negative space is the best feature: K06/K15/K24 (empty left half), K20 (empty upper left), K03 (empty lower right) leave shaped voids. K19 and K21 are the most all-over, K20 the most one-mass-in-a-corner. There is no tangle anywhere: the "nuanced tangle that becomes textural" is absent; nothing is dense-unpredictable.

**(g) Pairings to X (guesses, low confidence; K cells visibly come in families sharing a composition).**
Families by eye: {K01, K07, K10, K16}, {K02, K08, K11, K17}, {K03, K09, K12, K18}, {K04, K13, K22}, {K05, K14, K23}, {K06, K15, K24}, and three singletons K19, K20, K21 with different compositions. Guessing X ancestry: K01-family to X1 (lobed landmass, wandering thin lines); K02-family to X2 (mass at right, lobes); K03-family to X3 (central mass, sparse right); K04-family to X4 (rounded upper form plus curl at bottom, kidney at right); K05-family to X5; K06-family to X6 (sparse, right-weighted field). K19 to X1, K20 to X6, K21 to X3. Honestly the inheritance is not visible to me: X is scratchy, closed, overdrawn; K is smooth and open. I picked mostly on layout order.

**(h) Keepers and weakest.**
Keepers: K03 (close is the best line-with-volume, and the sheet has shaped void), K19 (most all-over, most varied, curls that swell), K20 (asymmetric, void, spiral plus scratchy small marks at lower left: the only cell with a second register), K10 (weighty belly, big form). Weakest: K18 and K09/K12 (sparse, hook-ended, dashes, nothing to rest on), K16 (fine as line but has no weight at all, reads as a lettering sketch), K22 (railway arcs, most tree-ring-like), K13.

**(i) What makes me ask what's going on.**
K20 (spiral plus twiglike lower-left marks: what are those?), K19 (the tangled centre near the dashed lobe), K02 upper-left (the band ending in a thin lead-in line). Elsewhere it is legible and pleasing rather than puzzling; these read as elegant flourishes, not "what is really going on".

**Limits.** Sheets are downscaled; I cannot judge true plotted pen width beyond the close views' stated scale. I cannot judge paper. Family grouping is from eyeballing similar layouts.

### 2. After reading recipes

**Pairings.** Real inheritance (same seed) is only K01/K07/K10/K16/K19 (seed 3) with X3, K02 family (13) with X5, K03 family (21) with X1. K04, K05, K06 families (seeds 7, 34, 58) have no X parent; X2, X4, X6 are decoys. My guesses were K01-X1, K02-X2, K03-X3 and, for the source-nothing cells, K19-X1, K20-X6, K21-X3. Score: 0 of 6 correct, worse than or equal to chance (1 in 6 each). My family grouping of K cells by shared composition was right, so composition is stable across the knobs, but I could not see any trace of the X parent. Inheritance is not visible by eye.

**Variant groups (what they show).**
- K01-06 defaults: smooth thin lines, hooked ends, a few tapered bands, no tangle.
- K07-09 band 0.5: bundles nearly disappear (K09 vs K03: the lens-shaped bands that made K03's close the best passage are reduced to thin double lines). The variant is clearly cleaner, and clearly weaker on the brief. Band 1 stays.
- K10-12 history 0: heavier, longer bands (K10 belly is a wide stack; K10 and K12 fill more of the frame). It is more weight but more nested-arc, tree-ring-like. Real effect; history 0.6 tempers it usefully.
- K13-15 no events/one clock: the composition survives almost unchanged from K04-06; the difference is subtle (K13 bands a little more even). Events did little that I could see.
- K16-18 white forced: dashes and broken outlines appear (K16 lower arcs, K18 top right, K17 left fragments). They read as broken lines, not as a deliberate channel of white.
- K19-21 source nothing: composition entirely different, the most sprawling and all-over cells, and the ones I liked best (K19, K20). So the territory source is compressing the compositions into six similar "bean and hooks" arrangements; with nothing, the range is wider.
- K22-24 flat activity: bands appear everywhere at uniform weight (K22 top arc, K24). Confirms activity is what creates weight contrast; flat is the tree-ring/railway failure.

**Changes to reading.** The one thing the recipes overturn: I had taken K19-21 as a different class of drawing, and they are the same engine minus the inherited territory; that means the interesting sprawl is a property of the meander renderer, and the inherited source is what narrows it. I also read K13/K22 as having lost their tapering because of an unknown cause; it is activity, a legible knob.

### 3. Recommendations

1. Try territory source at reduced strength (or blended with nothing) so compositions have K19-21's sprawl while keeping some parentage; cells K01-06 vs K19-21. Risk: loses the shaped void of K06/K15/K24.
2. Keep activity above zero and widen the contrast: K22-24 show flat activity produces railway bands; K01/K03 show the graded bands work. Push more strokes to zero band and fewer, thicker, crossing ones.
3. Break equidistance in bands: strokes in K22/K13/K10 nest evenly. Let members cross, drop out and fuse as in K03's lens bands, to keep the swell and lose the ring reading.
4. Add a genuine second register (scratchy, hesitant restatement like X1-X5 or K20's lower-left tick marks) applied to some passages, not all; today K01-K18 are one handwriting with bands added.
5. Loosen the hook terminals: identical curled ends in every cell (K02, K11, K19) push toward flourish/calligraphy. Vary or remove on some strokes.
6. Keep band at 1 (K09 shows 0.5 loses the best passage) and keep history near 0.6; history 0 (K10) is the heavier option to test but leans ring-like.
7. Make the white channel legible or drop it: K16-18 dashes are not findable in 2 s and just look like broken lines (K18 is the weakest cell of the round).
8. Shortlist for Ian to look at on paper: K03, K19, K20, K10, K02.

**Kill criteria by eye.**
- River/map: not fired. Flourish/ornament reading is the actual risk (K02, K19, K20).
- Tree rings: fired locally at close view: K22, K13, K10; not at sheet scale.
- Closed beans: partly fired: K01/K07/K10 big lower-left bean, K04/K13/K22 head arc; most other forms are open.
- Railway doubles: fired in K22, K13 close, K05 close arc; not in K03, K02.
- One handwriting: fired, all cells.
- Hairball: not fired; the opposite, no tangle anywhere.
- No protagonist: not fired in most cells (heavy band or big form leads); K09/K12/K18 have none.
- White channel findable in ~2 s: no.
- Inheritance visible: no (0/6 pairing guesses).

**Limits.** Screen only, downsampled sheets, no paper. One reviewer, one pass; my pairings score is 6 guesses, too few to prove invisibility, only that I could not see it. Sheet-X was viewed at sheet scale only.
