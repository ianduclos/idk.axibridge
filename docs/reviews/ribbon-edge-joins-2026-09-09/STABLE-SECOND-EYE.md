# Stable ribbon edge joins — second-eye review

This was not fully blind: I encountered the README's account of the earlier failures before inspecting the stable sheet. The A/B/C labels were still treated neutrally in the visual comparison.

1. **B is the clear regular edge-interpolated ribbon.** Its lanes remain legibly parallel through all three demanding passages: the left hook, the central peak, and the bottom return. The spacing breathes with the ribbon's width rather than opening isolated holes or producing crossings. The top loop and long right descent also retain the same boundary character as A: a fine bundle gathering into a dark edge, then opening again. The softer inside turns read as a plausible consequence of a flexible ribbon, not a loss of intent.

2. **A has useful severity, but the join failures alter the grammar.** The needle-like central cleft and the hard diagonal at the bottom are more arresting than B's rounder folds. I would protect that abrupt width modulation as a quality to seek elsewhere. Here, however, the right-side slit, the crossing wedge through the centre, and the convergence at the bottom make individual lanes stop reading as evenly interpolated edges. These incidents look locally accidental because they do not recur as a chosen rule.

3. **C works only as the experimental fractured version.** Its polygonal cutouts introduce a second, blunt boundary system against the supple ribbon. The upper fractures interrupt the left hook productively: the empty wedges briefly make the ribbon seem folded or excised. The lower polygon is less convincing; its large enclosure and long straight sides dominate the bottom return and obscure the width modulation beneath it. If fractures remain intentional, varying their authority would preserve the ribbon as more than scaffolding for cutouts.

Decision: use B for regular edge interpolation. It meets the evenness and continuity criterion with no visible unintended gaps or crosses. Keep C as a separately signalled fractured mode. The meaningful cost of B is reduced angular bite at the central peak and bottom turn; given that softer inner corners are acceptable, that loss is preferable to A's ambiguous breakage.

One focused next comparison would hold B's clean lane continuity while testing only the inner-corner radius at the central peak and bottom return. That would show whether some of A's sharpness can return without reintroducing crossings.

Limit: this is a screen-render review at one scale. Fine spacing and accumulated darkness may read differently in pen on paper.
