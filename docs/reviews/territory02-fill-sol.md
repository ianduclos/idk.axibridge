# Territory02 fill — bounded second-eye review

## Initial image-only reading

This is a blind reading of the neutral J/C/F/S sheets and the D/H/R calibration. I have not read the study notes, manifests, mechanism descriptions, mappings, or prior verdicts. The drawings are screen rasters of 0.1 mm vectors on the same 300 × 218 mm bed; paper behavior remains untested.

The calibration has one large visible difference and one unresolved one. H inserts dark, sharply delimited fields into several openings. The long left-to-centre trajectory becomes a solid wedge, the centre-right loops become unequal masses, and the lower-right contour acquires a counterweight. The empty field is partitioned into channels. D and R appear identical at the available scale; if there is a change, I cannot justify claiming that I see it. H is not automatically preferable: its largest wedge makes several finer turns supporting contour rather than coequal events.

Across the light populations, C supplies a very sparse course structure. F and S plainly add more than incidental texture, but the result is inconsistent about binding relationships. F is the more selective of the two. In F-13, the left taper, central almond, and three bent pockets around the junction make separate trajectories negotiate one another. Following the long diagonal now changes how the loop and descending contour read: they feel pinched through a common event rather than merely crossing nearby. F-82 similarly turns the upper-left loop and long lower sweep into an unequal pair held by a few dark joints. These are the clearest cases where added material binds courses.

Elsewhere F behaves mainly as local mass. F-179 places strong accents at isolated bends, but they do not make the intervening emptiness more particular; attention hops between badges. F-24 makes a dark itinerary down one oblique course while the rest remains uninvolved. F-233 contains two tapered pockets fitted to bends, but their shared diagonal still passes between separate events. F-113 and F-293 are mixed: some pockets brace neighbouring contours, while others merely thicken an endpoint.

S enlarges the intervention into broader, paler panels. At its best, S-13 preserves the multi-part knot found in F-13 while letting the added strokes remain visibly enumerable. S-113 also makes the central horizontal course carry weight across more than one local turn. More often, however, S fills available corridors so consistently that it reduces unequal authority. S-233 is the clearest failure: large triangular and quadrilateral fields occupy the main openings, and the underlying hairline structure becomes a scaffold for panels. The emptiness loses its prior long, eccentric shape and becomes leftover margin. S-179 similarly distributes grey sails around the sheet without establishing why one should govern another. This reads as a repeated answer rather than a response specific to each relation.

The awkward feature I would protect is the white guttering inside F-13 and F-47: narrow gaps split adjacent dark pockets even where smoothing them into one field would look more finished. In F-47-dense-close, the hooked upper pocket, black inner wedge, and broad lower fan almost meet but do not fuse. Those interruptions let a course change role from boundary to separator to crossing line. S-47-dense-close regularises the same area into cleaner panels; it is easier to parse but loses some of that unstable handoff. The irregular cutoffs and occasional nearly empty pocket in F are therefore useful, provided they remain consequences of the nearby courses rather than random damage.

J offers distributed bundles, broken repetitions, and small crossings. In J-82, J-113 and J-13-close, authority grows and fades along a route instead of arriving as a bounded patch. J-233-close is weaker but informative: its broken upper-right bundle and stray left crossings do not cohere, yet they leave the centre empty with authority. J should not be treated as a direct before/after control solely from matching seed IDs because its visible course structure differs; it is useful as a counterexample to patch-shaped weight.

Increasing from light to dense changes tonal weight more readily than relationships. At contact-sheet scale the F and S dense populations largely repeat the same placements and hierarchies. The F-47 dense close view becomes near-black in several pockets and produces screen moiré where many fine strokes compress; S-47 remains more evenly ruled. These raster effects caution against rewarding apparent solidity. Close views confirm that the dark areas are line fields, but they cannot predict how 0.1 mm marks will merge optically on paper. Some abrupt straight panel edges are visible at close scale and appear intrinsic rather than thumbnail aliasing; hard outer truncations may still be work-surface crops.

My present decision is conditional: F sometimes binds course relationships, most convincingly at F-13 and parts of F-82/F-113, but across the population it more often adds local authority. S mostly adds extended mass and tends to turn empty spaces into residual gaps. A focused next test would use F-13 and F-233 at light density with identical underlying courses and matched added-line budgets: one neutral variant keeps only fields visibly constrained by two different courses, while the other keeps fields attached to a single bend or endpoint. Compare whether attention can still travel from one course into another and whether the principal empty space changes shape. That isolates relational binding from the simple benefit of darker anchors without making density the reward.

## Reassessment after context

Knowing that F interpolates between two distinct rewritten courses explains why its best pockets feel relational rather than merely attached. It does not overturn the visible judgement. F-13 and F-179 both select three pairs sharing one principal course, yet only F-13 makes those encounters cohere visibly; shared-course topology is therefore insufficient. The geometry, spacing and handoff among fields still determine whether local relations become a sheet-level event. The mechanism binds courses locally, but the population does not yet show reliable overall coherence. S using the same polygons strengthens the F/S comparison: F's changing trajectories preserve more of the neighbouring courses' direction, while S's uniform scan direction makes the polygons read as panels. I therefore retain F-13's divided knot and F-47's white gutters as useful evidence, without adopting F as a winner.

D removes one fill bucket and R removes all fills, but this crop does not reveal D's remaining difference from R. My initial observation should be read strictly as crop visibility, never as evidence that no geometry was removed. I also withdraw the implication that “dense” necessarily increases density: the 8/18 m labels are allowances, and pitch saturation explains near-matches.

The next test should hold seed, three-region cap and total ink constant on the actually disconnected F-47 and F-233 selections, comparing them with connected selections. The question is narrow: does connectivity make attention travel across fields while preserving F-47's white gutters and F-233's principal emptiness? It may produce no aesthetic gain; that negative result would usefully show that selection topology is not the missing binding variable.
