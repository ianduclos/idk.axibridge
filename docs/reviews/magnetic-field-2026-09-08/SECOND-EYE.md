# Magnetic-field second-eye review

## Initial blind reading

The sheet reads as one structural family: rows change topology; columns change whether identical routes register as enclosing chambers (A), porous trajectories (B), or particulate haze (C). A3/B3 held my attention longest because the unequal cluster makes a bent central passage and gives the large upper-left emptiness real weight. A2 felt most inert: its symmetry and even spacing resolve too quickly. C1 remained ambiguous; its open chamber attracts, but the short marks generalize into static. The abrupt dark crowding at the poles is an awkwardness worth protecting because it gives the long empty passages their counterweight.

## Calibration correction after disclosure

I initially described X as having fewer nested contours. That was wrong: X and Y have identical underlying continuous routes and pen width. Reinspection shows a perceptual difference caused by mark treatment. X's constant short dash/gap cadence weakens linkage across each route, so the inner loops and outer envelope feel airier and less enclosed. Y's variable lengths, gaps, and slight drift create intermittent longer continuities and denser local bunching, especially near the pole ends; the same contour count therefore feels more continuous, crowded, and complete. My preference for X as the more useful calibration probe survives, but the geometric attribution does not.

## Position after mechanism disclosure

I retain B3 as the stronger whole-drawing starting point: it preserves the asymmetric passage and hierarchy at reduced size while making local continuity uncertain. This differs from the lead's preference for C3. C3 has the more consequential local marks and may offer the richer instability to develop, but on the population sheet its structure thins into a fairly even particulate field. A focused comparison should keep row 3 fixed and lengthen only the marks crossing sparse passages; the risk is losing the even pulse that currently binds B3 or the granular volatility that distinguishes C3.
