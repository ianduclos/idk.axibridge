# Territory 02 — round 1 independent visual review

## Initial calibration reading — image only, before reveal

Evidence: `tools/territory02/evidence/calibration-030/sheet.png`, inspected as the full sheet and as full-size cell crops. Labels were treated as neutral. I had no mechanism, sequence or preferred outcome. This is a screen reading; it cannot establish how the paths feel at plot scale or on paper.

### Overall field

A and C appear perceptually identical at this scale. In both, attention enters through the compressed, multiply redrawn knot at left, moves through the pinched central crossing and small inner capsule, then releases into the much quieter right-hand loop. The long descending line from above and the two open stems below make that compact event feel suspended rather than sealed. The most consequential relation is not any single contour but the change of pressure: left-side accumulation, a narrow transfer, then a large plain enclosure. The right loop's regularity is a little generic in isolation, but its comparative calm gives the left knot authority and makes the empty interior particular. Removing or decorating that calm would change the whole drawing's cadence.

### A

A holds attention through unequal passages. The small hesitations and near-retracings at the left crown and central pinch make the otherwise fluent sweeps feel subject to local resistance. Following the upper stem down changes the right loop from an isolated oval into one possible continuation of the central collision. The awkward feature I would protect is the cramped set of parallel reversals just left of the inner capsule: polishing them into one clean junction would remove the only place where several possible routes remain simultaneously active. A possible loss is that the large loop and long stems remain single-character curves; once the eye has understood the pressure-to-release structure, those stretches offer little further change along their course.

### B

B shifts attention from passage to outline. Faceting makes the upper stem, left crown, inner capsule and right enclosure read as related cut shapes, while the central junction becomes a brittle star of short segments. This makes the overall silhouette more peculiar, especially where the lumpy right mass meets the narrow neck. It also weakens the drawing's temporal sense: bends that previously accumulated or eased now arrive as uniformly abrupt corners. The left knot becomes easier to parse but less capable of changing on a second look. The useful awkwardness is the mismatch between the long, still-curving lower stems and the newly angular upper body; resolving that mismatch into one dialect would likely make the drawing more diagrammatic. The weak point is the large right enclosure: its faceting changes its contour without giving its successive segments distinct roles, so it risks reading as a coarse approximation rather than a consequential mutation.

### C

I cannot justify a different reading of C from A from the supplied pixels. The same left knot, central pinch, quiet right enclosure and suspended stems govern attention, and I do not see a passage whose presence, position or authority has materially changed. If A and C are intended as a subtle counterfactual pair, that change is below my perceptual threshold in this sheet and full-size screen crops. Treating them as different would be invented certainty.

### D

D redirects attention from the left-to-right pressure change to near-continuous surface agitation. The inherited silhouette is still recoverable, but the repeated curls give upper stem, outer stems, central bridges and right enclosure almost the same voice. Dense areas become darker snarls largely because already-close paths accumulate more oscillations; quiet paths become decorative borders. The right loop's empty interior still offers a pause, yet its restless edge keeps calling attention back to itself, so it no longer supports the left knot as cleanly. A potentially useful awkward passage is the upper stem compressing into the central bridge: there, the coils appear to tighten as the line enters congestion, so the change can be read as pressure rather than ornament. I am unsure whether that is deliberately differentiated or a by-product of overlap. Elsewhere the repeated curl erases the identity of individual courses and exhausts its surprise quickly. This is the clearest weak case, but it is useful evidence: changing every line strongly does not necessarily make the relations between lines more consequential.

### Calibration discrimination

The variants separate three distinct effects: A/C preserve a hierarchy of pressure and release; B makes the global envelope stranger while flattening many local transitions into one angular manner; D produces the most local activity while flattening the population into one ornamental manner. My strongest preference to carry forward is not for a surface style but for alterations that let a single course pass through visibly different states while preserving enough quiet territory for those states to register. I would next compare a restrained variant in which only one or two long passages change character near the central pinch, leaving the right-hand calm and some weak, uneventful paths intact. The risk is over-signalling the pinch and turning a currently ambiguous transfer into an illustration of a rule.

## Round 1 — blind image-only review

Evidence: all eight neutral 0.3 mm whole-population sheets in `tools/territory02/evidence/round1-030/` (`K`, `M`, `P`, `R`; sparse and full), followed by the sixteen supplied same-window close views for seeds 13 and 18. I had no identity, mechanism, maker notes or preferred outcome. Cell IDs below use `label-density-seed`. This remains a screen reading. The close views make path continuity clearer, but not pen pressure, paper interaction or physical scale.

### Population-level reading

The sparse sheets carry the clearest differences between labels. K tends toward distributed fragments joined by a few long, searching sweeps. M makes smoother, larger enclosures with congestion concentrated at pinches. P is very close to M in several matched seeds but adds a conspicuously straight or angled interior passage. R changes the global topology most consistently: lobes migrate, split, hang from one another or become satellites, so the empty spaces change with them.

The full sheets share a strong visible vocabulary across all four labels: parallel stream-fields, dark brush-like accretions, long ruler-like crossings, small doubled-back bars and occasional starbursts. They vary in placement and contour, but this common vocabulary often has more authority than the sparse drawing underneath. The better cases are those where a field bends around, compresses into or peels away from an existing passage. The weaker ones merely distribute varied marks across the rectangle. Density by itself does not deepen the sparse relation.

The fixed close views support that distinction. M-sparse-13 and P-sparse-13 are nearly the same drawing except for P's added oblique segment entering from below; seed 18 repeats the comparison with P adding an angular shelf across the large enclosure. Those additions are visible decisions, yet they remain single insertions rather than changes within an existing course. In the full close views, the parallel bundles can accumulate, taper and turn, but they usually retain one manner across the bundle; local density changes more often than line character does.

### K

K's sparse range is broad without becoming uniformly demonstrative. K-sparse-5 is a strong case: the eye moves from the low central knot into a long rising sweep, then has to reconcile that ascent with the detached left bowl and short upper hooks. The open distances are active because the few passages disagree about scale. K-sparse-47 is also useful: stacked cavities and a lower pinch make a peculiar vertical body, while the broken left arc prevents it from closing too neatly. I would protect that incomplete, slightly stranded arc.

K-sparse-18 is nearly promising. Its huge right-hand enclosure and tiny left fold make a striking inequality, but the connecting verticals and detached fragments do not yet make the large void change on a second look. K-sparse-82 is weaker: upper cluster, central knot and lower leaf-like form remain three episodes with little pressure passing between them. This quiet failure is useful because it shows the limit of mere distribution. In the full sheet, K-full-24 gives its lower lobes some real weight through nested fields, whereas K-full-13's small rectangular bars and peripheral traffic feel placed into vacancies rather than drawn into relation with the central curves.

Focused K experiment: choose one long sparse sweep such as the ascent in K-sparse-5 and let it undergo a single sustained character change away from the knot—split, stiffen or briefly become parallel—then return to quiet. Keep the detached fragments and most empty space. This tests whether K can make distance itself consequential; the risk is converting a searching line into an announced effect.

### M

M's sparse drawings establish coherent large envelopes and reserve their uncertainty for narrow junctions. M-sparse-13 remains one of the most sustained passages in the set: left crown, small capsule and large right loop exert unequal pressure without resolving into a single emblem. M-sparse-82 is the stranger global case: a small folded mouth at left opens into nested internal lobes and a very large right boundary, so interior and exterior trade roles as the eye follows the contour. M-sparse-18 is nearly promising for the same reason, but its enormous enclosure is so fluent and uniform that the left protrusion does most of the work alone.

M-sparse-24 is a weak case worth retaining. Its activity bunches at the right edge and the lower lobes settle into a familiar pair; the long empty left field feels unused rather than shaped. M-full-24 then covers that weakness with heavy accretions instead of changing it. By contrast, M-full-13's lower-right stream bends into a tight eye-like return and M-full-18's close view shows parallel currents changing spacing as they approach crossings. Those are useful because compression changes how nearby straight scaffolds read, although the source drawing can still be demoted to a support for the field.

Focused M experiment: attach one full-sheet stream-field directly to a sparse contour, let it compress at a pinch and peel away into fewer lines, while leaving the opposite half of the sheet untouched. This tests whether the bundle can become one changing passage rather than a separate plume. The risk is making the response too obedient to the contour and losing the productive foreignness of the field.

### P

P's sparse populations make a precise proposition: an interior straight passage can interrupt a soft enclosure. It succeeds most in P-sparse-18, where the angled shelf cuts a distinct lower void inside the large hanging body; following the outer contour now makes that interior floor feel unexpectedly architectural. P-sparse-47 is almost useful: the V-like incision gives the upper loop an internal directional force, but its endpoints still read as dropped into the cavity. P-sparse-5's diagonal does create a long axis out of the central knot, though it duplicates a direction already available in the surrounding curves.

P-sparse-24 is the weak case. Its bent insertion reads as an annotation inside an already uncertain lower area and neither reorganizes the pair of lobes nor changes the empty field around them. The awkward feature worth protecting across P is the refusal of straight insertions to share the curves' finish; their alien bluntness can make the soft shapes less self-sufficient. The problem is relational, not stylistic. P-full-13 almost finds a relation when a compact stack of short bent lines bears down on the large quiet arc, but the close view shows the stack as an object sitting above it. P-full-47 is more consequential because its broad field crosses and loads the existing diagonal structure, while much of P-full-82 returns to a scatter of handsome but interchangeable tufts.

Focused P experiment: in one M-like sparse envelope, replace the floating insertion with a straight passage that begins as an actual continuation of a curve, breaks character sharply, and ends by changing a second boundary or void. Use only one such passage. This tests whether P's alien geometry can produce a before-and-after relation; the risk is making the device read as a connector diagram.

### R

R has the most consistent global departures. R-sparse-18 is a strong case: the pinched left shoulder, huge descending pear-like enclosure, small lower satellite and high detached bars establish four different scales without equalising them. Removing the small satellite would make the large body far more self-contained and ordinary, so that apparently minor appendage is worth protecting. R-sparse-47 is comparably fertile: its stacked lobes refuse a single silhouette, and the tangled lower junction makes the upper cavities read as weight rather than decoration.

R-sparse-5 is a quiet, ambiguous case. Its central doubled fold has real local pressure, but the long diagonals mostly depart rather than alter what remains. R-sparse-24 is weaker: the compact top loops and dangling lower loop settle quickly into a closed, almost emblematic cluster. R-sparse-82 is nearly promising because the upper slanted plane, central bulb and long baseline occupy different spatial registers, though the whole risks becoming a seated figure too readily.

R's full cases often preserve more of the sparse topology than the other labels because the underlying lobes remain large enough to resist the fields. In R-full-13, bands bend around a large cavity while another dense stream presses into it; the unmarked central air remains useful. R-full-18 intensifies the scale conflict around the small satellite and large enclosure, but its close view also shows how quickly diagonal scaffolds and dark currents can turn into an all-purpose dramatic overlay. R-full-82 is the clearest saturation failure: many passages are individually articulate, yet too many areas claim equal authority.

Focused R experiment: keep one of R's existing global topologies unchanged and alter character only through a hinge where one lobe becomes another—especially the left shoulder of R-sparse-18 or the lower junction of R-sparse-47. Let a single line thicken into parallels or fracture briefly there, then resume. This tests local consequence inside an already strange whole; the risk is over-explaining an ambiguity that currently rewards return.

### Blind decision signal

R supplies the clearest evidence of stranger overall shape across a population. M supplies the most coherent sparse pressure-and-release relations but often leaves long courses too fluent. P isolates a potentially valuable incompatible passage, though it is commonly inserted rather than earned. K retains useful quiet, fragmentation and non-resolution, but its parts sometimes fail to affect one another. Across the full sheets, no label yet consistently makes a line become several things along its own course. The repeated stream-fields are most persuasive when their spacing and direction alter at a sparse hinge and least persuasive when they act as a generally expressive atmosphere.

## Thin-stroke reassessment — 0.1 mm, still blind

Evidence: the same geometry regenerated in the eight current sheets and selected consequential close views in `tools/territory02/evidence/round1/`. This is a presentation change only. The original 0.3 mm reading above is retained unchanged apart from its corrected archive path.

The thinner stroke materially improves the full populations. What read at 0.3 mm as dark brush-like accretion often resolves into separate lines with unequal lengths, internal gaps, doubled-back ends and changes of spacing. M-full-13's lower-right return is no longer a black eye with a fringe; it is a current that opens, compresses and turns. R-full-18 gains even more: the large field contains distinct tributaries and discontinuities, so it can press against the long diagonals without becoming a single dark mass. The earlier saturation criticism therefore softens for M and R, and somewhat for K-full-24 and P-full-47.

The thinner rendering does not repair placement. K-full-13's small bars are clearer but still occupy vacancies rather than alter the central passages. P-full-13's compact stack and P-full-18's broad currents become more legible as objects, which also makes their separation from the sparse drawing harder to ignore. In R-full-82 the many claims are less blunt, but authority is still too evenly spread. The common full-sheet vocabulary remains visible across labels; it now reads as a family of line fields rather than a family of dark patches.

Sparse judgments remain essentially unchanged. R still changes global topology most consistently; M still sustains the strongest pinched-envelope relations; P's straight insertions still range from consequential partition to floating annotation; K still protects the most quiet and incompletion while sometimes failing to connect its episodes. At whole-sheet screen scale, 0.1 mm makes the quietest sparse paths very pale, so fine distinctions could disappear at a smaller display or on an uncertain physical setup. In the supplied close views, however, the gain in separability is decisive. Paper remains unverified.

The focused recommendations also remain. Thinness reveals more character *between* parallel paths, but it does not yet make one path consistently become several things along its course. K should test one character change along a distant sweep; M one contour-attached field that compresses and peels away; P one incompatible passage whose endpoints alter two relations; R one local character change at an already strange lobe hinge. These are separate experiments, not a request that the labels converge.
