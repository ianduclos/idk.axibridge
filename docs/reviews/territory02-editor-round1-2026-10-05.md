Three Sol makers edited the same two drawings, each making and inspecting two
successive states. The changes are substantial. They also expose a shared habit:
all three reduce a dispersed field to a concentrated rough body with smooth
contours around it. **The loop produced clearer edits, but has not yet demonstrated
a wider visual grammar.**

## What the revisions reveal

All makers chose their second stage. The histories let that preference be checked.
The material maker collapsed separated patches in S1 and opened the pod-like
outline in S2. The connections maker compressed and overlapped the bodies after
its first smooth sleeves floated apart. The composition maker tightened large
empty lobes and brought an angular flank into the material.

Those are actual responses to inspected drawings. They also repeatedly solve
problems by concentrating, tightening and removing. The broad sweep in composition
S2-stage1 may be worth keeping even if its enclosing outline is too obvious.
Stage2 is more compact; that does not automatically make it more interesting.

The fills have changed shape, placement, cuts and overlap. Their underlying
parallel-grain behaviour remains mostly inherited. This run does not yet supply
the stranger range of material behaviours Ian has been asking for.

The connections assignment drifted from its opening question. It clipped,
repositioned and warped existing strands, then authored courses that imply
continuation. It did **not** implement literal endpoint exchange or a connection
model. That question remains open; relabeling the result would not answer it.

## What this run establishes

Three `gpt-6.1-sol` makers worked independently within their own folders, then
one `gpt-5.6-sol` read anonymous images before histories and identities. Each
maker inspected actual renders between stages. The primary checked every whole
state and selected details. Twelve saved edits, both inputs and both older
controls remain reproducible. All selections and rejected stages are preserved.

Geometry replay was byte-identical across all twelve edited states; inputs stayed
unchanged. Finite coordinates, page bounds, parent IDs, 300 × 218 mm dimensions
and 0.1 mm strokes passed. Selected new drawings contain roughly 5.6–11.7 metres
of paths; the untouched S1 reference contains 33.1 metres. Density is therefore
a substantial confound in judging loss of intricacy. These are free proposals,
not a controlled test of individual operations.

The HTML contact sheet and history offer PNGs and physical-size SVGs. Layout,
image loading, identity reveal and link targets are checked at desktop and mobile
widths using in-memory rendering. Actual local browser navigation, physical pen
behaviour and a general generator are unverified. No production or hardware
changes were made.

## Where to stop

This is the planned checkpoint. There is no third maker stage, automatic round
two, combined generator or selected winner. A response can keep an earlier
stage, reject the whole set, or name a particular passage to develop. The useful
next instruction concerns a visible drawing, not another abstract style label.
