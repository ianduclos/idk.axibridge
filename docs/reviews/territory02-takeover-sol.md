## Initial blind reading

### Calibration

**Visible evidence.** D, E and F share the large right-hand arc, the hooked three-line descent near the upper middle, and the smaller curling passage at left. Their consequential difference is around the left-to-centre convergence: D lets more trajectories cross and continue through that knot; E makes the lower sweep more continuous and gives it a clearer pointed return; F thins the convergence so that the small left curl and the long lower sweep read more separately. The differences are modest enough that I would not trust a claimed ranking from a reduced contact sheet.

**Preference.** D attracts me most. The centre-left knot changes a line from boundary to crossing to track without settling which strand owns the junction, while the nearly empty right side makes that small complication authoritative. F is more immediately legible, but the separation weakens the useful doubt about whether the left curl, central hook and lower sweep belong to one event. E sits between them; its cleaner pointed return is attractive, but it begins to explain the passage as a single fluent contour.

**Uncertainty.** I am responding to topology on screen. At 0.1 mm on paper, the close parallel lines and tiny separations at the knot may merge optically or gain physical irregularity.

### Population evidence

Across both sheets, L most often alternates long, quiet courses with tight local incidents: doubled turns, short hooks, crossings and near-joins. Q generally gives more of the field to broad rounded returns and repeated near-parallels. T usually keeps fewer incidents and makes the long contour dominant. These are not unchanged populations. At matched seeds, the location and authority of the active passage shift markedly, not just its finish.

L-13 is the clearest example of a course acquiring several roles. In close view, the left strand loops, crosses a fan of diagonals, becomes a parallel track through the central hook, and releases into a large blank bounded by the right arc. Following that arc back makes the small knot feel more consequential. Q-13 replaces that exchange with a huge bulbous enclosure and an inward bite; T-13 keeps the enclosure but introduces one leaf-like crossing and an angular lower break. Q-13 and T-13 hold a distinctive empty interior, yet their first interest exhausts sooner because most of the course remains one smooth enclosing gesture.

L-82 has another relation worth protecting: a doubled ridge rises toward a separate arch that almost meets it, producing a compressed triangular interval rather than a resolved junction. Q-82 moves the activity to a cropped cluster near the lower-right edge, leaving the selected frame mostly vacant; T-82 reduces it further to a single angular arrival. The close views make this a real loss of relation, not merely a density change.

L-103 is more forceful but less economical. Several tracked curves converge on a small triangular fan, then a single diagonal cuts through and escapes. The tiny fan changes how the broad upper loop reads, from a loose contour into something briefly fastened. Q-103 and T-103 retain large crossing directions but disperse the fastening; their empty intervals become larger and calmer, with less reason to return to the crossing.

L-31 is an ambiguous case. Its close view contains an unusually dense braid of doubled arcs, narrow loops and diagonals. It offers several extractable relations, but at this scale they compete rather than redirect one another; attention stalls inside the braid. Q-31 opens the same frame into two large arches with a small hooked interruption, while T-31 gathers the activity into a lower loop crossed by rising lines. I prefer T-31 of those two because the small loop alters the reading of the long courses without filling the whole interval.

L-47 is weak as a whole but useful as a warning and perhaps as material: long diagonals, detached curls and a tiny lower tuft remain scattered, so no passage changes the authority of another. Q-47 also feels undercommitted despite its larger recurves; the isolated vertical behaves like a divider rather than a participant. T-47's doubled hairpin caught by a vertical is more specific, though the whole drawing still tends toward a familiar stack of lobes.

### Provisional preference and interpretation

The useful awkwardness is the imperfect local fastening seen in L-13, L-82 and L-103: close companions diverge, cross or almost join, then one line resumes a much larger course. Polishing those moments into uniformly smooth lobes would lose the change of scale and the shaped emptiness they create. Q contributes some strong enclosing spaces, but repeated soft returns often make unlike seeds answer with the same rounded cadence. T supplies clearer long-course structure and, in T-31, a good compact interruption; elsewhere its restraint can become merely sparse.

For the limited decision, these populations contain material worth one focused continuation of the curve exploration, especially the L-type junctions tested against T-type long-course clarity. This is evidence for further material, not for a converged or default instrument. The next image test should hold the long outer course constant and vary only whether one local doubled passage crosses, nearly joins, or cleanly separates; that would show whether the awkward fastening itself changes the rest of the drawing or whether the appealing cases are fortunate accumulations.

## Additional blind population pass: U, Q and W

**Visible evidence.** U adds conspicuous local excursions to long courses: doubled bulges, pointed catches and short enclosed fingers. W retains the broad-course economy associated with T but interrupts it with sharper notches, flat turns and occasional small hooks. Neither is simply a denser or cleaner version of the earlier populations. U-13 replaces L-13's single contested knot with several separate, tubular incidents distributed across diagonals. U-47 turns the previously scattered seed into a substantial tracked S-curve: it loops, crosses its own continuation, narrows into a small hook and releases into a broad right-hand turn. U-82 is denser still, chaining large leaf-like regions through a central fan and a lower doubled loop. These three provide stronger local events than their Q and W matches, but also repeat the same swollen, doubled vocabulary enough to become recognizable as a device.

The new held-out cases keep that judgement mixed. U-107's broad triangular course is punctured by three differently scaled beaks or catches; the left close-view incident redirects the long horizontal rather than merely decorating it. U-227's doubled serpentine spine also makes several opposing spaces, though the accumulation is close to overstatement. U-281 breaks into unrelated bulb fragments and long exits; here the incidents do not make the vacancies particular. This weak case argues against reading U's strongest development seeds as a stable gain.

W is quieter but not consistently more consequential. W-82 contains a good small exception: a long descending course is briefly caught into a tight loop beside a horizontal, so the surrounding emptiness changes without becoming crowded. W-47 has more extensive kinks, but they mostly crenellate already large contours; attention registers the altered edge and moves on. W-13's selected close frame is almost vacant, and W-281 is similarly dispersed. W-107 and W-227 gain angular interruptions over smooth enclosing gestures, yet several look applied at contour corners rather than arising from a relation between courses.

Q's new seeds reinforce its rounded cadence without making it uniform. Q-107 and Q-173 are dominated by large hanging lobes and repeated near-parallels. Q-227 is the important exception: four broad regions pinch around a small central convergence, and the close view shows one tracked return thickening authority on the lower-right lobe. It is more spatially consequential than many Q cases even though its repeated loops remain fluent. Q-281, by contrast, fragments into separate curled passages.

**Preference and interpretation.** U-47 is the strongest new argument for further exploration because one course changes function several times while still governing a large blank field. U-107 and W-82 are useful quieter counterexamples. I would protect U's local catches but resist multiplying them across every course; when they recur at equal scale, the drawing becomes a catalogue of bulbs. Relative to L, U sometimes converts an uncertain near-join into an explicit motif. Relative to T, W restores interruption but often as edge styling. The focused comparison should therefore include L-13 or L-82, U-47, U-107 and W-82, with U-281 retained as the failure case. Screen and crop limits remain: tiny doubled gaps may change materially on paper, and several close frames omit the relation that carries the whole sheet.

## Context response

The disclosures explain the visible family differences without overturning my preference. Q being the ordinary sparse baseline fits its repeated rounded cadence. T/W as first passes and L/U as geometry-consuming second passes clarify why the latter make crossings, doubled tracks and local fastenings that can change a course's role. The current localized fit is visibly doing what “more bulbs or corners” asks: U-47, U-107 and U-227 contain stronger bulb/corner contrast and differing approaches into bends than their first-pass counterparts. The absence of a fixed motif library matters, because the recurrence is an emergent tendency rather than literal copying.

It does alter the claim about reliable intricacy. U demonstrates **actual local shape change**, but not yet dependable relational intricacy. U-13 loses some of L-13's central entanglement by distributing explicit tubular incidents; U-82 risks making every junction declarative; fresh U-173 and U-281 remain weak or fragmented. The fresh U-107 and U-227 cases prevent dismissing U as development-seed luck, but the held-out spread remains mixed. My disagreement, if the current fit is being treated as the answer to “that but more intricate,” is that increased bend articulation sometimes substitutes a recognizable bulb for the less nameable near-join that made L-13 and L-82 compelling.

Calibration strengthens that reservation. E is the full trial4 image; I preferred D after one emitted path was removed, while F's removal of the final two paths weakened the relation. Thus omission itself was consequential: at least one contribution in the full drawing explained too much, while two others helped hold the uncertain fastening together. The current U population is useful further material for a curve takeover, especially U-47, but the next comparison should test whether its localized articulation creates relations across a course rather than merely adding bulbs and corners. No claim here extends from screen to plotted paper.
