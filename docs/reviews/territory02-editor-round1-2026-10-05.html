Look at the eight drawings first. All starting states and revisions remain available. This ends round one; Ian's response comes before another round. No direction has been combined or selected for production.

Three Sol makers edited the same two drawings, each making and inspecting two successive states. The changes are substantial. They also expose a shared habit: all three reduce a dispersed field to a concentrated rough body with smooth contours around it. The loop produced clearer edits, but has not yet demonstrated a wider visual grammar.

What I see

My reading follows the whole sheets, all twelve edited states and selected close views. I know their identities; the independent first reading below did not.

What the revisions reveal

All makers chose their second stage. The histories let that preference be checked. The material maker collapsed separated patches in S1 and opened the pod-like outline in S2. The connections maker compressed and overlapped the bodies after its first smooth sleeves floated apart. The composition maker tightened large empty lobes and brought an angular flank into the material.

Those are actual responses to inspected drawings. They also repeatedly solve problems by concentrating, tightening and removing. The broad sweep in composition S2-stage1 may be worth keeping even if its enclosing outline is too obvious. Stage2 is more compact; that does not automatically make it more interesting.

The fills have changed shape, placement, cuts and overlap. Their underlying parallel-grain behaviour remains mostly inherited. This run does not yet supply the stranger range of material behaviours Ian has been asking for.

The connections assignment drifted from its opening question. It clipped, repositioned and warped existing strands, then authored courses that imply continuation. It did not implement literal endpoint exchange or a connection model. That question remains open; relabeling the result would not answer it.

Independent reading

The full independent reading preserves both passes. Sol preferred N1 at whole-sheet scale and N4/N5 as compact outcomes. It initially proposed a further edit to N2, then withdrew that suggestion after seeing its history: another pass risked polishing the same answer. Its final recommendation is no more editing this round.

Its most useful disagreement is with the material maker's S1 choice. It prefers stage1's distant rough lobe and broad arch to N3's consolidated specimen. Both remain in the history. I share its concern about concentration becoming an automatic solution; I remain more interested than Sol in N8's collision of grain directions, although that local interest does not establish the whole drawing.

The reviewer first saw a partial tool display of one calibration image and withheld a whole-sheet judgement. Reloading resolved it. It then distinguished the open corridor in the omission variant from the source's third dark anchor, without assuming that added or removed complexity must be better. The recorded counterfactual removed 121 whole strokes; it did not isolate a local rectangular patch exactly.

What this run establishes

Three gpt-6.1-sol makers worked independently within their own folders, then one gpt-5.6-sol read anonymous images before histories and identities. Each maker inspected actual renders between stages. The primary checked every whole state and selected details. Twelve saved edits, both inputs and both older controls remain reproducible. All selections and rejected stages are preserved.

Geometry replay was byte-identical across all twelve edited states; inputs stayed unchanged. Finite coordinates, page bounds, parent IDs, 300 × 218 mm dimensions and 0.1 mm strokes passed. Selected new drawings contain roughly 5.6–11.7 metres of paths; the untouched S1 reference contains 33.1 metres. Density is therefore a substantial confound in judging loss of intricacy. These are free proposals, not a controlled test of individual operations.

The HTML contact sheet and history offer PNGs and physical-size SVGs. Layout, image loading, identity reveal and link targets are checked at desktop and mobile widths using in-memory rendering. Actual local browser navigation, physical pen behaviour and a general generator are unverified. No production or hardware changes were made.

Where to stop

This is the planned checkpoint. There is no third maker stage, automatic round two, combined generator or selected winner. A response can keep an earlier stage, reject the whole set, or name a particular passage to develop. The useful next instruction concerns a visible drawing, not another abstract style label.
