# Second-eye review

## Image-only reading

The strongest material appears when the outer silhouette and interior strands agree as one width-bearing path but diverge locally. In F10 and F12, the deep lower notch compresses the strands into a dark hinge while the upper lobes continue across it; top and bottom produce two readings of the turn. F15 and P10 extend this into a return: the inner void becomes a shaped presence, and the upper and lower runs acquire unequal authority.

F03 is the clearest weak case. Its repeated beads and synchronized outline/interior spacing disclose their rule immediately. Much of F01–F09 shares this tendency more quietly: the centerline changes, but the width relation often gives the same answer along its course.

The defect I would protect is P10's awkward right-hand eye or loop. It interrupts the envelope and makes the cavity reread as a fold or misregistered return. Tidying it into a smooth bulb risks a generic ribbon. P11 is uncertain: its rupture has force, but dense radial crossings overwhelm the width relation and read as a separate event.

## After mechanism labels

Knowing the relation labels does not change the ranking. The independent/mirrored/related distinction is often less perceptible than the centerline family; within the straight, arch and S-curve fixtures, each triplet remains closely related visually. The hairpin and corner expose more consequential differences. Later diagnosis attributes P11's serrations and radial pile-up to a numerical defect: curvature protection alternates between a finite limit at original cubic sample vertices and infinity at inserted collinear points. That explains the rupture rather than turning it into useful protection evidence. A correction did not stabilize the narrowing, so protected hairpins remain unresolved and this review does not endorse them. P10's unrestricted loops remain purposeful stress evidence and still give the clearer silhouette–strand relation. The other main-mode cases can stand provisionally.

## Focused next experiment

Hold one F10-like corner centerline and strand count fixed. Compare only three width transitions around the central compression: smooth, delayed, and locally overdriven. Preserve the notch while testing whether width change can shift authority across the turn without producing P11's radial pile-up.
