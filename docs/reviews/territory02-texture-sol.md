# Territory 02 texture review

## Initial image-only account

This reading was made from the neutral calibration images, the A/N/V/B development and held-out sheets, and matched close views, before any account of the variants or prior preference.

The calibration establishes that the underlying drawing has strong blank areas. R is almost skeletal: a few elastic contours and oblique crossings leave most of the field active as shaped absence. H changes that balance radically. Parallel strands occupy nearly every passage, and the original curves become scaffolding for a continuous hairy surface. Its close view is articulate—uneven ends, gaps, doubled outlines, changes of spacing—but the whole is too uniformly answered. D is more persuasive because its bundles are intermittent. The long empty interval between the lower central bend and upper-right activity keeps the base lines authoritative. H's abundance loses hierarchy; R loses the possibility that a line can thicken into another event. The awkward feature I would protect is the blunt, unmatched ending: strands stop at different distances, kink or double back, and do not always close into a polished ribbon.

Across the population, texture does arise in relation to curves often enough to exceed ornament. Its direction usually inherits a bend, and its width, continuity, and placement change which curves dominate. V-419 is especially clear: a compact stack sits against a long shallow curve while a much larger fan collides with diagonals at left. The curve becomes edge, carrier, and obstruction. V-331 keeps a heavy upper-left lobe and a congested lower-right encounter against an open middle. A-13 uses short, tapered fans and narrow vertical bunches that do not simply trace the nearest contour; their stops make the central emptiness legible. B-24 is a useful quiet case: a few additions participate in the knot at right, so the large left blank retains a particular shape.

The recurring limitation is that a bundle's internal construction changes less than its placement. A, V, and B repeatedly answer different drawings with near-parallel strokes that swell into a comma, wave, curl, or tassel. V-389 demonstrates both strength and failure. Its close view contains separated bands, abrupt skips, and strands meeting a bend at different angles, but the whole sheet is so furnished that nearly every passage receives similar authority. B-389 is more selective in the crop, yet the whole still reads as an all-over family of curved brushes. A-18 and B-18 are weaker for the same reason: the source varies, but the answer remains familiar fans. Density often predicts the answer more strongly than the encountered curve does.

The best irregularities happen at encounters. In A-419, the compact central bundle is pinched and crossed by longer trajectories; its uneven lower edge matters more than its parallelism. B-419 intensifies that collision, though the large lower bundle begins to drown the delicate loop at right. V-463's close view has a potent U-shaped accretion whose inner and outer edges do unequal work, plus a scrappy lower crossing that resists the smooth bowl. B-463 is cleaner and more legible, but also more expected: bands wrap around forms instead of being visibly changed by them. V-47 nearly overfills its loop with a teardrop mass; B-47 leaves the centre open and therefore preserves the loop's cavity, though its radiating strands become diagrammatic. These differences are consequential, but they are not yet consistently carried by joins, retracing, interruption, or a change of line behaviour along one passage.

The weak extreme is broad, regular coverage such as H, V-389, and parts of B-47: existing lines become armatures for ornamental bundles and the blanks become incidental residue. The opposite weak extreme is represented by several empty close crops, especially N-24/B-24 and N-389: they prove that the common crop is not a neutral sample of each whole drawing. The crop was chosen around V's grown occupancy, so absence there cannot establish that A or B is globally inactive. A-463 is the clearest warning: its close view is almost only base curves, while the whole sheet contains substantial texture elsewhere.

One focused next comparison would hold a promising whole-sheet placement such as 419 constant and vary only what happens at two curve encounters: one version keeps the smooth parallel bundle, one introduces local interruption and unequal retracing, and one removes the bundle at that encounter. That would test whether the interest comes from responsive contact or merely from occupying the same region. These are raster previews of nominal 0.1 mm vector paths on a 300 × 218 mm bed. They cannot show whether close parallel lines merge, chatter, or acquire weight on paper, and the close views amplify line construction while excluding much of the population-level composition.

## Reassessment after context reveal

A's untouched Territory baseline explains why its texture can feel more embedded: it retains mature relations among old activity, history, and line authority. V's inherited echo, growth, dry-skip, retrace, and continuations explain its richness, but sharpen the concern that it can amplify its own added lines into a stock surface. B's rebuilt guidance is geometrically accountable but not categorically better. B-419 still overweights the lower encounter, and B-463's cleaner alignment remains less alive than V-463's scrappy crossing. D now reads as useful evidence that removing the d-grow layer restores hierarchy while retaining other texture, rather than proving that sparse output is preferable.

I revise one attribution: V-331's left lobe creates a strong whole-sheet counterweight, but knowing the bare drawing's primary activity is far right makes that lobe less convincing as integration with the rewritten curve. I still value the asymmetry; I would not use it as proof of response. The rejection of flat panel hatching supports preserving regrown, line-derived texture, yet it does not answer the central issue: the next gain must come from more consequential encounters, not simply restoring more inherited density.
