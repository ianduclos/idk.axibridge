# Meander round 2, research: tangle that becomes texture, graded from organic line

Scope: procedures, not looks. **Verified** = a source I searched or fetched this session (link given; mostly secondary summaries). **Unverified** = memory. Parameter numbers and all "rules" are my extrapolation and need testing. Round 1 material (Giacometti, Marden connectors, Tobey loose-to-tight, feibai fraying, LeWitt rules, Twombly cancels) is in `docs/research/meander/research-2-procedures.md` and is only cited, not repeated.

## What the two images say

- `round2/close-a.png` (current): every bundle is 3-7 strands that *stay parallel to their parent edge* and merely offset, fan slightly and drop out. That is a ribbon: one degree of freedom (lateral offset). The single exception is M04 bottom, a flat braid of strands crossing a horizontal axis: the only thing close to "tangle", and it is a straight strip with no entry or exit.
- `territory-v5-0929/close-Ha.png` (failed hairball): the same knot in Ha3, Ha5, Ha10 and Ha12. Many restatements circle a *closed* small blob (Ha5 lower loop, Ha10 left blob) or fan out from a corner as straight spikes (Ha5 top right, Ha3 centre). Three failures visible: (a) the tangle *is a shape* (a closed bean with hair), (b) it has a hard boundary and identical density profile everywhere, (c) strands are all the same length and all start and end at the knot, so it reads as a stamped object dropped on top of the line, not the line itself losing coherence.

The design consequence: a tangle has to be an *event on a long line*, not an object. Enter it from a single line, leave it as a single line.

## The unifying primitive (my synthesis, used by the rules below)

A ribbon band is k strands, each following the parent path P(s) plus a lateral offset o_i(s). Define a **coherence** c(s) in [0,1] along the parent arclength. Each strand's heading is
`theta_i(s) = theta_P(s) * c(s) + (own noisy heading, curl and drift) * (1 - c(s))`
so c = 1 is today's ribbon, c = 0 is a free wander that only stays *near* P (bounded by a soft leash of radius R(s)). Tangle is then simply "c(s) dips toward 0 for a stretch, R(s) opens and closes". Grading = the shape of the dip. Return to line = c rising again while strands drop out one by one, ending on the single surviving strand. This makes the tangle the same process as the bands (one parameter, not a second generator), which is what the brief asks.

## 1. Cy Twombly, Blackboard / Palmer-method loops

**Procedure (verified in part).** The Blackboard paintings (1966-72) are continuous loops and scrawls whose flow comes from the way Twombly worked (reported: sitting on a friend's shoulders who moved him side to side in front of the canvas), inspired partly by penmanship drills; the loops do not spell words ([Singulart summary](https://www.singulart.com/blog/en/2024/03/03/blackboard-by-cy-twombly/); [Brooklyn Rail](https://brooklynrail.org/2025/03/artseen/cy-twombly-1/)). Density comes from many passes of one rhythmic loop, and from erasure and overlay in the grey ground (erasure is unverified detail).
**Rule.** "Travelling loop": one pen path with heading rotating at a roughly constant rate omega plus a drift velocity v along P: position advances v per loop, so the loop train *progresses* along the parent and only overlaps itself locally. Density = loop radius r / advance per loop (pitch). Parameters: omega jitter 15-30%, r(s) and pitch(s) as smooth envelopes (r small and pitch large = line with a wobble; r large and pitch small = mass). Fade back: r shrinks and pitch grows until the loops open into a sine, then into a straight line. Stop: fixed total ink budget or when r < 0.3 mm. The sense of hand: r and omega are driven by one slow wander so consecutive loops are unequal, not a spring.
**Warning.** Uniform r and pitch is a coil spring or looping cursive (asemic writing: "one handwriting everywhere"), and lots of closed loops is bean/blob and brain coral. Keep loops *open* (spiral outward then drop the strand), no two consecutive loops the same size, and use it on at most one or two sites per sheet.

## 2. Brice Marden, later Cold Mountain / post-2000 tangles

**Procedure (verified in part).** Ink-dipped sticks and long-handled brushes, distance from the surface; lines are laid as *networks* that connect earlier gestures within and across columns, with ghost lines and earlier layers left visible ([Tamar Zinn](http://tamarzinn.blogspot.com/2013/01/brice-marden-and-drawing-cold-mountain.html); [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/360170)). The late tangles are single long lines with almost constant width that thread through themselves (from memory, unverified as a stated method).
**Rule.** "One thread, self-threading": a single long path is generated with a low-frequency curvature wander; the path is *allowed to revisit its own earlier positions* with a probability p_r(s) when within distance d_r, and when it does it steers toward the earlier strand's heading with weak coupling (0.2-0.4) then peels away. Density is a function of the revisit probability p_r(s), a smooth envelope along the path. Fade back: p_r goes to 0 and the path is just a line again. Stop: path length budget. This gives tangle as *self-restatement of one line*, which is nothing like a stamped object because it is made of one stroke.
**Warning.** Revisit coupling that is too strong gives a braid or a ribbon (same as now). Coupling zero gives ordinary crossings, which read as a knitting tangle or hairball. Keep revisits at oblique angles (20-70 degrees), and cap number of crossings per mm^2 so it does not saturate to black (see limits section).

## 3. Julie Mehretu, layered mark clusters

**Procedure (verified in part).** Architectural-plan or diagram underlay, then layers of calligraphic sweeps and loose drawing, with erasing, sanding and translucent medium that lets lower layers show ([Art & Object](https://www.artandobject.com/press-release/julie-mehretus-contemporary-take-history); [Art21](https://art21.org/read/julie-mehretu-to-be-felt-as-much-as-read/)). Clusters of small marks gather at *intersections of the underlying structure*, and the marks are of different speeds and character in different layers.
**Rule.** "Marks crowd where structure crosses": place clusters at junctions of the long lines already drawn (Ian's sketch: "density piled at the junctions"). Cluster density at a junction J is weighted by how many long lines pass through J and by their crossing angle (steeper = denser). Cluster marks are short strands with headings *sampled from the headings of the crossing lines* plus jitter, so the tangle inherits the local grain rather than inventing a new one. Fade: the cluster's marks are placed with radius-dependent probability falling off along each incoming line (a comet tail along the lines), not a disc. Stop: cluster mass budget per junction, largest at one or two junctions only.
**Warning.** Discs of jittered marks at each junction are the identical-knot problem (v5). The tail-along-incoming-lines rule and the strong difference between junctions (most get nothing) are what stop that. Also avoid adding the layer as a separate pass over everything: it becomes scribble-filter.

## 4. Henri Michaux, mescaline drawings

**Procedure (unverified from memory; also covered by name in territory-v5 research).** Drawing during or after intoxication in fast bursts: the line proliferates into agitated rows of tremulous, vibrating marks that fill the sheet, rhythm being the content. The line is repeated and drifts, never closes into a figure.
**Rule.** "Tremor as carrier": the tangle's strands are all the parent path plus a *high-frequency low-amplitude oscillation* (period 1-3 mm, amplitude 0.2-1 mm) whose phase differs per strand and whose amplitude is the coherence dial 1 - c(s). Density gradient = amplitude times strand count. It makes tone from *vibration* rather than crossing, so it has no knot at all and can sit inside a band and grow out of it. Return to line: amplitude to 0, strands merge and drop out.
**Warning.** Pure high-frequency wobble reads as electrocardiogram, fur or "hairy" edge. Combine with rule 1 or 2 so the amplitude is modulated at a slow scale and some strands stay clean. Rhythm needs a pattern of length variation, not noise (asemic writing warning from round 1).

## 5. Jackson Pollock, poured-line density

**Procedure (verified in part).** Taylor's fractal analysis found the drip patterns fractal and the fractal dimension *increasing over Pollock's career*; Number 14 (1948) D = 1.45 ([Nature 1999 summary](https://www.nature.com/articles/20833); [Discover](https://www.discovermagazine.com/the-sciences/pollocks-fractals)). The specific range 1.1 to 1.72 that I half-remembered was *not* confirmed by what I read: treat as unverified. Taylor also reports the paintings built up in layers, each layer with its own scale (from memory of the Leonardo paper, not checked). Practical procedure: the line is poured from a moving hand, so it has a length scale set by the hand's sweep and another by the drip's local coil.
**Rule.** "Two scales, layered": each long sweep (scale 50-150 mm) is overlaid with a smaller-scale coil pass (scale 3-10 mm) *only where the sweep's speed is low* (i.e. a local curvature peak or a pause), like a poured line that pools when the hand slows. Density function: local coil amplitude proportional to 1/speed(s), where speed(s) is a smooth field; that makes tangle fall out of the dynamics at hooks, turns and ends, which are exactly the places Ian's sketch piles density. Fade back: speed rises, coil amplitude falls.
**Warning.** Pollock-style all-over uniform density is wallpaper (round 1 said the same of Tobey). Keep the speed field lumpy so 80-90% of the length is ordinary line. Pooling at *every* turn = hairy beads; pick the top 15% of slow points only.

## 6. Gego, Reticulárea

**Procedure (verified in part).** From 1969 Gego made wire nets, called "drawing without paper": stainless steel and aluminium wire lines joined into net-like volumes with irregular cells, not repeating tiles ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Gego); [Met, Drawing without paper](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/841074)). Her nets are made of short straight wire segments with hooked or looped junctions and cells of unequal size (from memory, unverified as method).
**Rule.** "Net as a tangle that fixes its own junctions": between two nearby long lines, run k short bridging strands whose ends *hook onto* the lines (a small curl of 1-2 mm at the join), with bridge lengths and angles varied by a distribution, and cell area log-normal. Density = bridges per mm along the pair. Fade back: bridging probability falls off along the pair, and the last bridges are single and long, then none. This is the "net on one lobe" from Ian's reference note, but generated by connection between two existing lines, so it belongs to them.
**Warning.** Regular bridge angles or even cell size = a ladder, spiderweb or fishnet (weather-chart/engraving). Never radial. Use in one place per sheet, and keep it open on at least one side so it does not become a filled panel.

## 7. Monika Grzymala, line installations

**Procedure (unverified; my search timed out and I did not verify).** From memory: black tape (and earlier, ink) is applied in dense drawn masses across walls, ceiling and floor of a room, improvised on site, with the line thickening into thick rope-like bundles at nodes and thinning to single tape lines between them.
**Rule.** "Bundle at anchor, single in span": lines have two states, *span* (one or two strands, moving freely between anchors) and *node* (all strands of that line arrive at an anchor point and bunch tightly, then depart in a new direction). Density gradient along a strand is set by distance to the nearest node: k(s) = 1 + (K-1) * exp(-d(s)/L). Fade back: strands depart from the node in different directions, so the bundle *dissolves by splaying*, not by dropping out. Stop: per-node K cap.
**Warning.** This is a hairball when nodes are all identical radial-splay clusters: exactly the v5 fan. Ensure strands *pass through* a node at 20-60 degree deflection rather than *end* at it, and avoid a common centre point (jitter arrival points by 2-5 mm).

## 8. Chinese cursive (kuangcao, "wild cursive")

**Procedure (unverified from memory; feibai and cun were covered in round 1).** In kuangcao consecutive strokes are joined by thin connecting "hairlines" in one continuous movement: characters fuse, the writer's ligatures become part of the shape, and a stroke's start is inflected by the previous stroke's end (a rule of *continuity*: the pen never lifts between strokes in a run). Density comes from speed: fast, thin, high-tension connectors between heavy strokes.
**Rule.** "Ligature chain": generate a run of 3-6 short gestural strokes, then connect them into one polyline by connectors that leave a stroke end along its exit tangent and arrive at the next stroke's start along its entry tangent (Hermite join), with the connector drawn slightly *longer than needed* (a loop or bow of 10-30% extra length). The run reads as tangle because connectors cross the strokes; it reads as line because it is one continuous pen path and the connectors are smooth. Fade back: the last stroke is long and the run exits along it.
**Warning.** Looks like calligraphy (handwriting everywhere, asemic) if the strokes are stroke-shaped; make the stroke units come from the same parent curve segments (cut from the edge you are already drawing) so they inherit its geometry, not a writing vocabulary.

## 9. Rembrandt and Leonardo, searching knots (pentimenti)

**Procedure (partly verified; round 1 cited the secondary sources).** Leonardo's sheets keep trial outlines and corrections together, modification after modification. In the mature tangles (e.g. the Windsor "Neptune"/water studies and the Madonna cat sheets, from memory, unverified) a shape is *hunted* by successive approximations, each an attempt that gets closer, and the density peaks where the shape is uncertain.
**Rule.** "Convergent search": the tangle is a sequence of n attempts to trace one target curve T, where attempt j has error e_j = e_0 * lambda^j (lambda 0.6-0.8) plus a slow random drift. Attempts start and stop at different arc positions and overlap only partly. Density falls off from the uncertain end to the certain end, so the tangle *is* a line converging onto its final position; it is the graded form in which the mass and the line are literally the same curve at different times. Stop: e_j < pen width; final attempt is drawn longest and cleanest.
**Warning.** Fixed lambda and n on every edge = scribble-filter restatement (rejected). Put searches at 2 or 3 places per sheet; the target curve should also be *the long line between things*, not a lobe boundary. Use very different e_0 per site.

## 10. Sol LeWitt, "not straight lines, not touching"

**Procedure (unverified for the specific wall-drawing texts; the rule is quoted from Ian's prompt and my memory of Wall Drawing series).** A short written instruction is executed by draftspeople with local freedom, and the result is dense but unified. Instructions like "lines, not straight, not touching" produce dense fields where the constraint *stops* the tangle from becoming a knot.
**Rule (the important one for limits).** "Not touching" as a *spacing constraint on the tangle itself*: every new strand refuses to come within d_min of an existing strand except at a deliberate crossing (probability p_x). Use a Jobard-Lefer style streamline queue ([Hobbs on this technique](https://www.tylerxhobbs.com/words/flow-fields)). d_min sets the tone range: d_min = 0.5 mm gives near-black, 2 mm gives grey; d_min(s) varied along the strand gives the gradient. It also *automatically stops* the tangle: there is nowhere left to draw. Fade: d_min grows.
**Warning.** Too regular d_min = tight ruled parallels or flow-field engraving. Vary d_min by a slow field, and allow p_x of 0.03-0.1 crossings so it is not a comb.

## 11. Tyler Hobbs, flow-field density

**Procedure (verified).** Curves follow a vector field sampled on a grid (about 0.5% of image width); shorter curves look like "fur", longer like fluid, and an enforced minimum distance stops a curve when another is too close. Starting positions matter: a regular grid feels stiff, random gives clumps and sparse areas, circle packing balances the two; slightly distorting the grid between rounds gives variation ([Flow Fields](https://www.tylerxhobbs.com/words/flow-fields)).
**Rule.** "Field from the parent": the vector field is *the parent path's tangent field* blended with noise: F(x) = c * T_P(x) + (1-c) * N(x), where T_P(x) is the tangent of the nearest point on P (with distance falloff) and N is low-frequency curl noise. Strands are integrated from seeds sampled at random offsets in the leash radius R(s) (Poisson-disc so seeds are clumped but not gridded). Density = number of seeds per length. This is the implementation of the coherence primitive above.
**Warning.** Fur (short curves) is the main risk; keep strand length >= 20 mm and always overlap so no strand starts or ends at the same place as its neighbours. Flow-field streamlines with a minimum distance are engraving/topographic look; do not use the collision-stop as the only stop.

## 12. Anders Hoff, differential line and sand spline

**Procedure (verified in part: the two algorithms are described in search summaries; the pages returned 403 so the exact rules below are my memory, unverified).** Differential line: a closed or open polyline with nodes that repel near neighbours and are held together by springs along the line; new nodes are inserted when segments stretch, so the line buckles and folds into a dense, wall-to-wall meander, like a walnut or intestine ([inconvergent](https://inconvergent.net/generative/differential-line/)). Sand spline: a B-spline whose control nodes are moved a little at random and the spline is sampled densely with dots; as the nodes wander, the smooth curve becomes erratic ([inconvergent](https://inconvergent.net/generative/sand-spline/)).
**Rule (differential line).** Run it on an *open* short segment cut from the parent path, pinned at both ends (anchors), with repulsion radius r and growth by insertion; stop by a node cap or when mean edge length < pen width times 3. The result is a buckled strand that folds *between two fixed points on the line*, so it is a wiggle of the line itself, not a separate object. The wiggle amplitude is graded by making repulsion r a function of arclength (large at the middle of the segment, zero at the anchors), so it enters and leaves as a plain line. Rule (sand spline): sample a *single* stroke as a thin B-spline under slowly random-walking control points; density comes from re-sampling the same spline many times as the nodes drift (a dot cloud); for a plotter, replace dots by short segments along the tangent (a "sand" stroke). It gives density without any strands crossing at a fixed angle.
**Warning.** Differential growth is the *brain coral* look from the rejected list, and a folded closed loop is a blob. Use open, pinned, small, single instances, cap growth early, and never let it fill a region. Sand spline as dots would be stipple; use tangent segments or it is not an ink line.

## 13. Casey Reas, Process series

**Procedure (unverified from memory).** Each Process is a written *rule set* on a small set of elements (e.g. "elements move in a straight line until they meet another, then turn by an angle"), rendered with different parameters and compositions. The variety and the unity come from the same short rules.
**Rule.** "Stop by contact": each strand carries a small state machine: {advance, curl, halt}. It advances along the field; on contact with another strand it either halts (probability q), curls (turns 60-140 degrees toward the strand it touched), or passes. Density emerges from q; the fade back to line is the region where contact events become rare. This is the local, agent-based version of the leash-and-coherence primitive, and it is deterministic by seed.
**Warning.** Pure agents look "computational" (stringy random-walk fuzz) and homogeneous. Give agents a slowly varying q(x), and inherit one shared heading field so they look as though they belong to one drawing.

## What sets density, how it fades back, what stops it (cross-cutting)

- **Density variable candidates**, in order of likely usefulness: (i) coherence c(s) along one parent line, (ii) slow-point pooling (speed field, from Pollock), (iii) revisit probability of one line (Marden), (iv) convergent error e_j (Rembrandt/Leonardo), (v) junction weight (Mehretu). All are one scalar field along an *existing long line*, which is the "same process as the ribbon bands".
- **Fading back to line**: always by *strand death*, one at a time, staggered, ending at a single strand that continues as the long line. Never by a shape boundary. Test: at the tangle's ends, the strand count must go 6, 4, 3, 2, 1 over >= 15 mm, not 6 to 1.
- **Stops**: (a) ink budget per tangle in mm of pen path (say 200-600 mm on a 300 x 218 sheet, a handful per sheet), (b) minimum spacing d_min, (c) endpoint rule "last strand is longest". A tangle stopped only by geometry (collision) looks engraved; a tangle stopped by budget alone can blacken, so use both.
- **Rarity**: 0 to 3 tangle events per sheet, sizes lognormal, 60-80% of ink still single lines and 2-4 strand bands.

## Ranked top 5 for "tangle from the same process as the ribbon bands; graded; rare"

1. **Coherence dial on the existing bundle (unifying primitive + Hobbs field-from-parent, section 11).** Adds one degree of freedom (strand heading decoupled from parent, leash radius R(s)) to code that already makes bundles. Graded by c(s), fades by strand death, rare by placing 0-3 c(s) dips. Lowest risk of hairball because tangle is the *same strands* as the band.
2. **Convergent search (Rembrandt/Leonardo, section 9).** Tangle equals repeated attempts at one curve with shrinking error; density concentrates at the uncertain end and resolves into the final line. The best answer to "tangle that recovers" and directly the graded form; needs the strongest per-site variation to avoid scribble-filter.
3. **Slow-point pooling with two scales (Pollock, section 5) plus revisit (Marden, section 2).** Tangle emerges at hooks and turns and is driven by the drawing's own dynamics; sits at "density piled at junctions" in Ian's sketch. Marden's self-revisit coupling makes it a single thread.
4. **Ligature run (kuangcao, section 8) with strokes cut from the parent edge.** Gives crossing tangle from one continuous pen path, not stamped; the connectors read as the "long open lines between things". Higher handwriting risk, so it must reuse the parent geometry.
5. **Tremor carrier (Michaux, section 4) modulated by a slow envelope.** Cheapest possible texture: it grows out of a band with no knot, then disappears. Held to fifth because vibration alone reads as fur; use as the *texture* end of the gradient with 1-3 above providing the crossing tangle.

Held back: Twombly travelling loops (looping cursive risk, but a good spice for one site), Gego bridging net (one per sheet, only as the "net on one lobe"), Grzymala node-bundles (fan/hairball risk), differential line (brain coral risk), LeWitt spacing constraint and Reas contact rules (useful as *stop conditions* for any of the above, not as generators).
