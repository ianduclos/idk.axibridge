# Meander research 2: artistic procedures (width/volume from bundles; landscape abstraction that is not a map)

Scope: procedures, not looks. Verified = a source I opened or searched this session (link given). Unverified = from memory. Rules are my extrapolation unless stated. Precedents already covered in `docs/research/territory-v5/research-2-precedents.md` (Cohen, Twombly, de Kooning, Picasso Bull, Molnar, Michaux, Bourgeois, Guston, Mehretu) are not repeated in full; where they matter I cite them by name only.

What I saw in the images: Ian's sketch has bundles of 2-6 drifting restatements that *converge and fan* along one path (the sling band under the central bulb, the hair-like fan at upper left), single thin lines running free, and hooks. In v5 seeds 3/13/21 the weight comes from near-parallel restatements at an even offset; the bundles read as "thick line" but not as *swelling and tapering*, and never as a form turning. ## Part A. Width and volume from bundles of one-width lines

### A1. Giacometti (drawings and paintings) — verified in part
**Procedure.** Contour is never stated once. He re-draws the same edge again and again from re-looking; earlier lines stay, the form is a *zone of agreement* where the lines cluster. His own account: the more he looked at the model the thicker the "screen" between him and it grew (quoted in [Mara Marietta](https://www.maramarietta.com/the-arts/painting-drawing-sculpture/artists-b-g/giacometti/), secondary). The reworked skein floats in a diffuse field, the ground is left open.
**Rule.** Bundle as *distribution, not offset*: for a contour of length L, draw n restatements whose lateral offsets are sampled from a narrow Gaussian around the true path (sigma proportional to a local "uncertainty" field), not evenly stepped. Apparent width at s = local spread of offsets; density peaks at the centre. Parameterise: n (3-8), sigma(s) from noise, each restatement covers only a random 40-100% arc-interval (so the bundle thins to one line at its ends). Check: measured line-count profile along the bundle is bell-shaped, not flat.
**Warning.** Uniform sigma along the whole edge gives "scribble-filter restatement of every edge" (rejected: economy off) and fur. Restate only where uncertainty is high; leave most edges single or absent.

### A2. Converging/diverging fan — the swell-and-taper primitive (Leonardo/Rembrandt pentimenti, Cezanne) — partly verified
**Procedure.** Leonardo's sheets keep first outline, strengthened contour and corrections together; modification sets off modification ([Most Iconic Art summary](https://www.most-iconic-art.com/blogs/famous-paintings/leonardo-drawings-minimalist-wall-art-guide), secondary). Cezanne is credited with using pentimenti deliberately to give the three-dimensional appearance ([Principle Gallery](https://principlearttalk.com/2015/05/26/technique-tuesday-pentimenti/), secondary). The mechanism for volume (unverified as a stated method, but visible on any such sheet): when two or three contour restatements *diverge* over a stretch and *rejoin*, the eye reads a band with an interior that swells, i.e. a rounded edge turning away.
**Rule.** "Lens bundle": choose two anchor points on a path; between them, k strokes leave the path with lateral offset o(u) = A * sin(pi u)^p * r_i, where r_i in [-1,1] are per-stroke distribution weights (not equally spaced), and rejoin. Parameters: A (widest gap, 0.6-4 mm), p (1 = lens, 2-3 = flatter belly), asymmetric peak position m in (0.2, 0.8) so the swell is off-centre like a shoulder. Stagger start/end u of each stroke so tapering is by strokes ending, not by lines merging cleanly. Check: max gap / pen width >= 4 at belly; count of strokes vs u is unimodal; belly position varies across the sheet.
**Warning.** Perfectly symmetric lenses in a row are the tight ruled parallels / tree-ring look (rejected). Vary belly position, A and k, and let some lens bundles be one-sided (the extra strokes only on the outside of a curve).

### A3. Turning the form: converge on the shadow side (Ingres/Matisse contour economy, Rembrandt drawings) — unverified from memory
**Procedure.** In economical contour drawing the line is *lighter/single* where the form faces the light and *doubled or thickened* where the edge turns away, and lines end where a form is understood. Matisse's late pen drawings are the opposite pole: one line, no restatement, but variable speed. Rembrandt's pen sketches build a shadowed edge with a few hurried returns on one side only.
**Rule.** Directional bias: define a phantom light direction Lv. For each bundle, the restatements are offset predominantly toward the side whose normal opposes Lv (say 70/30 split), and the outer edge on the lit side stays a single line. Result: a rounded volume from consistent one-sidedness without any hatch. Parameters: Lv angle (global or slowly varying across the sheet so different masses "turn" differently), bias b in 0.5-0.9. Check: mean signed normal offset of restatements correlates with Lv (|r| > 0.5 over the sheet), and reverses across at least one inflection.
**Warning.** A single global Lv makes every mass a lit ball: blobs/pebble bodies (rejected). Let Lv drift, and let some masses have no bias (flat).

### A4. Marden, Cold Mountain drawings — verified
**Procedure.** Works on paper made with ink-dipped twigs and branches; long-handled brushes (2-3 ft) for paintings to keep distance. He began with columns of calligraphic glyphs and then *connected them within each column and across columns*, keeping an underlying grid; gouache painted over (not obliterating) earlier gestures; "networks of lines and ghost lines" ([Tamar Zinn](http://tamarzinn.blogspot.com/2013/01/brice-marden-and-drawing-cold-mountain.html); [Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/360170)). Tool-imposed constraint: a stick gives a naturally variable, uncontrolled line.
**Rule.** Two-stage connection: stage 1 lays down separate short gestural strokes on a loose armature (not a visible grid); stage 2 draws *connectors* between stroke ends of different strokes, which then become the long lines "between things". Parameter: connector probability p_c per end, max span, and a rule that connectors join ends of strokes with unlike heading (>30 degrees) so they hook rather than parallel. Check: >=50% of long lines run between two earlier stroke ends. This matches Ian's "long open lines between things".
**Warning.** Regular armature exposes a grid look and calligraphic/"writing" feel (one handwriting everywhere). Marden's ghost lines: keep the armature invisible or jittered by >30% of cell.

### A5. Chinese brush: flying white (feibai) and cun — verified in part
**Procedure.** Feibai: as the brush dries its bristles separate and the stroke breaks into thin parallel lines with white gaps, giving mist, water or glancing light; cun (texture strokes) are made with the side of a dry brush, e.g. hemp-fibre (long wavy fibres, gentle slopes) and axe-cut (angled, chopped, hard rock) ([Asian Art Museum](https://education.asianart.org/resources/an-introduction-to-chinese-character-and-brushstrokes/); [Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Texture%20strokes/30786)). Key for us: one stroke = a *bundle that frays along its length*, with wide-to-thin as the bundle disaggregates.
**Rule.** "Fraying stroke": one path drawn as k parallel-ish strands that start tight (offset ~0.3 mm) and progressively separate and drop out along arc length, with the outermost strands ending last and wobbling more. Density gradient along the stroke replaces width. Parameters: k, fray rate, and dropout order (random, outer-first, or inner-first). Check: gap between strands increases monotonically on average, count decreases stepwise. Use *different fray types in different regions* (long wavy fibres vs short angled chops), as in cun vocabularies, for "different registers".
**Warning.** Long parallel fibres evenly spaced = fingerprint/engraving/weather-chart; short chops = "short straight hatch dashes" (rejected). Strands must diverge unevenly and one or two must wander. Do not imitate landscape motifs (mountain-shaped chops) or you drift to pastiche.

### A6. Ink/graphite tone by mark rather than smear (Celmins) — verified in part
**Procedure.** Celmins "re-describes" a photographic surface mark by mark; one implement for a whole image; the graphite point is "a point of reality", no smudging or grading ([Art21](https://art21.org/read/vija-celmins-building-surfaces/); [Smith College](https://scma.smith.edu/blog/vija-celmins-always-surface) via search). Density variation is obtained by mark count, with a uniform mark type.
**Rule.** Tone by count field: one field D(x,y) in [0,1] with slow low-frequency variation plus a couple of sharp step edges; every stroke's probability of passing a point is proportional to D. Volume appears when D has a smooth gradient across a form (dense to sparse), not when a form is outlined. Check: bundle line-count varies by >5x across a mass; gradient is spatially smooth (low Laplacian) inside masses.
**Warning.** Uniform "stipple" tone. Celmins's all-over field with no horizon is a good lesson against a map (no centre, no edge), but her photorealism is not a target.

### A7. Michaux, Twombly, Oehlen, LeWitt, Molnar, Mohr — brief
- **Michaux** (see v5 research 6): pulse and tremble as *rhythm along a line*; transferable: rhythm of restatement length (long, long, short, cluster) drawn from a repeating pattern with slip, not white noise. Warning: asemic writing.
- **Twombly** (v5 research 2): cancels. In the bundle context: 1 in ~8 restatements deliberately *crosses* the bundle at a steep angle then hooks back; this raises the sense of density without adding width. Warning: looping cursive.
- **Oehlen** (unverified; see `docs/IDEAS-oehlen-pass.md`): regimes collide; rule: bundle character changes at a boundary (tight/smooth to loose/tangled) within one line. Warning: two textures pasted side by side reads as collage. Blend across the boundary over 10-20 mm.
- **LeWitt wall drawings** (unverified from memory): a written rule executed with local freedom, e.g. "lines from four sides, not straight, not touching"; the rule set, not the hand, produces the whole. Transferable: **write 3-4 short rules per sheet and use them everywhere** (this is the "more unified" lever). Warning: LeWitt's rigid geometry pulls toward ruled parallels.
- **Molnar/Mohr** (Molnar covered in v5): Mohr's cube-based work (unverified) generates coherence through one small vocabulary of moves iterated by a fixed logic; same as LeWitt for unity. Warning: pure geometry.

## Part B. Landscape as process, not map

### B1. Diebenkorn, Ocean Park — verified in part
**Procedure.** After a 1970 commission from the Bureau of Reclamation he studied aerial views of fields, irrigation lines and land masses; the Ocean Park paintings (from 1967, aerial logic strengthened after) are built on a sketch, then painted over, scraped and corrected, with pentimenti and ghosted lines left visible ([Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/54173/watcher-from-the-skies-richard-diebenkorns-ocean-park-series/); [SFMOMA](https://www.sfmoma.org/artwork/72.59/)). Structure: a few long *straight-ish* lines cut the field into unequal zones; slight shifts, a line dropped and picked up again, a stroke left half-covered.
**Rule.** "Armature with drift": lay 3-6 long lines across the sheet at unequal spacing (ratio ~1:1.6:2.7 not equal), then *re-state* each line 1-3 times displaced by small consistent drift; a line may end in mid-sheet and resume 8-20 mm away, offset laterally (a break with continuity). Check: line spacing ratios non-uniform, at least 2 lines have a break-and-resume.
**Warning.** Straight cut lines quickly become "topographic/weather-chart" or an architectural grid. Curved-slack versions only, and never let lines meet at right angles in a repeated way. It also leans toward geometry versus Ian's organic ask; use as a *field armature*, not the drawing.

### B2. Mark Tobey, white writing — verified in part
**Procedure.** All-over: no focal centre, a "tangle of thin, continuous linear strokes" laid on an abstract field of thousands of densely interwoven strokes; built by accretion of layers, moving from loose strokes to tighter brushwork, dark to lighter (accounts in [HistoryLink](https://historylink.org/File/3894) and [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Tobey), secondary). Tobey tied the linear notation to Oriental calligraphy.
**Rule.** Loose-to-tight ordering plus all-over: draw in passes; pass 1 = few long, loose strokes covering the sheet (low density everywhere); pass 2 = medium; pass 3 = short, tight strokes concentrated in a small number of "knots". Sequence matters: the *last* passes are the shortest and densest and sit on top. Check: stroke length monotonically decreases with draw order; density map at end has >=2 knots and a large low-density field. Note: v5 already reversed arrival order and that helped; this is a separate axis (length by order).
**Warning.** White writing all-over, if uniform, becomes wallpaper and fingerprint tangle. Keep the field-with-knots imbalance. Pseudo-calligraphic marks are the "one handwriting" failure.

### B3. Emma Kunz — verified in part
**Procedure.** Pendulum dowsing on graph paper: the pendulum's swings and pauses give *points*; she then supplied the lines and colour, with symmetry and equally spaced structure common ([Springer, "The Envelopes of Emma Kunz"](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00283-024-10402-w); [Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/2024/05/26/emma-kunz/), secondary). The Springer paper (title only checked) frames her line families as envelopes.
**Rule.** Process-derived points, artist-chosen connection: generate a set of nodes from a dynamical process (e.g. a damped pendulum or a meander evolution), then draw *envelope families* (curves that all touch a moving curve) rather than the process trace. Envelope strokes fan and converge: a natural source of swelling bundles. Check: strokes share tangency with a parent curve, spacing along parent varies smoothly.
**Warning.** Envelope families are string-art/spirograph if regular, and bilateral symmetry brings mandala and moire. Truncate envelopes at random and never mirror.

### B4. Aboriginal Australian country painting (Yolngu rarrk / miny'tji) — verified in part; described respectfully
**Procedure.** For the Yolngu (Arnhem Land), designs are clan-owned and carry ancestral meaning; the surface is ochre-grounded, the design drawn out, then fine cross-hatched lines applied with a brush of human hair to produce *bir'yun*, shimmering brilliance, which signifies ancestral power ([NMA](https://www.nma.gov.au/audio/old-masters-exhibition-lecture-series/transcripts/meaning-in-yolngu-bark-paint); [Kate Owen Gallery](https://www.kateowengallery.com/page/Cross-Hatching-Painting-Rarrk); [Wikipedia, Mawurndjul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mawurndjul)). Country paintings from other regions (e.g. the Western Desert) show land as an aerial/plan-like arrangement of tracks, water and sites; the content is not a map but a story (unverified; not researched here).
**What to take, and what not.** The designs, motifs and cross-hatch patterns belong to specific clans and carry law; do not reproduce or evoke them. The generic technical fact we may learn from: shimmer arises from the *interference of two families of thin lines at a small angle*, which moves under the eye.
**Rule (technique only, our own vocabulary).** Interference patch: two bundles of thin lines crossing at a shallow angle (8-25 degrees), each with its own drifting spacing, clipped by a soft irregular region so that the patch edge is *a fade in stroke count*, not a border. Check: crossing angle within band, region membership by density falloff. Use in at most one or two places (the "net on one lobe" of Ian's brief).
**Warning.** Cross-hatch fields = tight ruled parallels/engraving (rejected) and would visually quote Indigenous art. Keep it a small, irregular net, not a filled panel; do not use the diamond/clan-panel format at all.

### B5. Aerial and process-derived abstraction (Denes, aerial photography, Hodgin) — partly unverified
**Procedure.** Agnes Denes (unverified, memory) presents ecological process as diagram or drawing with the paperwork visible; aerial photographers/painters (Diebenkorn above; Maya Lin's water-line works, unverified) flatten landform into contour plus texture. The failure mode in generative art is Robert Hodgin's *Meander*-type map: literal simulation shown from above.
**Rule.** "Process as author, not subject": run the meander/erosion process to make *decisions* (where a bundle swells, where two lines pass each other and cross-restate, where a hook forms at line end) but never draw the channel centreline as the subject. Apply the process's outputs to a different object (a drawn mass), e.g. use curvature-driven migration to decide restatement offset direction (bank erosion: bundle grows on the outside of a bend, single line on the inside). Check: no output line is a river centreline; but the offset field correlates with signed curvature.
**Warning.** If bundles form point-bar scroll patterns (arcs at regular spacing) it becomes tree rings; if you draw oxbows as closed loops it becomes map/blob. Break scroll spacing (irregular gaps; some scrolls skipped) and leave oxbows open.

## Ranked top 6 rules for "illusion of width and volume through lines" + "more unified" + "not a map"

1. **Lens bundle with off-centre belly and staggered ends (A2, with A1's non-uniform offset distribution).** This is the direct answer to "illusion of line width and narrowness": swell = strokes diverging then rejoining; taper = strokes ending. It works with the existing restatement machinery (change offset profile from constant to sin^p shape, plus stagger).
2. **Shadow-side bias for offsets (A3).** Turns a bundle from a thick line into a form turning. Cheap: one vector field and one bias parameter. Drift Lv slowly so masses do not all read as lit balls.
3. **Rules-per-sheet (LeWitt) as the unity lever (A7).** "Same 3-4 moves everywhere" makes seeds read as one hand and one logic without making them uniform. Choose rules that each name a *move* (fan, connect, hook, cancel), not a shape.
4. **Fraying stroke with density gradient along its length (A5).** Gives weight change *within a single stroke*, so a stroke can go from thick to thin to single without changing pen. Different fray behaviours per region give registers.
5. **Process as author, not subject (B5) with curvature-driven bias.** Use meander mechanics to drive offset direction and swell, never to draw the channel. Directly avoids the map risk; ties to rule 2 (bank erosion = shadow side).
6. **Connector stage (Marden, A4) + loose-to-tight order (Tobey, B2).** Long open lines between things; last strokes are short and dense and sit on top, giving knots on open ground.

Held back: interference patch (B4) as a one-use "net on one lobe" and armature-with-drift (B1) as a field skeleton; both riskier for rejected looks.
