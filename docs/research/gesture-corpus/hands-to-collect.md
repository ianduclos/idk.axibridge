# Non-artist hands to collect (research, 2026-10-16)

Ian asked for hands beyond painters: tribal tattoo forms and hot-rod culture
(pinstriping, flames). Web research only — **nothing downloaded**; every
download still needs Ian's go (name, source, size). "Unverified" = seen in a
search snippet, not opened.

## Recommendation (maximal contrast with Matisse's calm single contour)

1. **Copperplate / Spencerian flourishing** — Ames' *Book of Flourishes*
   (1890, LoC scan, ~125 designs, "no known restrictions";
   https://archive.org/details/amesbookofflouri00ames/). Tight loops, extreme
   thick/thin, high curvature. Comes with a kinematic model (Plamondon's
   sigma-lognormal, used by Berio & Leymarie for graffiti strokes).
2. **Carriage and wagon striping + scrolls** — the 19th-century ancestor of
   hot-rod pinstriping and the legal stand-in for it: symmetric tapered brush
   strokes, S-curves to points. Hillick, *Practical Carriage and Wagon
   Painting* (1903, Gutenberg; striping chapter lists 36 illustrations, scroll
   painting 18): https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/48173/pg48173-images.html ;
   Schriber (IA `completecarriage01schr`), Gardner (IA
   `carriagepainters00gard`) — plate counts unchecked.
3. **Art Nouveau whiplash** — Mucha, *Documents décoratifs* (1902, 72
   plates; Mucha d. 1939, PD in US and life+70). Commons category has ~98
   files mostly ~1000×1400 (check each file's licence tag):
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Documents_Decoratifs_(1901)
   Runner-up: **Celtic knotwork** from J. Romilly Allen, *Celtic Art* (1904,
   IA `celticartinpag00alle1904`) — avoid the Book of Kells (Trinity requires
   written permission).

Also available: sign-painter manuals 1902–1916 on IA (e.g.
`expertsignpainte00kell`), Ames' *Compendium* (1883, PD Mark but a flawed
watermarked scan), Grammar of Ornament (1856, IA `gri_33125008700086`; mostly
colour and dense), Cooper Hewitt open-access arabesque/grotesque prints
(check rights per object).

## Licensing and ethics — what to avoid

- **Modern tattoo flash** is the artist's copyright; buying flash licenses
  tattooing, not reproduction (*Tattoo Art v. TAT International*). No curated
  public-domain flash source was found; don't assume one exists.
- **Von Dutch / Ed Roth** work: treat as in copyright. Use carriage striping
  manuals for the technique instead.
- **Tā moko and other named indigenous systems** (Samoan pe'a, Marquesan…):
  moko is tapu and genealogical; non-Māori use is widely regarded as
  appropriation (Wai 262 treats designs as taonga). **Avoid**, including the
  Grammar of Ornament "Savage Tribes" plates 1–3 (colonial framing, objects
  collected without attribution). For "tribal" geometry use only generic
  Western neo-tribal blackwork from PD or self-made sources, or Ian's own
  spiky-blade forms — never labelled as any culture's.

## Geometry for these families

These hands are mostly **outlines of filled shapes** (blades, flames, licks)
or **symmetric pairs** (pinstripes), not single centrelines: the panel needs
a symmetry measure and a "filled blade" mode (outline κ(s) + width profile
along a medial axis) — see stage 2 of `docs/IDEAS-gesture-bank.md`.
Literature: Berio & Leymarie, sigma-lognormal graffiti strokes
(https://doc.gold.ac.uk/autograff/post/papers/strokes/); Berio, Leymarie &
Plamondon, kinematics from static traces
(https://doc.gold.ac.uk/autograff/post/papers/lognormality_2019/); Kaplan &
Cohen, computer-generated Celtic design (Eurographics 2003); Fisher &
Mellor, topology of Celtic knots (Bridges 2004); clothoid / pseudospiral
volutes (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.1737). No paper analyses whiplash,
flames, pinstriping or tribal forms geometrically — open ground.
