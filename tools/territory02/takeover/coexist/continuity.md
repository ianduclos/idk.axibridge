# Continuity experiment

Each shared smooth passage replaces its real rough source interval. Original source vertices before and after that interval survive; the old middle is absent. A few nearby surviving base strokes then carry the new direction briefly before returning to their old grain. Donors have staggered capture, release and occasional second snags. Some release sections are actually torn, with their source ranges recorded.

The complete drawing retains the existing base everywhere else. Followers are actual surviving base strokes, never invented as hatching. Their material-versus-bare classification is inferred: selection excludes a prefix equal to the original bare-stroke count, but prior clipping can remove or split strokes, so that prefix does not guarantee material ownership. Donor records and metadata explicitly carry this attribution limit. Every donor vertex survives subdivision and displacement except recorded tear gaps and recorded fragments below 0.12 mm; this can stretch its physical grain during the transition. Projection folds split the donor rather than connecting distant stations. Displacement contracts as a whole to fit the page; individual coordinates are never flattened against the boundary.

`develop(prepare(seed, baseName))` returns the complete drawing, replacement and donor provenance, and two diagnostic stages: source exchange and changed donor strokes alone. Development seeds are 13 and 24 with opening, pressure and unravel. Visual judgement remains with the primary agent; geometric checks alone do not establish artistic success.
