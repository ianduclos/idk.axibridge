# hdist: distinct hands under the zoom (round 16, thread B)

**Outcome.** The hands now look different under the zoom. Three things made them converge in round 15, and none of them was the hand. The biggest was the calibration: every hand was scaled so one quarter-turn was the same size. That made every hand equally curly. In v2 each hand is scaled by its own reference length (the size of its own drawings), so a curly hand stays curly and a sweeping hand stays sweeping. The camera is now the pen, and the zoom stops where the hand has nothing finer to show. On the eye measure, distinctness more than doubled: from 0.40 to 0.90 (the hands' own unzoomed lines score 1.31). On the 74-feature panel it went from 0.36 to 0.67 (native lines: 0.77). Each zoomed line also reads more like its own hand: its own hand's median rank went from 20.5th of 44 to 8th.

## What made the hands converge

I tested each suspect from the brief. No single knob in lcorp's voice made a real difference. The cause was the three together.

1. **One far scale for every hand.** lcorp set every hand's quarter-turn to the river's far quarter-turn (2.2 mm, about 1.6 mm after the fit). In their own drawings, hands turn at 7 to 30 mm per quarter-turn. So every far stretch was about 7 times too small to read: the same fuzz for every hand. Worse, setting one quarter-turn to one size removes the most distinctive thing about a hand: how much it turns for its size. river_bend turns 1.8 quarter-turns per reference length; mime_penguin turns 27. Calibrated by the quarter-turn, they come out equally curly.
2. **One camera for every hand.** σ = 1.76 mm / z^0.4 smoothed a stretch zoomed ×24 at about 12 mm on paper. Every corner became an arc, and every curl became a bend: "one or two big bends".
3. **One zoom for every hand (×24).** That is the river's range. Most hands run out of finer detail within ×4 to ×10. Past that point, every hand is a smooth balloon.
4. **`valley` was not a cause.** Removing it (valley0) made the hands slightly *less* distinct. The river-like course is a layout choice. It is not what averaged the hands.

## What I built

- `kit/voices_hdist.py`, voice `hdist_zoom`. It keeps lcorp's mechanism: the heading θ(u) is re-integrated at ds = c·z·du, the schedules are the same, and the width is constant. It imports `hand_chain`, `magnify_lod` and `zoom_measures` from lcorp; lcorp's files are not edited. Per hand:
  - `ladder`: the hand's scale ladder. This is where its turning lives in octaves of its quarter-turn: `k_fine` is its finest real band, and `span` is its range.
  - `calib "R"` (the default): one reference length of the hand is `R_mm` = 80 mm at z = 1. The zoom runs until the hand's finest band reaches `peak_fine_mm` (4 mm), so some hands get no zoom at all (river_bend, fly_open, trail_slime, trail_oaknet). `calib "ladder"` is the first fix, kept as a control.
  - The camera is the pen: `res_mm` 0.45 at every zoom (reveal 1).
  - `valley_R`: the course is straightened only above one reference length, never inside a drawing of the hand. `span` straightens a little more, by steps, until the line spans 80 % of its box.
  - `fill`: a second pass sets the drawn length so the fit scale is about 1, so the far scale is the scale on the sheet.
  - `source "relay"`: hands in turn, each whole and each at its own scales. Each hand gets one push, far at both ends. Each hand starts at the point in its chain that keeps the relay moving forward.
- `experiments/hdist_measure.py`: the measurement (below), the knob sweep and the zoom bands.
- `experiments/hdist_review.py`: rebuilds the six images. `experiments/hdist_look.py` makes the working sheets.
- `tests/test_kit_hdist.py` has 10 tests. They check:
  - the ladder;
  - the R calibration keeps curliness;
  - the zoom stops at the finest band;
  - the pen camera keeps the corners that lcorp's camera rounded;
  - one contiguous line in the box at one pen width;
  - far scales differ by hand;
  - in the relay, each hand is whole and every handover falls where both hands are far;
  - the eye cues read obvious cases correctly;
  - a score with hands as layers runs;
  - output is deterministic across processes.

  `test_kit.py`, `test_kit_synth.py` and `test_kit_lcorp.py` still pass.

## How distinctness was measured

- **Protocol.** 44 hands, 6 seeds each. Each line is drawn twice as long, in a cell twice as wide, at the same paper scale, so the measure reads the process and not one specimen's luck. Each line is read at its sheet size and pen width.
- **Panel.** The 74-feature panel with standard preprocessing (σ at 7 pen widths, the medium projector). Shape groups only (k, ext, infl, turn, cl), because the stroke groups read the format, not the hand.
- **Eye.** Six cues the eye uses: corner share, straight share, small loops, quarter-turn size, fine share and inflections. These are standardised on the hands' own unzoomed lines.
- **Scores.**
  - **d′:** the distance from a hand's centroid to its 5 nearest other hands, in units of the pooled within-hand spread (median over hands).
  - **Self-recognition:** the share of lines whose nearest centroid is their own hand (chance is 2 %).
  - **Own rank (the caricature check):** the rank of the zoomed line's own hand among the 44 hands' unzoomed lines (1 means it reads as itself).

| reading | v1 (lcorp) | first fix (`calib ladder`) | **v2** | v2 without zoom | hands' own lines |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| panel d′, 5 nearest | 0.36 | 0.45 | **0.67** | 0.67 | 0.77 |
| panel self-recognition | 0.05 | 0.10 | **0.19** | 0.21 | 0.24 |
| eye d′, 5 nearest | 0.40 | 0.58 | **0.90** | 1.22 | 1.31 |
| eye self-recognition | 0.16 | 0.19 | **0.38** | 0.47 | 0.48 |
| own hand's rank (median of 44) | 20.5 | 21 | **8** | 9.5 | 1 |

One knob at a time on v1 (eye d′ / panel d′):

| knob | eye d′ | panel d′ |
|---|---|---|
| v1 | 0.40 | 0.36 |
| no level of detail | 0.49 | 0.30 |
| reveal 1 | 0.45 | 0.36 |
| no valley | 0.28 | 0.30 |
| far quarter-turn 6 mm | 0.40 | 0.48 |
| far quarter-turn 12 mm | 0.33 | 0.47 |
| zoom ×6 | 0.46 | 0.33 |

Every knob alone stays close to v1.

What the numbers say:

- **The zoom itself still costs some distinctness** (eye 1.22 without zoom against 0.90 with it). A zoomed line mixes two sizes of the same hand.
- **The first fix was not enough.** An own camera and zoom set from the quarter-turn barely helped, because the quarter-turn calibration still made every hand equally curly.
- **Against the library's own pages, the rank is chance for everything**, the hands' own lines included (about 21st of 44). A single line in a cell is not a page of strokes, so I compared against the hands' own lines in the same cell instead.

## Duds and surprises (working images in `.local/gesture/out/hdist/`)

- **The quarter-turn calibration** (image 5) is the main lesson. Normalising by any turning-derived scale normalises curliness away.
- **Distinctness by measure walks to the density extremes** (`draft-7-extremes.jpg`). Over all five bands (×0.25 to ×4), 5 of the 8 most distinct hands were at an end band: one huge sweep (river_bend and fly_fru at ×4) or a dense crinkled strip (×0.25). Two of those dense strips, fly_open and river_meander, look alike to me, even though each measures far from the rest. This is the retired move ("density pushed to either extreme"), so the plate keeps to the middle three bands.
- **A bug in my own eye measure.** At first the loop cue counted a run of loops as one loop, so all the loopy hands read as loop-free. A synthetic test caught it, and I fixed it and re-measured everything. The headline numbers barely moved. The plate changed: the dense strips fell out, and polygon_walk, meander69 and dance_staccato came in.
- **Forcing the line to span the cell** by straightening the course harder (my first width fill, valley down to 2 quarter-turns) turned every hand back into a river. In v2 the straightening is in reference lengths, and the line may stay short of the width (shikasta and fly_fru stay compact).

## Honest read

- **Specimen and beside (1, 2).** These answer Ian's note directly. By eye the hands now part: polygon_walk is angular, trail_borer has big circles, river_bend is one sweeping wave, fly_fru is a knotted cluster, trail_miner is crenellated, and ingres is long open curves. I think he sees the difference at a glance.
- **What it costs.** The zoom is quieter. Many hands zoom only ×1 to ×3, and four not at all, because they have nothing finer to open. The far fuzz is gone, and with it the far-to-near drama of round 14. If he misses that, lcorp's zoom stays for that job; v2 is for hands kept apart.
- **Caricature.** By measure there is none: the zoomed lines sit nearer their own hands than before (rank 20.5 to 8). Nothing is exaggerated. A hand is only shown at its own size and its own resolution.
- **The plate (3).** It is truthful to the measure. There are eight different characters:
  - one sickle (trail_borer);
  - one wave (river_bend);
  - three dense knotted clouds (dance_staccato, mime_penguin, shikasta);
  - a knot–thread–knot (fly_fru);
  - crenellations (meander69);
  - angles (polygon_walk).

  The three clouds are the weak point: by eye they are closer to each other than the measure says. I am not sure he finds it beautiful. It is a map of difference.
- **Relay (4).** The strongest use of the difference: every handover is unmistakable. Seeds 3002 and 3003 travel well. In 3001 the red hand curls back over itself.
- **Score (5 hands, 6).** A busy, chaotic composition. The hands are distinct, but the yields (0.8 mm gaps) are too small to read at this size. I think it is not a keeper. It shows that hands-as-layers works in the score.
- **Legibility.** I checked by eye. dancer_anna has a "V"-like check stroke. mime_monkey_hands has repeated arches that could read faintly as cursive "nnn". No word reads.
- **Would he keep it?** I think the voice as the way hands are called (images 1 and 2) and the relay, yes. The plate and score are less certain.
