## The reference, conceptually (not as gestures to copy)

1. **Polyphony.** Several voices with incompatible logics share one surface and
   stay distinct; the interest is in their coexistence, not in any one of them.
2. **Threshold legibility.** Marks behave like language (rhythm, alignment,
   repetition, spacing) without content; meaning is suggested by arrangement.
3. **Weight as emphasis.** The same kind of utterance appears quiet and loud;
   weight is a decision about stress, not a smooth spatial field.
4. **Accumulation becomes a sign.** Restatement collapses into a thing, and a
   bound turns process into an object.
5. **Near-objecthood.** Forms pull toward recognition and stop short.
6. **Annotation.** Some elements cross or comment on others, so the parts are
   in conversation.

## Ian's clarification (read the fragments together)

"How different the grammars are, but how they are interesting and coherent yet
strange in themselves." The unit of interest is the **set of distinct
grammars**: each fragment obeys its own rules consistently (coherent), the
rules are unfamiliar (strange), and the fragments differ from one another
without merging. Principle 1 (polyphony) is the frame; the others describe
what makes a single grammar strange yet coherent.

## What it changes

- A. The bench is a **conversation between distinct voices** (hands, regimes,
  machine artefacts), designed for switching and contrast, never averaging.
  This is why blends collapsed while drift and the "failure" worked.
- B. **Regimes as prosody**: weight follows the line's own rhythm and emphasis
  (timing is the closest we have), not a spatial preset.
- C. **Legibility as a dial**: how strongly an arrangement behaves like
  language, independent of which shapes it uses.
- D. **Process as material**: diagnostics, intermediate states and errors are
  eligible voices.
- E. Corpora supply behaviours, never forms.
