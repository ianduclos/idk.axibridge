# Round 12, thread 6: revisit (2026-10-17)

The question was which of the ideas from rounds 5–7 are worth re-running with today's kit. The target is one of Ian's round-6 sentences that never got a real answer:
"the less obvious shapes, how they seem to communicate something".

## What I read and what I picked

- **Round 6: signs.** Signs were stamped beside the line. They read as objects laid on top of the drawing.
- **Round 7: signs as line events.** 17 operators (drop, knot, comma, comb, body, vessel …) grow the sign out of the line. The judge picked them over stamps 12 of 12 times. But the learned placement was at chance (4/12). So attachment carried the "belonging", and nothing decided which sign appeared, or why.
- **Round 6–8: structure fields** (Strahler/trunk, `regime-field-*`).
  - These cohere, but the kit already makes regimes and beads.
  - Re-showing them would be nostalgia.
- **Old dud, round 8: dance_structure "relative" and "graft".** They scribble at 15 s windows.
  - That failure comes from the window length, not the idea.
  - At a 1–2 s window it becomes usable. That is what this thread does.

**Idea.** A sign should say something, so its form needs a source. Round 7 sampled a shape from a fixed vocabulary.

Here the shape comes from the body:
- One limb's trail (the host) is one contiguous wire line.
- Wherever that limb holds still, the line leaves its path. It draws, small, what the other limbs did during the hold, as seen from the stopped limb.
- Then it closes back onto the stop and goes on.

Every sign is different and none is invented. They form one family because they come from one body.

Line → nib → wire: each figure rises into a nib (and toward mass where the watched limb nearly stopped), then falls back to wire. This is the round-6 "become one another", placed where the dance itself paused.

## What was built

- **`kit/voices_revisit.py`: voice `revisit_glyph`.**
  - Inputs: a dance trail, a host limb, and an optional second host on the same surface (`with_`, or `others` for all five).
  - Detects host holds (speed < `still` × the host's p90 for ≥ `min_hold_s`).
  - Builds each figure from the watched limb's motion relative to the host, over ≤ `max_fig_s` around the stop. The default watched limb is the "loudest" one, i.e. the longest path.
  - The figure is detrended into a closed excursion (`close=excursion`), or left open with the rest of the line displaced (`carry`).
  - Size: either `fit` (`fig` × the extent, × (motion / median)^`spread`) or `true` (honest scale).
  - Channels: r / W / M / phi, `glyph` (categorical), and `dance_t`, `dance_speed`, `dance_acc`.
  - `meta.figures` records each stop, its hold and the watched limb.
  - `draw=stops` draws the plain line with the same stops recorded, so other answers can sit at exactly those places.
- **`kit/modulators_revisit.py`: modulator `revisit_notate`.**
  - Reads event *kinds* off the dance clock: hold, reversal, launch, two limbs meeting, phrase end.
  - Each kind is always spelled by one round-7 operator (the `code`).
  - `source=stops` takes the voice's stops instead.
- **`kit/renderers_revisit.py`.**
  - `revisit_signs`: round-7 `apply_events` + `render_events`, one ink per mark group.
  - `revisit_nib`: the nib renderer with one Flexoki ink per mark (two pens).
- **`experiments/revisit_glyphs.py`.** Stages `sweep`, `sweep2`, `sweep3` and `review`; scratch output in `.local/gesture/out/revisit/`.
- **`tests/test_kit_revisit.py`.** 9 tests, 3 s.

Seed 1201 throughout. The voice draws no random numbers; the seed only feeds the c+e nib passes.

## What happened

1. **Notation v1** (`revisit_notate` on the plain dance voice: hold=knot, reverse=comma, launch=comb, meet=body, end=hook).
   - mantis: 26 ends, 21 launches, 4 reversals. levitation: 8 holds, 3 launches, 1 meeting.
   - It reads as beads and curls: hooks render as twin rings and knots as black dots.
   - **The dud of the thread.** A fixed operator code driven by real data still looks like decoration. Recurrence alone does not make the signs communicate.
2. **Figures v1.** Long holds (6–8 s on levitation) made figures as large as the drawing.
   - Capping the figure window (1.6 s) and fitting sizes fixed this.
   - `limbs=all` (one petal per limb) clots into dots. `loudest` reads.
3. **Excursion vs carry.**
   - Carry turns the figures into heavy strokes inside the line: close to what prosody and nib already do.
   - Excursion makes stand-alone figures: insect-, flower- and Michaux-like. Kept.
4. **Every trail × host (45 sheets).**
   - Dense dances (outofbalance, thrusting and staccato hands, hummingbird) bury the figures.
   - Sparse hosts carry them: levitation's hands and head, the mantis's head and feet, and the feet of particles, staccato and thrusting. A foot gives a floor line with figures where it stood.
5. **Two hosts, two pens.** Particles, right foot (black) + head (red), at true body positions.
   - The foot's one great lift is a bold single arch.
   - The figures sit on its floor line, and the head writes above it.
   - Head: 5 stops of 0.4–1.0 s; 37 % of its line is figure.
   - Foot: 5 stops of 2–11 s; 24 % of its line is figure.
   - Watched limbs: the hands, every time.
6. **Size spread.**
   - At equal size (`spread` 0.4) the floor figures line up on a baseline like letters ("g ø B").
   - `spread` 1.0 lets the bigger motions grow, and the figures overlap and stop reading as a word.
   - 5 figures per host read better than 8.
   - Mass cores (`slow_mass` 0.85 with a 3.5 mm mass) put round dots at figure ends; Ian has rejected dots for transitions, so they were dropped.
7. **The rows layout** (every limb in its own ruled band, like a score) turns into five lines of handwriting. That is the round-11 "too similar to real" trap, so it was dropped.

**Frame: 150 × 210 mm portrait.** The figure is a standing body, head over foot, and its content is taller than wide. Portrait plots on the AxiDraw rotated (210 < 218 mm). The variant is landscape 240 × 180, because levitation's content is wide.

## Honest read

- **The sentence it answers.** I think this answers "how they seem to communicate something" better than rounds 6–7 did.
  - The signs differ from each other for a reason you can sense: each one is a stop.
  - They are the same kind of thing as the line they hang on.
  - The comparison image (3) shows it more clearly than any claim I can make.
- **Would Ian keep the piece? Maybe.**
  - For: the bold foot arch (he liked bold single gestures), two pens (the chorus), "kinematics driving other stuff", and the figures are not dots.
  - Against, risk 1: the floor figures still flirt with letters (a "g", a "B"/flag shape), even at `spread` 1.0.
  - Against, risk 2: the head's stacked figures make a tangle rather than a sign.
  - Both need his eye, and a legibility/face check by a person.
- **The variant** can read as a creature.
- **The dud** is the most useful negative: a sign code keyed to real events still decorates. The form of the sign has to come from the event.
- **Sentence 2** ("become one or the other in space") is answered only partly: wire → nib figure → wire at the stops. Mass is deliberately light, after Ian's notes on dots.
