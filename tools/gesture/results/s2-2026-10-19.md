## listen as a writer: how it works

- Speakers and the river are parts with a written place (centre, turn), so a
  hand swap re-lays the new line in the same spot.
- Listeners are a part (their own hand) plus a grow relation that picks the
  gesture on the source and places itself beside it at play time: edit a
  speaker and its listeners follow. A listener that can no longer be placed
  draws nothing (`unplaced`); a pin holds one still.
- The void is a written place (its edge a `lines` layer) plus a `mask`
  relation: earlier layers are cut, later ones cross it, exceptions in `flip`.
- Writing as the first writer draws from round-15 listen's own random stream,
  so palette, flow, bold voice, speaker count, river and the speakers match
  round 15 for the same seed. From the first listener on the sheets diverge.
- 0.8–2.0 s to write, 0.8–1.5 s to play, per sheet.

## Lead's read (written before Ian's notes)

1. **The written sheets read as listen.** Same frames, palettes, speakers and
   weight hierarchy as round 15 and integ16. Moving the history into the
   score didn't lose the composer.
2. **They are a little denser in the middle.** Listeners now place
   themselves one by one against the ink already there, with awareness acting
   step by step rather than once at the end. 15103 is the densest and most
   angular (close to "jumbled"); 15104 and 15107 are more open than integ16.
3. **Follow is visible, and wholesale.** One hand swap (15102's L2 to
   hokusai) moves its listeners to the other side of the sheet; pinning one
   listener holds it. A single edit can rearrange most of a sheet. That is what
   "follow" asks for, but it may be more than you want from one click.
4. **Recompose keeps the lock, not the palette.** The locked red line stays
   put while each new seed picks its own pens. The kept line then reads as a
   foreign pen (purple at 15201, magenta at 15203). That could be a feature
   (one voice from another drawing) or a clash; the alternative is that the
   writer builds its palette around the kept pens. 15202 comes out sparse and
   left-heavy.
5. **Every study works as a sealed layer.** The charts and atlases sit as
   blocks of document inside a drawing; the line studies (leaf_miner, bold
   swell, brood gallery) sit as figures the hand line crosses.
6. **ccolour and arch stay closer to their composers than listen does.**
   Same found ground, containers, traverses, intruders and round swell beads
   at the same seed (their decisions come from the composers' own streams);
   catches and pen events land elsewhere. Some cores open up (15044, 4544),
   some hold (4548).
7. **Two writers on one sheet make a diptych, not a dialogue.** listen left
   and arch right never meet, so no relation fires between them. Overlapping
   regions are the next thing to try.
8. **Legibility check:** ccolour 4544's blue line at bottom left reads like
   cursive Latin, in the composer and the writer alike (inherited).
9. **Speed:** ported ccolour/arch play in 1–5.5 s against 0.5–2.4 s for the
   composers; live preview will lean on the per-layer cache.
10. **My likely error this time:** judging fidelity ("does it still look like
   listen?") when what matters is whether the sheets are alive, and calling
   Follow a success because the mechanism works when a single edit may
   rearrange too much.

## Contract gaps found

- A pinned relation-only layer failed validation (fixed: a pin is content).
- `unplaced` counted against a budget (fixed: neutral).
- `*` filters are symmetric: "ground × mine, not ground × ground" can't be
  said; the listen writer uses `except` with the ground's ids. Open.
- Sealed composers were validated at a 10 mm probe frame (fixed: their region).
- Open, for an S0″ before or during S4 (both writer threads hit the first):
  1. side-specific wildcard filters (`src_roles` / `dst_except` …);
  2. a budget shared by several relations (`budget_group`): ccolour splits
     its one per-sheet event budget by weight;
  3. a relation can't read its dst layer's renderer spec (only `pen_mm`);
  4. `src: "*"` with a named dst fires on every later layer too (by design;
     document it more loudly);
  5. the list-src cap of 8 trims catch's heard lines on found-heavy sheets.
