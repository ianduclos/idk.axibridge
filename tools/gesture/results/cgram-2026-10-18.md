# cgram: compositions as a grammar (round 14, thread b3, 2026-10-18)

**Outcome.** One procedure, one fixed vocabulary, and twelve consecutive seeds
(1460–1471, not picked) give twelve sheets that do not share a layout. They run
from four moves on a nearly empty sheet to an all-over sheet of 38 moves. Total
ink varies 34× across the twelve and the heavy-pen share varies from 0 % to
72 %. The flat control (the same grammar with no per-sheet temperament) varies
ink only 5×, and every sheet becomes "a bit of everything". The control is not
dull soup, though, and some of its sheets are good. The grammar works. The
temperament is what makes it vary.

Code: `kit/arrangers_cgram.py` (arranger `cgram`, voice `cgram_free`, sampler
`sample_sentence` / `recipe`). Experiment: `experiments/cgram.py`. Tests:
`tests/test_kit_cgram.py` (13 pass; `test_kit.py` passes too). Working images:
`.local/gesture/out/cgram/`.

## The grammar in short

A composition is a **sentence**: moves separated by `;`. Each move acts on the
sheet so far and can refer back to an earlier move with `@k`:

```
reserve x=0.18 y=0.5 w=0.38 h=0.82 ; seed p=fly x=0.79 y=0.52 r=0.11 n=2 len=3.6 pen=0.71 play=catch ; …
cover at=dense … ; cross over=@10 p=coiled pen=1.6 ; contain … ; cross over=@10 p=thrusting pen=1.42
```

It has 19 moves. The note's list is complete: **reserve · seed · traverse ·
intrude · contain · spill · cover · cross (the cover) · contaminate · trail
(off) · stop (in the air) · counter(weight) · repeat**. The plan adds
**paste · answer · zoom · morph**, plus `line` (a free middle hand) and
`soup` (mutual as a block). The players are the recipe's parts, named by
label. The vocabulary is the same for every sheet:
- found matter: the river (pasted at a zoom drawn per paste, 250–3600 channel
  widths, never one fixed far zoom), the chart, and the handover specimens
  (key, waves, coiled);
- seven real hands;
- the morphing lines (gmorph, belt, passage, waves) and the seed-3966 free hand;
- limb signs and river loops;
- mutual's soup.

Lines that listen use the kept mechanisms as they are. `play=ride` is found
ground's negotiate (ride, leave at a tangent, ignore). `play=catch` is
contagion's walk (catch the river, the chart or an earlier line's hand, carry
it away, shed it whole). `answer` is found_play's answer: it traces beside the
caller's end and grows out of it.

**The sampler is the point.** It draws a *temperament* before any move:
- sentence length, log-uniform from 3 to 44;
- move weights, a sparse Dirichlet draw (α 0.45), so each sheet has a few
  dominant moves;
- player weights (α 0.3), so each sheet has a dominant register;
- a density dial, Beta(0.45, 0.45), so the extremes are common;
- a core position and an optional second core;
- a decided blank, opposite the core;
- whether a heavy intruder comes, and the weight range;
- how strictly the habitual order is kept (found → structure → hand → accents).

Then it draws the moves one at a time, only among the moves the sheet allows
(a cross needs a cover, a spill needs a container). Arguments are drawn around
the sheet's own decisions. Nothing is scored, kept or bred (§7).

Frame: **220 × 300 mm portrait, the same for every sheet**. 20 of the 29 Oehlen
sheets are portrait (about 1.3–1.45). The note says a series *keeps* its
format, so the format is the one thing held fixed.

## Where the note's principles land

- **The moves list** is the move set. Each move is a function on the sheet, and
  the log (`marks[0].meta["cgram"]["moves"]`) records the ink every move touched.
- **"Same vocabulary, distributed and weighted differently, with the
  composition's decisions (core, blank, dominant register, intruder, exits)
  re-made per sheet."** The vocabulary is the fixed player list, and the
  weighting is the temperament's Dirichlet draws. The decisions are drawn per
  sheet: core, a decided blank (`reserve` pushes later placements out of it;
  traverse, trail and cross may still run into it), dominant player, intruder
  yes/no. Exits come from `trail`, which runs into the blank and to an edge,
  and from `traverse`, which runs edge to edge.
- **"Dials whose settings should themselves vary per sheet, including to their
  extremes."** The density dial is Beta(0.45, 0.45). Sentence length runs from
  3 to 44. The order habit runs from 0.4 to 0.97, so a cover or an intruder
  sometimes comes early (T5's reversal). Weight runs from a 0.15 mm hairline to
  2.6 mm bars.

## What happened on the way (numbers as evidence)

- **Moves that did nothing.**
  - The first sweeps logged dead moves: undrawn containers, trails from ends
    already at the paper's edge, counters at a few mm, covers over blank.
  - Each now acts on the nearest thing it can, or is drawn: a cover over
    nothing moves onto the densest passage, a trail uses the free end, a late
    blank with nothing under it clears the core instead.
  - Over 40 more seeds, only rare cases remain (a trail when every line runs
    edge to edge).
  - The test asserts that every move touched ink. A reserve made first is
    tested instead on holding less than its share of the ink: in 1430 it held
    5 % of the ink on 17.5 % of the area.
- **A real bug in the shared clipping, found here.**
  - `clip_rect` and `split_by_covers` (in `arrangers_oehlen_found`) use shapely
    intersection. Shapely *nodes a line that crosses itself*: a test knot fully
    inside the sheet came back as 51 pieces.
  - Pieces shorter than `min_stub` are then dropped. So dense self-crossing
    lines lose fragments at every crossing, and a cover also deletes short
    pieces far away from it.
  - cgram uses its own point-mask clip (`runs_by_mask`), and a test pins this.
    The shared files were not changed (see below).
- **Legibility.** The seed-3966 free hand drawn small reads as a line of
  cursive ("…tosonz…"). It is now never drawn under 150 mm wide. Shrinking
  zooms are kept to ×0.35 or more.

## Honest read

- **Different every time: yes.** This is the population's strength.
  - Twelve unselected seeds cover T1's whole range (four marks on a blank sheet
    up to all-over), and container or no container.
  - They also differ in dominant move (cross, repeat, stop, morph, cover,
    seed), heavy against hairline, and where the core and blank sit.
  - None of them is a fixed layout with a varying line.
- **More dynamic inside a sheet: partly.**
  - 1467 and 1463 have real contrast: scale (hairline loops against a knotted
    core), weight (1.6 mm crosses against 0.3 mm), density against a decided
    blank, and order (a cover cut back, then crossed).
  - Many sheets read as scattered, though: small events spread around without
    one long line to tie them (the note's T2 traverse). The temperament often
    draws no traverse at all.
- **The relation between regimes is weaker than in the single-mechanism pieces.**
  Rides and catches happen, but a move rarely *depends* visibly on what is
  there. The covers and crosses read best because they are relations by
  construction. The pastes are often too small or too far from the lines to be
  ridden.
- **Would Ian keep it?** I think he keeps the procedure and perhaps one or two
  sheets: 1467, and 1464 if he still rewards "too much". He will find some
  sheets too sparse or too random to be pieces (1460, 1468, 1471). Those are
  the extremes the note asked for, but a random extreme is not a decided one.
- **Concerns.**
  - The handover "key" and the stair contamination bring right-angle staircases
    onto several sheets, near what he disliked ("staggered barbed straight
    lines").
  - The heavy "st" stroke pair at the bottom of 1467 could read as letters.
- **Next, if kept.**
  - Make a sentence more often contain one long traverse that the other moves
    refer to. It would be a weight, not a rule.
  - Let paste place found matter where later lines will pass.

## Proposed core changes (not made)

- `arrangers_oehlen_found.clip_rect` / `split_by_covers`: replace shapely's
  intersection/difference with a point-mask split (as `arrangers_cgram.runs_by_mask`),
  so self-crossing lines are not noded into fragments and dropped under
  `min_stub`. This would change output for `oehlen_found`, `rules`, `contagion`,
  `found_play` wherever a dense line meets a cover or a paste edge, so it needs
  a parity check and Ian's eye. It is not default-preserving.
