# Sources as drivers, not hands — round 12, thread `drive` (2026-10-17)

The piece is one line drawn by an existing hand (`river_meander`) and steered by two recorded
animals, a stork and a mosquito. Neither animal draws its own shape. While the stork is on the
ground, the line is a broad-nib ribbon; while it flies, the line thins to a hairline. The
mosquito's heading turns the nib, so when the fly spins in the simulator the ribbon twists. The
mosquito's steering picks the pen: black, red or blue. With the drivers hidden, the drawing
still has a rhythm the hand alone does not have: long calm ribbon, one knot of twisting colour,
and a few sudden hairlines. A phase-randomised control shows that the fly's burstiness is what
makes that rhythm.

## What I built

- **Drivers** (`library/drive_signal/drive_*`, new kind, 1.1 MB, frozen by
  `experiments/drive_sources.py`). Each is a time series only; no positions are stored.
  - `drive_mosquito`: a tethered mosquito in a closed-loop flight simulator (zenodo 5017751),
    12.5 min resampled to 40 Hz. Channels are steer (L−R wingbeat), effort (L+R), heading
    (continuous, trial resets removed) and trial.
  - `drive_stork`: "Oduella", Sicily 2015, 161 days of 5-min GPS. Channels are altitude,
    speed, gap and hour.
  - `drive_tropicbird`: one 10 h foraging trip, 1-min GPS, speed.
  - `drive_mosca`: mosca-draw `open_fru_s11`, L/R motor-pool rates. Simulated.
  - `drive_surrogate_mosquito`: a control. The mosquito with its Fourier phases randomised
    (seed 5813). It has the same spectrum but no bursts.
- **Modulators** (`kit/modulators_drive.py`). Each one lays a window of a driver along the
  part's arc length, so the whole window spans the whole line. The window, transform
  (value, abs, rate, envelope, highpass), norm, smoothing and mapping are all bounded params
  and are recorded in `meta["drive"]`.
  - `drive_width` writes the swept width `dw` (or `W` / `M`).
  - `drive_regime` sets wire / nib / mass by Schmitt thresholds, with a minimum run and a ramp.
  - `drive_phi` turns the nib angle by the driver's angle, with a soft cap in °/mm.
  - `drive_pen` writes `pen_i` with hysteresis and a minimum run.
  - `drive_knots` turns pauses into knots, sized by √duration.
  - `drive_tremor` takes the signal's own high-pass, gated by its envelope.
- **Renderer** `drive_ink` (`kit/renderers_drive.py`). It splits a mark into pens, each
  carrying `Ink.meta["colour"]`. The body is either a swept disc (line-built) or the research
  nib. Knots are wound or beaded. `render_colour` / `save` write colour previews.
- **Arranger** `drive_beside` (`kit/arrangers_drive.py`). `show` = hidden | beside | measure.
  The measurement is laid out as a drum record: the same line straightened into rows, inked by
  the same renderer, over the raw driver traces that steered it. The pen trace is coloured by
  the pen it chose.
- **Experiment**: `experiments/drive_compose.py`. Its stages are width, pens, hands, nib, combo,
  seeds, stork, pair and final.
- **Tests**: `tests/test_kit_drive.py`, 16 tests, about 2 s.

## What happened

1. **Width from the stork** (`.local/gesture/out/drive/width`). Altitude mapped to width gives
   sausages, the capsules Ian dislikes (dud a). Climb rate gives rosaries: clusters of beads
   for each day's thermal climbs, separated by long bare stretches. The rhythm is real, but it
   reads as "still dots" (dud b).
2. **Pens from steering**. The raw steer signal is biased to one side, so only blue ever
   appeared. A high-pass filter (τ 3 s) plus `stretch` after smoothing brought red back.
3. **Nib angle from heading**, the turn of this thread. The mosquito spins in some trials, up
   to 18,000° in 30 s. Uncapped, at gain 1.0, that becomes a barbed sawtooth (dud c). Capped at
   12°/mm it becomes a ribbon that twists for a stretch and then settles. Nothing else in the
   bank does this.
4. **Stork as weight**. The rule is ground → ribbon, flight → hairline (inverted speed, flight
   at 12 % of the window's p98, minimum run 4 mm, ramp 2 mm). At 8 days on 1448 mm (about
   181 mm per day) this gives 10 hairline passages totalling 177 mm.
5. **Piece numbers** (seed 3301, frame 280 × 210 mm, inside the AxiDraw's 300 × 218 so the
   measured version plots on one sheet).
   - Stork: days 60–68, 0.0021 mm per stork-second.
   - Mosquito heading: 420–520 s, 14.5 mm/s.
   - Mosquito steer: 380–580 s, 7.2 mm/s.
   - 15 pen runs, 19 % of the line coloured; the ribbon is twisting (> 6°/mm) on 28 % of it.
6. **Honest test** (image 3). The phase-randomised control spreads pen changes evenly: 33 runs,
   49 % coloured, and twist burstiness (CV over 50 mm windows) of 0.80. The real fly gives 1.38.
   The surrogate looks like a pleasant, evenly sprinkled chorus; the real fly gives one incident
   and long calm. A calm window of the same fly gives 8 runs and little twist. So what drives
   the rhythm is the source's intermittency, not just modulation.

## My honest read

I think Ian keeps **1** and **2**, maybe as a pair:
- The twisting ribbon is new.
- Pen changes come from a fly's turns rather than a rule (multi-pen, as he liked in the
  chorus).
- The drum record is a "drawing beside its measurement" whose measurement is the drawing
  itself, straightened.

Risks:
- The hand (river_meander) is calm, and some may find it too smooth or "designed".
- The stork's part is the quietest. Hairlines are a light touch, and at viewing distance some
  of them read as nib thin-spots.
- In image 2 the traces are a chart on purpose; the drawing is not.

I would not keep the bead or tremor directions.

## Could not / caveats

- The fly data could not be read with h5py, which the venv lacks; I used Homebrew `h5dump`.
- `hover_vision.h5`'s `/df` is byte-identical to `mos_vision.h5`'s, so there is only one fly.
- The Iceland seismogram scans were not used, because trace extraction is a project of its
  own.
- `drive_mosca` and `drive_tropicbird` are frozen and tested but not in the piece.
- `test_kit.py::test_committed_library_shape_and_budget` fails because the local corpus dirs
  `animal` and `fabric` have no library corpus entries. That predates this thread; the round-11
  harvest added them. All other `test_kit.py` tests pass, including the discovery check.
