## a. The bodies of work on paper, as procedures

Dates are those the sources give. "Found" means printed or pre-existing matter
that Oehlen did not draw. "Made" means his own marks.

### A1. Collage-drawings: line over printed matter (c. 1982 onwards; dense 2004–2010, 2013, 2025–26)

- **Media and scale.** Collage, ink, pencil, sometimes etching, plastic sheet,
  white paint, on paper. Mostly sheet size: 15–56 cm (2004–05 collages at
  Hetzler), 25–35 cm (2008–2010 at Corbett vs. Dempsey and Luhring Augustine).
  Some are very large, such as the Pinakothek der Moderne's 2009 collage on
  paper at 200 × 154 cm, and a 100 × 70 cm sheet shown in Athens.
- **What is found.** Posters, postcards, stickers and magazine
  advertisements (Gagosian Athens 2022, describing six 2009 collages).
  Several 2009 sheets are drawn **on found artwork**: CvD lists "ink, pencil,
  etching and mixed media collage on found artwork" and "on found paper".
- **How the line treats the print.** Oehlen wants the found pieces to read as
  formal shapes, not as stories or adverts (Taschen interview extract, The
  Quietus). He describes the found base as noise that he then smooths
  down (Interview Magazine, 2009). For the 2009 poster paintings, Brooklyn Rail
  read the overpainting as a floating foreground, close to graffiti on a wall.
  Neither layer wins: they clash or merge, and you have to sort out which. The
  pictures stop short of finished on purpose. Halves are left blank, and the
  parts sit like windows on a screen.
- **Covering as a mark.** In the Conduction and Computer works on paper, some
  passages are covered with collaged paper or smeared with white paint, which
  leaves a pentimento (Max Hetzler, 2026 press text). Subtraction counts as an
  event in the drawing.
- **Order is fixed.** Posters are glued down first and painted over second,
  never the other way round (Quietus extract). On canvas this became the
  Finger Paintings (2008 on): cheap advertising posters stuck down, then
  worked with the bare hands, rags and spray.
- **Recent.** Hetzler Marfa 2022 showed six "sculpture drawings" in ink,
  graphite and collage on paper. Four have cut-out images. Their lines are
  partly freehand and partly stencilled. Hetzler Berlin 2026 shows
  pencil, ink and collage sheets from 2013, and new 2025–26 sheets in
  silkscreen, lacquer, pencil, ink and collage on handmade paper.

### A2. Drawing over someone else's drawing: the Krebber leaves (1994)

- In 1992 Michael Krebber published a portfolio of 48 loose plates
  reproducing his works on paper. In 1994 Oehlen drew a **series of 16
  sheets** directly over selected plates, and showed them in 1995 in
  *Abortion of the Cool* in Augsburg.
- **Procedure.** On some sheets Oehlen brings in motifs from his own work.
  On others, **the forms emerge out of Krebber's reproduced drawing beneath**.
  The other artist's line is the substrate and the cue. Buchholz showed the
  16 sheets again in 2018 (*Works on Works on Paper*), next to the Krebber
  originals that can be seen under them.
- Other collaborations on paper and print: Kippenberger and Büttner
  (*Wahrheit ist Arbeit*, catalogue, Folkwang 1984), prints with Christopher
  Wool (1018 ART, 2007), *Line Packers* with Peppi Bottrop (2018; Oehlen's
  computer paintings hung on Bottrop's charcoal wall drawings), Carroll Dunham
  (*Bäume / Trees*, 2019–21), Kim Gordon (CvD 2025). Online sources only list
  these; how the collaborative sheets were made is not described except for
  the Krebber leaves.

Sources: [Buchholz press release, via dailyartfair](https://dailyartfair.com/events/download_press_release/7722) ·

### A3. Small source, hand enlargement: Computer drawings and *Conduction* (1990–2008; 2009 onwards)

- **Computer.** In 1990 Oehlen drew with a mouse in a basic paint program on
  a laptop. He printed the drawings, then silkscreened and enlarged them, and
  finished them by hand in oil, acrylic, charcoal and spray (Hamburger
  Kunsthalle 2024; Kunstkritikk). There are works on paper too: a 2008 sheet
  of 199 × 240 cm in India ink, charcoal and spray paint on paper. Its
  inkjet-stepped lines turn into loose charcoal arabesques, and grids sit
  against plant-like tendrils, with faces that form and dissolve (Sotheby's
  2018 lot note). Revisited from 2024 on handmade paper (Hetzler 2026).
- ***Conduction*.** From 2009 the computer line is dropped. Oehlen makes
  **ten small drawing collages**, then enlarges their graphics by hand onto
  canvas or acrylic glass, painting and drawing into them with spray and
  charcoal (CvD 2011, via The Visualist). Jahn und Jahn (2025) says each
  enlarged stroke keeps the energy of its small source. Oehlen describes his
  practice as a procedure of instructions and automatisms with no target look
  (Thomas Dane / CvD catalogue 2011, as quoted by Phillips).
- **The name.** Butch Morris's "conduction" is structured improvisation. A
  conductor gives real-time cues (sustain, repeat, change) to improvising
  players, which Hetzler 2026 calls controlled freedom. John Corbett's phrase
  for Oehlen is order without dominion.

Sources: [Hamburger Kunsthalle](https://hamburger-kunsthalle.de/en/node/3002) ·

### A4. Large charcoal and the wall drawing (2012, 2016)

- **Media and scale.** Charcoal only, on paper of about 300 × 200 cm: Museum
  Brandhorst P 03/12 and P 05/12, a Phillips lot of 299 × 199.5 cm, and seven
  sheets of 295–300 × 200 cm at Hetzler 2016. The 2016 show also had a
  **wall drawing of 350 × 1034 cm**, drawn on site.
- **Procedure.** Few lines, each one a trace of a whole-body gesture with
  sudden turns. Oehlen **doubles existing lines**, **smudges** the charcoal,
  **erases parts completely**, and **leaves blank spaces deliberately**. The
  result looks impulsive but is constructed throughout (Hetzler 2016;
  Gagosian Rome 2012, via Wanted in Rome). Holzwarth's book text says the
  wall drawing's apparent spontaneity must follow an exact plan.
- **Line versus mass.** This is line, with mass only as smudge. Hyperallergic
  (Cleveland 2017) called the charcoal lines diaphanous and linked them to
  Surrealist automatism. It also judged them too thin for their size, a
  useful warning about scaling a light line up.

Sources: [Hetzler, Works on Paper 2016](https://www.maxhetzler.com/exhibitions/albert-oehlen-works-paper-2016) ·
[Holzwarth Publications](https://holzwarth-publications.de/pages_books/_out/oehlen_paper.html) ·

### A5. Small ink and watercolour drawings (2014; 2020 LA; 2022; 2023–24)

- **Media and scale.** 2014: ink on paper, 24.5 × 18.5 cm, a run of at least
  19 sheets (Hetzler 2016). 2020: ink on handmade paper, large and small,
  drawn in Los Angeles (Jahn und Jahn 2021). 2022: watercolour, ink and
  pencil on carton, 30.5 × 22.9 cm, plus larger ink, pencil and watercolour
  sheets tied to the Ö-Norm paintings (Gagosian Athens). 2023–24:
  watercolour, and watercolour over graphite (Art Institute of Chicago,
  2024, 110 × 107 cm).
- **Procedure.** Restless dashes cross and overlay, condense into **tortuous
  bundles**, then **run off into the empty sheet**. Bodies or single limbs
  show for a moment and dissolve back into abstraction (Hetzler 2016). The
  line goes from whisper-thin tracing to dense knots, and motifs flirt with
  figuration and fall back (Jahn und Jahn 2025). Lines often reach the edge
  of the sheet (Gagosian Athens 2022). The drawing is fast, but the
  structure is controlled in detail (Jahn und Jahn 2021).

Sources: [Hetzler 2016](https://www.maxhetzler.com/exhibitions/albert-oehlen-works-paper-2016) ·
[Jahn und Jahn 2021](https://jahnundjahn.com/exhibitions/detail/albert-oehlen-1) ·

### A6. The tree as a program (1988 onwards; on paper as collage and drawing)

- **The rules (1988).** The figure is vertical and centred, and the lines
  narrow as they move away from the tree's midpoint (Guggenheim Bilbao
  teachers' guide). Oehlen called the tree a program for his work rather
  than a motif (Cleveland press text). Later only the figure and ground
  colours are fixed in advance, and the branches go by impulse. In 2015 he
  said no detail and no aspect is fixed.
- **Collage.** *Bäume* (2004, oil and paper on wood, 265 × 385 cm) puts one
  painted tree among appropriated printed rows of trees along a road
  (Cleveland). The found trees and the made tree sit side by side, the same
  motif in two regimes.
- **Look versus making.** The 2013+ trees are hand-painted so cleanly that
  they read as design-software output. The 2022 sculpture drawings carry the
  same eccentric tree lines onto paper (Hetzler Marfa).
- Ian's early note ("Think Oehlen's trees") fits these trees: contiguous,
  eccentric lines, a rule-built figure, no hatching.

Sources: [Guggenheim Bilbao, Untitled (Tree 1)](https://guggenheim-bilbao.eus/en/learn/schools/teachers-guides/untitled-tree-1) ·

### A7. Working rules (across all bodies)

These are paraphrased from interviews.
- Self-imposed rules have been a way to start without a style since his
  student years with Polke. Some are trivially simple, such as "Work slower".
  Some contradict each other, and he tries them anyway.
- He changes materials on purpose to create insecurity. For example, he
  switched from acrylic to oil because oil gave him less control (Interview
  2009; Hyperallergic, *Beer with a Painter*, 2017).
- He admires de Kooning's Black Mountain exercise of spending days on one
  drawing: time as an open experiment that may improve the drawing or
  ruin it.
- He keeps a practical to-do list, for example "smaller now", or "this motif
  across several pictures" (Quietus).

Sources: [Interview Magazine](https://www.interviewmagazine.com/art/albert-oehlen) ·
[Hyperallergic, Beer with a Painter](https://hyperallergic.com/370874/beer-with-a-painter-albert-oehlen/)

**Not covered online.** Two catalogue essays are the deepest sources and
are print-only. Jenny Graser's essay covers the drawings and collages from
the 1980s to now. Kito Nedo's essay covers the Ömega collages. Both are in
*Albert Oehlen*, Jahn und Jahn / Walther König, 2025. The 1980s collaborative
sheets, and the 1983 MoMA drawing in crayon on seventeen pieces of paper, are
documented only by title.

## b. What this adds beyond `docs/IDEAS-oehlen-pass.md`, and what to avoid

The July pass read the **Computer Paintings**: machine-native marks at hostile
scale, regime collision, line weight as composition, interruption, and the bad
hand recorded faithfully. It turned all of that into AI-age vernacular
(certainty as weight). The works on paper add **relations and procedures**,
not marks:

1. **Two relations to a found layer, switched as events.** The line either
   **borrows**, taking its forms out of what is beneath (the Krebber leaves),
   or **ignores**, passing indifferently over it (the July pass's
   "indifferent line"). The July pass had only the second.
2. **Covering is a mark.** A pasted patch or white smear subtracts a passage
   and the drawing goes on over it. Pentimento is part of the composition.
3. **Scale transfer as performance.** A small, fast source is re-performed
   larger by hand so that the energy carries across. This differs from the
   July pass's monumentalising of a pixel: here the copy is a second
   performance, not a blow-up.
4. **Cue-based structure (conduction).** A conductor gives discrete cues to
   improvising voices. Structure lives in when things change, not in a grid.
5. **The motif as a rule-set.** The tree rules (vertical, centred, narrowing
   from the midpoint) generate figures. The tree is a program, not a picture.
6. **Few lines, doubled, with blanks reserved first.** In the charcoal
   procedure, emptiness is decided and a line is retraced as a second
   decision.
7. **Figure flicker.** Limbs and faces surface for a moment and dissolve.
   This lines up with Ian's "less obvious sign-like shapes" and his dislike
   of "too similar to real".

**Avoid:**
- **Literal imitation.** No Oehlen-looking trees, poster grounds, spray
  textures or compositions. The procedures transfer and the looks don't.
- **Retro pixel nostalgia.** No staircases, dithering or bitmap blocks as
  the "computer" layer (the July pass's trap, still live).
- **Collage-as-texture.** A found layer pasted behind a drawing as wallpaper
  is the opposite of what the sheets do. If the line does not visibly take
  something from the found layer, or visibly refuse it, the found layer
  should not be there.
- **Fake mass.** A plotter cannot paste or smear, so covering has to be a
  cut (a gap). Charcoal smudge has to be a knot or bead in the mass regime,
  never hatching or fill.
- **Copying a source's forms.** Rule E of round 8 says corpora give
  behaviours, never forms. A found layer drawn as is (a river, a fly walk,
  someone's drawing) is a form, which is the open question for D1 below.

## c. Candidate directions for a deep build

Each uses the project's own material as the found layer, the way Oehlen uses
printed matter. Frames are chosen per direction (round-12 rule: no automatic
A4). Kit ids in backticks exist today (`kit.list()`).

### D1. Found ground, negotiated line (after the Krebber leaves and the 2009 collage-drawings)

- **Found layer.** Drawn first, in a thin coloured pen, from the project's
  own output: a measurement plate (`measure_atlas` / `measure_shadow`
  output), a kept Gesture Studies sheet, or a hand's own line from an
  earlier round.
- **New voice.** It draws over in black and switches between **borrow**
  and **ignore** as events, not as a field. In borrow mode it snaps onto a
  found contour, rides it for a stretch, and leaves at a tangent. In ignore
  mode it crosses without regard.
- **Build.** A new modulator that takes sheet-level found marks; two pens;
  `single` arranger.
- **Frame.** Two sheets side by side: the found plate alone, then the plate
  drawn over. This is the "drawing beside its own measurement" Ian loved.
- **Risk: collage-as-texture.** The found layer may just sit under the
  drawing. The success test is that someone can point to where the line
  left the found layer. There is also a policy question. Natural traces
  (rivers, fly walks, seismograms) drawn as is would break rule E, so they
  need Ian's say-so. The default is the project's own outputs only.

### D2. Cover cuts (after the collaged and white-smeared passages)

- **Procedure.**
  1. Draw layer 1.
  2. Place one to three large cover shapes that **cut** everything beneath
     them, leaving gaps. The shapes are round, bead-like or torn outlines;
     a fabric-stain outline can serve as a torn-paper edge.
  3. Draw layer 2 over the covered area as if nothing were there.
- **Reading.** The cover shows only through what is missing and what
  carries on across it.
- **Build.** A cover modulator that splits marks by polygon. The app's
  occlusion already does the same at layer level.
- **Risk: gaps read as breakage.** Covers can leave stubs and stray marks,
  which Ian dislikes, especially at edges. Covers must be few and large,
  sit away from the margin, and always have a later line crossing them.
  Stubs under a minimum length are removed.

### D3. Conduction: small source, enlargement by another hand

- **Procedure.** A small, dense source drawing (about 60 × 45 mm, a fast
  voice) is re-performed about ×5 by a different hand. The source geometry
  acts only as a **cue track**: turn events, stops, repeats. The second
  hand's own tempo, tremor and drift fill each segment between cues. This
  answers Ian's round-11 wish to use the kinematics to drive other things.
- **Variant.** A conductor track issues discrete cues to a multi-pen chorus,
  building on `handover`, `call_response` and `dev_chorus`.
- **Frame.** Wide, with the source small in a corner and the enlargement
  filling the rest.
- **Risk: plain scaling.** The enlargement may come out as the same shape,
  only bigger, so nothing is re-performed. Or the cue grammar may show as
  systematic structure, which Ian accepts only as an accessory.

### D4. The tree program (after the 1988 rules)

- **Procedure.** A program arranger applies Oehlen-style rules to any
  existing voice:
  - a centred vertical spine;
  - pen weight and step size narrowing with distance from the midpoint,
    across several pens (for example 0.8 → 0.5 → 0.3 → 0.1 mm);
  - branch events fired by the hand's own kinematics (stillness, turn
    bursts), not by a grammar.
- **Fixed in advance.** Only the figure, the ground and the narrowing rule,
  as Oehlen fixes only figure and ground colours.
- **Frame.** Tall portrait. This is the direction closest to Ian's original
  "Oehlen's trees" remark.
- **Risk: the botanical cliché.** It can turn into an L-system tree that
  reads as an illustration. The test: if a viewer's first word is "tree",
  the rules are too literal.

### D5. Double and blank (after the 300 × 200 cm charcoals)

- **Procedure.** Three to six body-scale bold gestures; Ian called bold
  single gestures "very promising".
  1. Reserve the blank zones first.
  2. Draw the gestures.
  3. Retrace each line once with a second pass that drifts away and rejoins
     irregularly.
  4. Render the smudge as a few round mass knots on the line, as beads,
     never capsules or fill.
- **Frame.** Deliberately 3:2 portrait (200 × 300 mm, or two stacked
  sheets) to keep the charcoals' proportion at plotter scale.
- **Risk: doubling becomes parallel twin lines.** An offset copy reads as
  hatching. The second pass must diverge and converge at irregular
  intervals and never run parallel for long. The Hyperallergic note on line
  versus size also applies: a thin pen on a big frame can look weightless.

**Recommendation.** **D1 + D2 together** is the most Oehlen-on-paper
build: a found plate, a negotiating line, a cover cut, then a line over the
cover. Second choice is **D3**, which uses the kinematics as Ian asked in
round 11. D4 is the most direct answer to his early "trees" remark, but
also the riskiest for literalism.
