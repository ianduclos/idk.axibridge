# cimpro — the composer as improviser (round 14, thread b2, 2026-10-18)

**Outcome.** One arranger, `cimpro`, looks at the sheet so far and decides the
next move, every time it runs. Twelve consecutive seeds (14001–14012, not picked)
give twelve compositions that do not share a layout. They differ in where the
core sits, whether there are one or two cores, how dense the sheet is (4 to 40
moves; coverage 0.06 to 0.60), which register leads, and whether a heavy mark
comes in at all. The strongest sheets (14012, 14006, 14007) have two pulls that
do not settle, large blanks, and contrast of weight and scale inside the sheet.
The weak ones are the sparse extremes (14008, 14010) and a few that end up as
scattered small knots.

## What it does

Each move is perceive → decide → act.

- **Perceive.** The sheet so far is binned on a 4 mm grid (ink length × pen
  weight per cell), then blurred at a local scale and at core scale. From that
  it reads:
  - coverage;
  - the core: where it is, its radius, its share of the ink, and its edge cells;
  - a competing second density;
  - the centroid's offset from the middle;
  - the largest blank disk;
  - which scales have been played (tiny, mid, large);
  - line ends left in the air (calls);
  - covers nobody has crossed yet.

  One look takes 0–15 ms (logged per move as `ms.look`).
- **Decide.** A weighted random draw over the moves the state allows. The
  weights are the sheet's temperament times what the state invites.
  - The temperament is drawn once per sheet: the density appetite
    (log-uniform 0.05–0.62, so the extremes do occur), gamma-distributed verb
    weights, so some sheets never intrude or cover, a dominant player, a core
    form and a vocabulary of three hands.
  - The draw is never an argmax and never a score that gets optimised. The
    same state gives different moves under different draws (tested).
  - Habituation: a verb loses 30 % of its weight for each time it was played
    in the last four moves.
- **Act.** The moves are seed, thicken, found, traverse, intrude,
  counterweight, cover, cross, answer, trail, ladder, sign, reserve, guest and
  stop. Each is placed from what the look found. The players are the
  techniques that work:
  - found ground: the Amazon zooming along itself (thread c1's `river_zoom`,
    asymmetric schedules only), the braided river, oxbow loops, the
    seismograph chart with its lettering dropped;
  - contagion catches: made lines walk the sheet and catch the river, the
    chart and the last two hands (`contagion_walk` + found_play's `hand_gen`);
  - handover specimens: waves, the Cohen key, the bowed key, the coiled key;
  - accrue's 3966 free hand, exact;
  - belt and gmorph morphing lines, and passage;
  - limb signs (`revisit_glyph`, glyphs in the heavy pen);
  - "sweep": a hand at under two reference lengths laid out large, for the
    slow long arcs.
- Cores come in five forms (hands, coil, specimens, beads, found), so a core is
  not always the same scribble ball. Lines that leave the inner frame are cut
  ragged and pulled back 3–25 mm, so they never draw the sheet's edge.

## The three principles, and where each lands

- **T7 Unresolved, yet held.** "The eye does not settle. There are two
  competing densities, or an off-centre core with a counterweight somewhere
  else. Lines stop in the air."
  - `settled` is high when the eye can rest: a centred mass, one lone core
    with no other pull (0.75), or an even spread.
  - It falls with a competing density (×0.35), a second core (×0.7) or a
    heavy mark (×0.6).
  - A high `settled` raises the weights of intrude and counterweight. The
    stop weight scales with (1.6 − settled), so an unresolved sheet stops more
    readily.
  - Seen in the strip (14003): after the first core, `settled` is 0.75
    ("one core holds 99 %"). The next move is a counterweight, and after the
    traverse `settled` drops to 0.
  - In 14006 the counterweight came out bigger than the core and became the
    second core. The sheet holds two competing densities and a blank lower half.
  - Answers and trails leave ends in the air on purpose.
- **T3 The heavy intruder.** Heavy marks "rarely sit at the densest point.
  They sit at the edge of the core and cut across it, like something that came
  in from outside."
  - `intrude` takes the core's edge cells from the look. It prefers the edge
    cells facing the largest blank (the outside), then aims 25–75° off the
    inward direction, so the mark crosses the edge instead of pointing at the
    peak.
  - The shapes are a bar, a bent bar, two dashes, or a heavy round knot. Each
    is one contiguous line of overlapping passes at pen 1.6–2.8 mm.
  - A test checks that the heavy mark spans the core's radius: part of it
    inside, part outside.
  - Six of the twelve sheets got one or more; the rest never did, and that
    count is set by the temperament.
- **T1 Core against blank.** The density dial is per sheet and has extremes:
  4 moves at appetite 0.06 (14010), 40 moves at 0.60 (14012). A hungry dense
  sheet plays more elements per move.
  - `reserve` decides the blank before it fills: either a side of the empty
    sheet or the largest blank at the time.
  - Dense moves and found pastes keep out of the reserve. Traverses and
    trails may cross it.

## Frame

The frame is 300 × 200 mm for all twelve: the AxiDraw's full width at 3:2. It is
one format on purpose. The note says a series keeps its format and varies its
decisions, so the sheets here differ only by what the improviser decided.

## Duds and what they taught

- **v1: the squeezed traverse.** At first a hand laid across the sheet was
  squeezed sideways to fit a band. It read as a smeared, stretched hand, a
  grammar that was no longer whole. Now the scale is only uniform, and a line
  that would be too wide ends short, in the air.
- **v2: every core a scribble ball.** Six of nine sheets had the same
  multi-hand knot as their core. That was a template one level down. Hence
  the five core forms and the per-sheet form weights.
- **Rotated bands fitted after rotation.** Specimen bands came out as 5 mm
  hairy specks: a fit bug, now fixed (`fit_rot`). Tiny scattered specimens
  and beads still read as confetti in places (14004, 14011).
- **Thicken loops.** Without habituation, one sheet thickened 25 times in a
  row.

## Honest read against the judging

- **Different every time.** Yes, on the evidence of twelve unpicked seeds:
  openings, core positions, numbers of cores, density and registers all change.
  What repeats is the vocabulary, which the note says a series should keep: the
  zooming river, bead knots, hairline tangles, heavy bars.
- **Dynamics.** The best sheets have real contrast inside one sheet:
  - scale: a 150 mm bold sweep against 10 mm bead knots;
  - speed: the river's tight zigzag against slow loops;
  - weight: hairlines against 2.5 mm bars;
  - density: a crowded corner against an empty half.

  14012 is the densest and the most alive.
- **Weaknesses.**
  - Mid-density sheets can look like a scatter of small events (14004, 14011).
  - The heavy dashes are sometimes too small to read as an intruder.
  - Contagion catches are hard to see at this density. The relation between
    regimes is there in the logs but often not on the paper.
  - `settled` reaches 0 early and stays there, so from mid-run T7 acts mainly
    through the stop rule.
- **Would Ian keep it?** I think 14012 and 14006 have a chance as drawings, and
  the procedure has a better one: it is the intuition session repeatable, with
  a log that says why each move happened. He may find the sparse sheets thin
  and the bead scatter confetti-like.
