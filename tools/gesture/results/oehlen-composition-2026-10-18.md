# How Oehlen builds a composition — an abstract note (round 14 (a), 2026-10-18)

Written by the lead before the round-14 composition briefs, from the 29 sheets in
`.local/gesture/corpus/artists/albert_oehlen/` and research A1–A7
(`oehlen-research-2026-10-17.md`). Round 13's `rules` thread read rules off the
sheets literally (R1–R5). This note answers a different question: how a whole
sheet is built. It is meant to be used "vaguely and abstractly" (Ian), as
tensions to hold and moves to make, **not rules to implement**. Numbers in
brackets are filename prefixes; the annotated reference sheet is local only
(`.local/gesture/out/round14-refs/lead/composition-refs.jpg`).

## Seven tensions

**T1. Core against blank.** Most sheets have one dense core, often off-centre
(high, or right of the middle). Density falls away from it, and a few lines run
out into reserved blank and reach an edge (05, 12, 13, 14, 27). The blank is
decided, not left over: whole lower thirds stay nearly empty (12, 14). But the
ratio swings wildly between sheets of one body of work: all-over density (17,
18, 19) on one side, a few lines on a huge empty sheet (20–24, 26) on the
other. Density is a dial set per sheet, and the dial has extremes.

**T2. A ladder of scales, held at once.** One sheet carries at least three
scales: tiny periodic matter (staff rows, a coil spring, dotted runs, spirals),
a middle hand that loops and knots, and long arcs that cross the whole sheet
(13). The long traverse is what ties the strata together. It passes through
the core and the blank alike, and it belongs to neither.

**T3. The heavy intruder.** A few heavy marks set against many fine ones: a
thick diagonal bar (15), a bent L-shaped bar (16), a clef-like black sign (13),
two black dashes (14), one black knot (05). They are often straight or
geometric where everything else curves. They rarely sit at the densest point.
They sit at the edge of the core and cut across it, like something that came
in from outside. The weight contrast is also a contrast of regime.

**T4. Container and spill.** Many sheets have a frame inside the frame: a drawn
rectangle (03, 04, 05), a pasted sheet (12, 13, 17), a grid block (14, 15, 16).
The content both respects it and escapes it. Lines start inside, cross its
edge, and finish in the margin. The container gives the sheet a structure that
the drawing then refuses to obey completely.

**T5. Order with reversals.** The broad order is found matter first, then
structure (grid, stepped machine line, chart), then the free hand, then the
heavy accents. But at least one late act cuts back into what is already there:
a pasted patch or white cover (17, 28, 08), a smear, an erasure. Then a line
crosses the cover as if it were not there (17). The order is a habit, not a law.

**T6. Registers contaminate each other.** Machine vocabulary (grid, ruled rows,
stepped pixel line, numbered chart) sits beside free hand vocabulary (tendril,
loop, scribble). Somewhere each borrows the other's behaviour: a hand line goes
stepped (17), a free contour turns into an even banded field (15, the stacked
swirl), a ruled row bends. The borrowing is local and brief, not a style.

**T7. Unresolved, yet held.** The eye does not settle. There are two competing
densities, or an off-centre core with a counterweight somewhere else. Lines stop
in the air. Motifs flicker toward figure (a face, a limb) and fall back. What
holds it together anyway: the long traverse (T2), the container (T4), and a
small set of registers repeated across the sheet, so that everything
looks related without lining up.

## What a series keeps and what it varies

Two sheets of one body of work (17 and 18; 20, 21, 22; 25 and 26) keep:
- the vocabulary: the same registers and media;
- the format and the container habit;
- roughly the same scale ladder.

They vary:
- where the core sits, and whether there are one or two cores;
- the density dial, sometimes to its extreme;
- which register dominates (in 17 the stepped machine line; in 18 the smear and
  the pasted panel);
- whether the heavy intruder appears at all;
- which side the lines leave by.

So "different every time" does not mean a different vocabulary each time. It
means the same vocabulary, distributed and weighted differently, with the
composition's decisions (core, blank, dominant register, intruder, exits)
re-made per sheet.

## Moves (verbs a composer could use)

reserve (a blank) · seed (a core) · traverse (cross everything once) · intrude
(heavy, foreign, at the core's edge) · contain · spill (out of the container) ·
cover (cut back into earlier layers) · cross the cover · contaminate (borrow
another register's behaviour briefly) · trail off (into blank, to an edge) ·
stop in the air · counterweight (a second, smaller density elsewhere) · repeat
(a register elsewhere, so it rhymes).

## What not to take

His looks (pixel staircases, spray, posters, trees). Any single tension as a
fixed rule: round 13 showed that five rules always on become a style. The
tensions above are dials whose settings should themselves vary per sheet,
including to their extremes.
