# Restyle as translation: structure from A, hand from B (2026-10-16)

Stage 6, operation 3 of `docs/IDEAS-gesture-bank.md`, built from grammar v3's
minimal `restyle`. Code: `gesture/restyle.py` (uses the FROZEN
`grammar_v3_frozen`), `experiments/restyle_study.py`, `tests/test_restyle.py`.
Raw output: `.local/gesture/out/restyle/` (`grid.md`, `grid.json`,
`grid_rows.jsonl`, `separability.json`, all sheets).
Committed sheets: `restyle-synthetic.jpg`, `restyle-topology.jpg`,
`restyle-slider.jpg`, plus the plot `restyle-tradeoff.jpg`.

**Verdict.**

- **Visually, it works as translation at moderate amounts.** At amount 0.5 every source is plainly
  itself: a circle, a spiral, the Matisse torso, the Oehlen tree. Each is redrawn
  with hand-drawn kinks, wobble, hooks and corners, and nothing reads as hatching.
- **The measurements cannot confirm *which* hand.** The panel-based hand classifier
  reads every output at chance, and it cannot reliably name the grammars' own
  drawings either. By eye, the three hands differ only weakly and inconsistently at
  0.5; the differences are clearer at 0.8.
- **The recommended configuration is `gauss` + `trace`** (with `chain` as the cheap
  alternative). It wins on looks and on the fidelity/cost balance. It does not win on a
  hand metric, because none of the variants does.

## What was built

1. **One representation.** Every stroke is handled as its turning function θ(s): a
   start point plus uniform arc-length samples (step R/2000). The output is
   `θ_out = θ_structure + r_hand + δθ_fix`.
2. **Decompositions** (`method`). These are the structure half of question 1:
   1. `gauss`: the curvature-scale-space split. κ(s) is Gaussian-smoothed at σ_s, and
      positions are pinned every 2 σ_s.
   2. `clothoid`: θ comes from `clothoid.fit_stroke` at σ_s, which keeps corners sharp.
      Positions are pinned as in `gauss` and also at every fitted corner. If the fit
      fails, it falls back to `gauss`.
   3. `anchors`: only the curvature extrema of the σ_s view are kept. These are
      Attneave points, each with position and tangent, plus fill-in anchors at most
      4 σ_s apart. Between anchors the base is a linear θ, and the hand gets a band
      1.5 × wider.
3. **Re-synthesis** (`source`). This is the hand half of question 2:
   1. `chain`: a grammar stroke of the same length, its θ high-passed at the residual
      scale. This is the unconditional residual.
   2. `transplant`: real residual windows taken from the grammar's training strokes
      (both directions). Windows are matched by their coarse-κ profile, chosen at
      random from the 6 nearest, and overlap-added with a Hann window. This is
      Kalnins' "base path + learned residual" method.
   3. `trace` (**new**): the hand redraws the structure token by token. The chain
      proposes k continuations from its own context. The one kept is the proposal
      whose points, plus a look-ahead ray, stay closest to the structure path at the
      matching arc length. Nothing is inserted or bent, so every kept segment and
      corner is one the chain proposed. k = 16 → 2 as amount goes 0 → 1, so selection
      pressure is the fidelity knob.
4. **Fidelity constraint** (`fit_pins`). This is the smallest low-band correction δθ
   that puts the line within 0.02 σ_s of the pins. It runs in two steps:
   1. A robust start: each pin interval gets a chord rotation plus a step stretch,
      spread piecewise-linearly along the line. Without this start, Gauss–Newton
      failed by up to 600 px on R ≈ 5000.
   2. Damped Gauss–Newton on Gaussian bumps.

   Closed strokes close exactly: the residual and the correction basis are periodic.
   The step is stretched by 1/⟨cos r⟩ so that wiggle does not pull the pins apart.
5. **Slider.** σ_s = 0.12 · amount^1.5 · R. At 0 the source is returned unchanged. At 1
   the structure keeps only about 4 pins per circle-sized form.

## Method comparison

Grid: 10 sources × 3 decompositions × 3 re-syntheses × 5 amounts × 3 hands = 1,350
restyles, seed 1, in about 9.5 min on 10 processes. Sources:

- primitives: circle, ellipse, spiral
- `scene_matisse_like`
- an Oehlen-ish recursive tree (32 strokes)
- 12 nested wobbly contours (the dense multi-line case)
- 2 real long-stroke drawings each from Matisse, Hokusai and Ingres

"target hand vs the others" from the classifier below, macro-averaged; chance is 0.5.

Reading:

1. **Decomposition.**
   - `gauss` and `clothoid` are nearly interchangeable on smooth sources: the clothoid
     fit of a circle is an arc. `clothoid` is 10–20 % closer in d because it keeps
     corners and pins them, and it costs a fit per stroke.
   - `anchors` gives the hand the most room. Its d is about 1.5–2× higher at the same
     amount, and it loses fine structure first: at 0.7 the spiral collapsed into a loop
     (`sheet-methods.jpg`, bottom right). Attneave's principle holds for silhouettes
     but is too sparse for spirals and nested contours, where the information sits
     *between* the extrema.
   - **Pick: `gauss`** as the default, with `clothoid` when the source has real corners.
2. **Re-synthesis.**
   - `transplant` is the weakest. The training "strokes" are clothoid token lists, and
     90 %+ of them are 1–2 smooth segments. High-passing them leaves almost nothing, so
     transplant swaps in small local kinks and stays closest to the source. It is also
     the slowest: 3–21 s per drawing, building window pools.
   - `chain` gives smooth, bulgy wobble. Its residual is the hand's segment-to-segment
     κ changes, so the line reads as "a wobbly copy" more than as a hand.
   - `trace` gives the most *drawn* result. The line is made of the hand's own segments
     and corners, so the hand's segment lengths and corner habits show. It inherits
     v3's known corner excess: `cl_corner_rate` is 2.7–3.8 against 1.3–1.6 real.
   - **Pick: `trace`**, with `chain` as the 10× cheaper fallback.
3. **Pins hold.** The worst pin error over the whole grid, after the robust start, was
   ≤ 6 px on R ≈ 5000. Closed forms close.

## The slider (question 3)

Structure distance rises monotonically with amount for every variant in the median.
gauss/trace: 0 → .0006 → .0026 → .0057 → .0111 → .0201 (×R) for amount 0, .2, .4, .6,
.8, 1. The test suite checks this on a fixed example for chain and trace.

The perceptual zones (`restyle-slider.jpg`):

- **0.2:** texture only, as tiny ticks and hooks.
- **0.4–0.6:** "this shape, hand-drawn". The circle becomes a hand circle and the
  spiral a hand spiral.
- **0.8:** the hand starts to own mid-scale shape. The circle becomes a pentagon or
  a bulge, and the spiral starts to tangle.
- **1.0:** the hand dominates. Hokusai's circle becomes curls and loops. Structure
  survives only as rough placement.

The useful range is about 0.4–0.8.

## Hand fidelity (question 4): an honest negative

1. **Classifier.** Logistic regression on 51 texture features (k_, ext_, infl_, turn_,
   cl_; not len_/rel_, which the structure dictates). It uses the standard
   preprocessing: σ ≥ 7 w, then medium projection. It was trained on the 92 real
   long-stroke drawings.
   - 5-fold CV accuracy on the reals is **0.64** (chance 0.33). Width-free texture is
     a weak fingerprint.
   - Outputs are measured at one common line width (w/R = median of all hands), so
     σ does not leak the target hand.
2. **Ceiling.** The grammars' *own* drawings (`generate_drawing`, 8 per hand) score
   AUC 0.62 (Matisse) / 0.58 (Hokusai) / 0.90 (Ingres) and accuracy 0.50. The
   instrument cannot reliably name even the pure hands. (In `grid.md` the ceiling
   line prints these in classifier order, H / I / M.)
3. **Restyles.** Every cell sits at chance: macro AUC 0.41–0.58, cross-artist
   accuracy 0.17–0.50 at n = 30 per cell. The ±0.1 swings are noise, and none is
   monotone in amount.
4. **Separability check.** Panel features of the output minus those of its source,
   then a leave-one-source-out classifier of target hand. It is also at chance:
   0.13–0.60 against 0.33 (`separability.json`). With 30 outputs and 51 features,
   this cannot detect a modest hand signal.
5. **Key features against the real long strokes** (gauss/trace, amount 0.6, M / H / I;
   real values in brackets):

   | # | feature | gauss/trace @0.6, M / H / I | real, M / H / I |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | K1 | `cl_corner_rate` | 2.7 / 3.8 / 3.0 | 1.5 / 1.3 / 1.6 |
   | K2 | `k_q50` | 7.6 / 9.7 / 8.0 | 5.5 / 8.1 / 6.3 |
   | K3 | `turn_tcorr_10` | .57 / .57 / .48 | .71 / .46 / .73 |

   - The *ordering* of k_q50 follows the real hands: Hokusai is the most curved.
   - Corners are about 2× real in every hand. This is v3's texture gap, carried over.
   - Tangent correlation is pulled toward the source's.

So restyle reproduces the grammar, including the grammar's flaws. The grammar is the
bottleneck for "reads as *that* hand", not the translation layer.

## The sheets, honestly (question 6)

1. **`restyle-synthetic.jpg`** (gauss/trace; amounts 0.5 and 0.8 per hand).
   - Primitives at 0.5 read as "a circle/ellipse/spiral drawn by hand" in all three
     hands.
   - At 0.8 the hands separate a little:
     - Matisse makes the circle a soft pentagon.
     - Hokusai makes squarish turns and curls in the spiral.
     - Ingres makes long straights with sharp angles, and its ellipse goes polygonal.
   - The spiral loses itself at 0.8 for Matisse and Ingres.
   - The tree and the dense contours keep their structure and gain kinks and
     side-hooks. In the dense case at 0.8 the rings start to cross and tangle, which
     reads as nervousness rather than as a style.
   - Overall: structure + "hand-drawn", with a weak hand identity.
2. **`restyle-topology.jpg`.** Two skeletons from the other agent's
   `topology.generate_structure`: `oehlen_tree` (46 strokes) and `learned`
   (27 strokes).
   - This is the closest to Ian's ask. The Oehlen tree is unmistakably the same tree
     in every column, and the line character changes:
     - Matisse 0.8 is jagged-curvy.
     - Hokusai 0.8 adds loops and soft blobs.
     - Ingres 0.8 is angular, with right-angle notches.
   - Lines stay contiguous and there is no hatching. The awkwardness is real and
     sometimes good, for example Hokusai's loop dropped into a branch.
3. **`restyle-slider.jpg`.** A real Matisse sheet of short fragments, and the
   primitives, each at 0–1. It is monotone to the eye.
   - The real Matisse fragments are too short to carry a hand. Their changes read as
     noise at every amount.
   - The primitives show the zones listed under "The slider".
4. **Real-to-real translation** (`sheet-real-gauss-trace.jpg`, not committed).
   - Matisse KKS1971-302 (the torso) translated into Hokusai or Ingres is the
     best real case: the same figure with more broken, hooked contours.
   - The Hokusai and Ingres sources mostly look "more scribbled" in any hand.
5. **Method sheet** (`sheet-methods.jpg`, not committed). The three re-syntheses look
   genuinely different:
   - chain is smooth and bulgy;
   - transplant is nearly the source, with local kinks;
   - trace is angular and drawn.

   With anchors, trace breaks the spiral.

## As an axibridge effect later

1. **Params** (all bounded):
   - `hand`: an asset holding the grammar JSON;
   - `amount` [0, 1];
   - `method` gauss | clothoid;
   - `resynth` trace | chain;
   - `seed`;
   - `hand_gain` [0, 2];
   - `tol` [0.005, 0.1] σ_s.

   Lengths are in R of the layer's geometry, so it works in paper space as effects
   must.
2. **Contract.** The effect is pure and deterministic, keeps `closed` and keeps stroke
   count/order. `Path.filled` is passed through unchanged; restyled fills may
   self-intersect, which needs a shapely `make_valid` before occlusion.
3. **Cost.**
   - trace: about 0.3–1.8 s per drawing of about 20 strokes (median 1 s at 0.6;
     slower at low amount, where k = 16 proposals per token).
   - chain: about 0.1 s.
   - The memo key is (geometry hash, params), so `gencache` makes it free on re-render.
   - Grammar JSON is 1–3 MB and loads in about 0.05 s.
   - Pure numpy/scipy, so it runs on the Pi; `clothoid` adds scipy only.
4. **Not ready to ship:**
   - The grammar's corner excess goes straight into the plot.
   - Hand identity is not measurable yet.
   - The `frozen` grammar import must be re-pointed when v4 lands.

## Weaknesses and next steps

1. **The hand classifier is too weak to judge a restyle.** Its accuracy is 0.64 on
   reals and it fails on the grammar's own output. Options:
   - a pairwise "which of these two is hand H" test on matched structure, which needs
     far less data than absolute attribution;
   - more seeds per cell (n = 30 is tiny);
   - Ian's eye.
2. **Corner excess comes from the grammar, not from restyle.** The v4 fixes (rounded
   corners, splice κ-penalty) will lift restyle for free. Trace also adds selection
   bias toward turning tokens near the path, the same mechanism v3 found.
3. **Transplant needs real strokes, not clothoid tokens.** Its pool should be the
   extracted long-stroke polylines smoothed at 2 w, which carry within-stroke texture
   that the token fits throw away. Today it is mostly a no-op.
4. **The structure scale is absolute** (σ_s in R). Fine sources lose themselves before
   coarse ones: the spiral is gone at 0.8 while the tree survives. A per-stroke scale
   (σ_s relative to the stroke's own curvature-scale-space) would make the slider mean
   the same thing on every source.
5. **Short real fragments carry no hand.** Restyle needs contiguous structure. The
   topology skeletons are the right input; scans of broken contour are not.
6. **Inter-stroke relations are inherited from the source.** T-junctions can come
   apart by up to the pin tolerance, and branch tips on the tree drift off their hosts
   by ≲ 0.02 σ_s. A junction pin, pinning a branch's start to its host's *restyled*
   line, would keep trees welded.
7. **Single seed.** The grid is one seed per cell. The visual reading above rests on
   seed 1.
