## Per thread


### measg1 (Codex: scale_ruler, density_isoline, heading_rose) — committed
- Clean, exact, thin. The isoline mostly reads as a halo offset around the line (blur small) with small inner pockets where lines crowd: elegant, and it does read the drawing's crowding. Rose beside: a true axial heading reading, star-like; good as a "beside" panel. Ruler: correct, the "198.01 MM" span label is the nice touch (it measures the drawing, not the paper).
- Risk: the halo isoline on a dense sheet could become a second drawing; try 2 levels max over dense sheets.

### measg2 (Codex: arc_ticks, survey_marks, misfit_grid) — committed
- misfit_grid is the find: a quiet grid bent by where the ink is; reads as a field, not graph paper, as long as the pitch stays wide. Under, at low strength.
- arc_ticks: the ticks are fine, the cumulative numbers scatter over the sheet and collide ("24040" at a crossing). Fewer numbers (every 400 mm) or numbers only at ends.
- survey_marks: corner coordinates are a nice dry touch; corner crosshairs clip at the paper edge (agent noted) → only usable with a margin.
- Together (all three over) it is too much apparatus; one or two devices per drawing.

### C arch (committed)
- Population (8 consecutive) sits in the middle density band Ian asked for, lighter and calmer than round 14; colour carries it (ccolour base). Nothing reads as a jumbled mess; 15005 and 15004 risk "boring" (mostly empty with a small knot).
- Strongest moves: the danced traverse (15006: blue line edge to edge with beads at pauses, it gives direction) and the bold swell intruder (15002: two round black heads at the core's edge, the only weight; a single big circle loop). Ian was cool on thick lines ("not the biggest fan … but they work here"), so the swell is a risk: it works only as the single weight.
- Limb flicker: the 15005 "K" reads as a letter → legibility flag. Passer (accrue 3966 through the container) too quiet to see.
- Picks: 15002, 15006; 15003 (orange mono) as the most cohesive.

### A listen (committed)
- The most elegant line work of any composer so far: thin, open, middle density, nothing jumbled. Crossings break (weave/yield), so overlaps are aware without being obvious; that answers Ian's river note at sheet scale.
- 15102 is the pick: a void in the middle with a frayed zigzag edge that some lines stop at and the big blue loops pass by, the heavy blue exaggerated loop on the right answering a small black one. Closest to 4507's trick without copying it.
- Risk: the population shares one texture (thin gestural lines, angular hooks), so "different every time" holds for frame/palette/layout but not for line character. It may read light ("a little thin", agent agrees), on the edge of "boring" for 15109/15112.
- About half the relations can be named on paper (imitate, meander translate, echo, exaggerate, void). Legibility flags: 15110 echo reads "7"; S/Z strokes in 15104/15106.

### B lcorp (committed)
- Specimen (river + 44 hands zoomed, constant width, free course): a real resource and an honest result (33/44 yield by measure). But by eye most yields share one look: fine fuzz + one or two big bends. The river, dance_levitation, dancer_cristel, trail_tracks and lichtenberg are the distinct ones.
- Relay (matisse > lichtenberg > dancer_cristel > trail_slime, one zooming line, a pen per hand): my pick. Elegant, constant width, the hands are recognisable and the zoom carries between them; handovers hide in the far twists. It is the zoom tool used as composition.
- Style transfer: the surprise is "a Hokusai line in the river's bends": Hokusai's figure outlined in tiny meanders, both sources recognisable. The river in Hokusai's bends reads mostly as river with spikes (weak).
- Aware overlaps (depth / sympathy): sympathy is subtle as Ian asked; check the close-up.
- Lens: direction but not a keeper alone (agent agrees).
