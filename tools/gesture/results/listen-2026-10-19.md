# Round 15 thread A `listen`: layers that listen to each other (2026-10-19)

**Outcome.** One composer, `listen`, builds each sheet as a short history. One or two speakers draw.
Then each later layer takes an earlier layer's line as input and does something with one of its
gestures. There are eight relation kinds plus a rare void. Every crossing between layers is woven or
yielded, never bare. Every sheet records its full score: every layer, every relation with its kind,
src, dst, `at` and `s`, and the decisions. Across 40 seeds the sheets sit at 3.7–9.7 mm of ink per cm²
(mean 6.8), with 6–10 layers. The void fires on 15% of them. Some relations can be pointed at on paper:
imitate, translate (meander), echo, exaggerate when it is bold, and the void. Others mostly read as
"more lines": refuse, calm, nest and bind.

## The relation vocabulary (score `kind`)

Spliced into the listener's own line. The listener's hand runs, takes up the other layer's gesture for
a stretch, then resumes; heading stays continuous. The whole line is turned and placed so that the
borrowed stretch sits beside the gesture it heard.

- **imitate.** The gesture, 3–5 mm beside its source, for 55–120 mm. On paper, two lines run in unison
  like a doubled stroke, then part.
- **exaggerate.** The gesture's sideways excursions are multiplied by 1.7–2.5, the whole is scaled by
  1.2–1.5, and it is centred on the original. On paper, a bigger, wilder copy surrounds the original.
  It is the one place a bold pen is allowed when no speaker already holds it.
- **translate.** The same move in another line style:
  - `meander`: the gesture's course drawn as a river meander;
  - `hand`: the course carrying the listener's own small turns;
  - `ruled`: a few straight chords.

  On paper, the meander reads clearly. The other two read less clearly.
- **refuse.** The listener's own line is placed across the source. Wherever it would come within
  2.5–5 mm of the source, it walks the source's halo instead. On paper, a line bends around another at
  a constant distance. One relation is recorded per detour.

Separate small layers, later in the drawing order:

- **echo.** The gesture again, 35–90 mm away, at the fringe of the drawing, at 0.75–1.0 scale. It can
  repeat once or twice, decaying. In accent mode it is drawn in the accent's 400 pen. On paper, the
  same small shape appears again, quieter, nearby. It reads best when it repeats.
- **calm** (own). Exaggerate's opposite. The gesture's turns are flattened to a quiet arc whose two
  ends land on the gesture's own ends. On paper, it is a bowstring under a wiggle. It is too quiet to
  be seen at this density.
- **nest** (own). A small copy of a line's own gesture, set inside a crook of that same line (an
  enclosed space 7–18 mm across). On paper, a small sign sits in the bay of a curve. It is too small
  to name.
- **bind** (own). A ligature from the end of one layer into the start of another, tangent at both
  ends. In pair mode its pen hands over halfway. On paper, two layers become one gesture. It is
  invisible unless the colours differ.

The void and the overlaps:

- **mask / ignore.** A torn void at an ambiguous point: where two layers overlap most, near the
  centre. It is placed late in the drawing order:
  - layers drawn before it are cut by it (`mask`);
  - layers drawn after it cross it (`ignore`);
  - with a 15% exception either way;
  - if nothing would cross, the last layer crosses; if nothing would be cut, the first is cut.

  Its edge is drawn only in part. Default `mask_p` is 0.25, so it stays one move among many.
- **weave / yield.** Every crossing between two different layers is aware. In `weave`, the two lines
  alternate which passes under, with a gap of pen/2 + 0.5 mm. In `yield`, the later line always gives
  way, with a gap of pen/2 + 1.0–1.6 mm. One relation is recorded per pair of layers, at the centre of
  their crossings. A line's crossings with itself are left alone.

**Colour carries the listening.** Each sheet picks one of three colour modes:

- `accent` (55%): one pen for every voice, and a second pen only where a line listens (the borrowed
  stretch, the detour, the echo).
- `pair` (30%): two speakers in two pens; a listener is in the other camp's pen and borrows in its
  source's pen.
- `mono` (15%): relations by shape alone.

Weight: at most one bold voice per sheet (0.6–1.0 mm), usually the first speaker, otherwise the
exaggeration. Everything else is 0.3–0.45 mm.

**Found matter.** `river_zoom` (free course, push schedule, constant 0.3 mm), with probability 0.3, in
a 400-level pen. It is never the κ chart.

## Duds and what was cut

- **The first build was a jumbled mess.** It had 10–14 thin layers of equal weight, piled on the
  speaker's centre, in four light pens. Ian's "jumbled mess" exactly. The fixes:
  - a candidate search that places each listener's own stretches on empty paper, with the sheet's
    flow;
  - gestures chosen where other layers are not already talking;
  - a cap per relation kind;
  - one bold voice;
  - accent colour.
- **The `stepped` translate was cut.** It made staircase polygons, which is the retired pixel
  staircase.
- **`meander69` was dropped from the hand pool.** Its closed polygons read as paint-program doodles.
- **Exaggerate by heading gain was replaced.** It curled gestures into spirals and made them smaller.
  The replacement amplifies in the chord frame.
- **Lone echoes in the blank read as stray marks.** Echoes now land only at the drawing's fringe, or
  not at all. One still reads as a "7": 15110 in the population, at the left. That is a legibility
  flag.

## Honest read against the bar

- **Middle density: yes, maybe a little thin.** By ink length the sheets carry about half of 4507's or
  14007's (6.8 against 15–18 mm/cm²). They look middle because the lines are long, spread and mostly
  hairline. Nothing approaches the dense end. 15109 is near the sparse end.
- **Different every time, never a mess: mostly.** No sheet is a central tangle any more. 15101 and
  15103 come closest to "too busy". The population still leans on one figure: a hand-drawn speaker
  with listeners fanned along the flow.
- **Can you point and name it?** Yes for:
  - imitate (a doubled run in another pen);
  - the meander translate;
  - echo (when it repeats);
  - a bold exaggeration (15112);
  - the void (15102, 15111).

  No, not without the annotation, for refuse, calm, nest and bind. They need to be stronger (a wider
  halo, calm in a contrasting pen, nest at 2–3× its size) or rarer.
- **The trick for the eye:** the void works in 15102. It is 15% of sheets, so it is not the style.
  The weave gaps are the quieter trick. They are visible up close and invisible at contact-sheet size.
  That may be right ("shouldn't be obvious").
- **Would Ian keep it?** Probably as a method: the score and the spliced relations. 15102 and 15112
  are the candidates as drawings. Line elegance is still the weak part. The hands are lovely alone
  (the hand probe), but their listeners are hairline copies that thin the sheet out more than they
  add.
- **Legibility flag.** Black bold strokes in 15104 and the red strokes in 15106 come close to
  Latin-like S/Z shapes, and 15110's echo reads as a 7. They need a human read before anything is
  kept.

  - score completeness: every relation's src and dst is a layer, every mark is in a recorded layer,
    every derived layer is explained;
  - caps and variety;
  - determinism in-process and across `PYTHONHASHSEED`;
  - the density band;
  - inside the paper, no stubs;
  - no bare crossings;
  - the void cuts exactly whom it says, and someone crosses it;
  - `s` ranges find their stretch;
  - the mechanisms: amplify, calm, translate styles, halo detour, echo ≤ 1.

  `test_kit.py` passes too.

## Proposed core changes (not made)

- `_score.relate` has `s` for the dst only. The src stretch a relation heard is the other half of the
  substructure. Here it lives in the dst layer's `params.gesture_src_mm`. A `s_src` field would put it
  where a bench expects it.
- A shared `s0` convention on marks (where a drawn piece sits along its layer's uncut line) would let
  any device or bench find a score range after cuts and gaps. `listen` writes `meta["s0"]`.
