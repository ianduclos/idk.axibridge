# lcorp: line corpora as compositional technique (round 15, thread B)

**Outcome.** The river zoom is now a tool for any line. `lcorp_zoom` takes a hand's chain, a relay of hands, a dance trail or the river, and zooms it at constant width on a free course. It also comes as a modulator, so any voice can be passed through it. Most hands yield (33 of 44 by the measure). The new piece is the zoom moved onto the sheet: a lens that every line opens in as it crosses. Applied style and relays work, and both keep their sources recognisable. Aware overlaps work as a close-up mechanism, but they do not help the lens piece.

## What I built

- `kit/voices_lcorp.py`
  - Voice `lcorp_zoom` (sources: hand, relay, dance, river).
  - Pure functions: `hand_chain`, `relay_chain`, `dance_line`, `zoom_line`, `magnify_lod`, `restyle_theta`, `pinch_schedule` and `zoom_measures`.
- `kit/modulators_lcorp.py`
  - Modulators `lcorp_zoom_mod` (the voice wrapper), `lcorp_style` and `lcorp_aware`.
  - `aware_marks` does the overlap work on any list of marks, so an arranger can use it too.
- `kit/arrangers_lcorp.py`: arranger `lcorp_lens` (a zoom field over the sheet). It records a `_score.Score`.
- `kit/renderers_lcorp.py`: renderer `lcorp_ink`. The pen width is constant. Colour is optional: by voice (the hand speaking) or by part.
- `experiments/lcorp_specimen.py` makes the working sheets. `experiments/lcorp_review.py` rebuilds the five images.
- `tests/test_kit_lcorp.py` has 17 tests. They check:
  - a constant zoom is a pure magnification;
  - ds = c·z·du;
  - the level of detail limits the far band;
  - every source gives one contiguous line, inside the box, at one pen width;
  - relay handovers fall in the far twists;
  - the measures separate flat lines from open ones;
  - applied style keeps each band whole and pins the course's ends;
  - depth cuts only the farther pass;
  - sympathy moves only the near pass;
  - the lens score is truthful and nothing is clipped;
  - output is deterministic across processes.

  `test_kit.py` still passes (41 tests).

## How the general zoom works

- **The source.** Any line is reduced to its heading θ(u). It is re-integrated with ds = c·z·du, as in round 14.
- **Far scale.** The far scale c is calibrated so that one quarter-turn of the source spans the river's far quarter-turn (2.2 mm). So every source starts equally dense, whatever its units.
- **Level of detail (`lod`, `reveal`).** This was the step that made hands work.
  - The camera has a fixed resolution on paper. At zoom z, the source's heading is smoothed at σ = res / (c·z^reveal).
  - Without it, hand chains drew fuzz in the far stretches and long straight lines when zoomed.
  - The river never needed it: its stem was already smoothed at 1.5 channel widths.
  - At reveal 1 (the true camera), a hand with bends inside bends looks the same at every zoom, so the zoom disappears.
  - At reveal 0, zoomed bends open into smooth balloons.
  - Reveal 0.4 sits between the two: the bends open large and keep a few smaller ones inside.
- **Valley.** Hand chains have no valley, so they ball up. `valley` removes the heading's drift above 4 quarter-turns, so the line travels like a river.
- **Measures.** Two measures are read off the drawn line:
  - `contrast`: how much larger a quarter-turn is when zoomed than when far. The river scores 15.
  - `inner`: the share of the zoomed turning that sits in features under 4 mm.

## Which hands yield, and why

The hypothesis was half right. There are two ways to fail, not one.

- **Flat (inner > 0.5):** river_bend, river_belt, mime_roadrunner, mime_mantis, trail_miner and dancer_madeline. These are uniform small-wiggle hands. Their zoomed stretches are as wiggly as their far ones, so the zoom becomes invisible. Hokusai counts as flat only on contrast (3.5). By eye it is middling.
- **Balloon (inner < 0.065):** fly_open, fly_fru, bubble and nastaliq. These are smooth hands with nothing finer inside their bends.
- **Yield:** everything in between: some large bends plus sparse small curls (inner 0.1 to 0.45).
  - Best by eye: dancer_cristel, dancer_amber, dance_staccato, dance_thrusting, trail_tracks, trail_slime, trail_oaknet, lichtenberg, marden, mime_dragon, mime_monkey_hands, molnar_letters and river_oxbow.
  - Weak yields: dance_particles, dancer_anna and river_meander.
- **The real reason a hand yields:** it has a *few* bend scales, sparsely mixed. Neither one scale (flat, or balloon) nor a continuum (self-similar, the zoom hides) works.
- **Raw dance trails** (one effector, 45 s) are too short. They have about 30 quarter-turns where the river has about 200, so there is no dense far stretch. Their learned hands (dance_*, dancer_*) yield instead.

## Duds (not shown, all in `.local/gesture/out/lcorp/`)

- **Hands without level of detail** (proto1 to 3): far stretches turned to fuzz, and zoomed stretches were long straight lines with tangles.
- **`lead` (the zoom leans in where the source has detail):** it evens the line out and kills the contrast. It is kept as a control parameter.
- **Depth inside the lens:** in a spatial field, passes crossing at one place share their zoom, so depth never fires. With the equal-zoom tie rule (`tie_z`), it cuts the opened tangle into small fragments: stray marks. The lens is shown without it.
- **First lens runs:** lines balled up at the left edge and were clipped along the frame. Fixed by straightening the course, keeping lines in lanes, and stopping a line inside the box instead of clipping it.

## Honest read

- **Specimen.** The zoom as a tool is the solid result. It answers Ian's question directly: it says which hands to call and why. I think Ian finds about a dozen hands worth calling, and agrees the flat ones go flat.
- **Lens (2).** It has direction and a middle density. It plays the 4507 move, an event in the middle that some lines cross and others skirt, as magnification instead of a void. The weakness is that the far texture of all five lanes looks alike. The level of detail makes hands similar when they are far. The lanes also read a little as stripes. My guess: "interesting direction", not a keeper as it is. It is stronger as a move inside a composition than as a sheet.
- **Style (3).** The river in Hokusai's bends reads as both. The dance trail in the river's bends is the surprise: the dance's sweep stays, and the river crawls along it. The Hokusai line in the river's bends loses Hokusai.
- **Aware (4).** Sympathy is the subtle one Ian asked for. Depth reads as over/under, which is more obvious. Neither changes much of a whole sheet, because the piece has few mixed-zoom overlaps.
- **Relay (5).** Clean and elegant at one line. Colour carries the hands. It might be the one he keeps.
