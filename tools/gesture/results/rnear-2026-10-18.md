# rnear: the Amazon river approached and left (round 14, thread c2, 2026-10-18)

Outcome first:

- The far Amazon stem now zooms in and out along itself, and it reads as one
  river approached by a camera. Dense twists open into large bends and close
  again, four times, unevenly. The kit voice is `river_approach`.
- The magnified stem does almost all of that work. The closer river hands,
  handed whole bends at their own zoom, join without a visible seam, but they
  add little you can see. In black ink you cannot tell where they are; only
  the colour map shows them. That is continuity, not new detail.
- The one close-up feature that does read is a real cut-off bend (Li et al.
  neck centrelines) at the nearest approach. Oxbow lakes hung at the bend
  read as stray marks (the dud).
- Nothing was downloaded or plotted. No shared file was edited.

## What I tried

- **Carrier.** The stem's own heading θ(u), u in channel widths, drawn at
  paper speed ds = k·du. Heading is continuous by construction; curvature on
  paper is κ/k. The zoom schedule k is defined over the line *as drawn*
  (paper share), so the twists grow and shrink gradually on the page. Each
  approach is a bump in log k with its own rise and fall (asymmetric), its
  own peak, and the deepest one is placed at random. The line starts and
  ends at k = 1, the far view.
- **The stem budget decides the composition.** The stem is 2268 w long. At
  the far zoom it is used up 1 : 1, at 40× it is nearly free. So a full sheet
  needs most of the drawn line inside approaches (`width` 0.8) and the far
  view in short dense passages between them. My first tries (far view most
  of the time, short spikes) gave a sparse band with one giant arc.
- **Handover ladder.** Each closer hand owns the zoom band it was learned at
  (`river_belt` 400 w → 4×, `river_meander` 150 w → 11×, `river_bend` 60 w
  → 27×). In its band, a share of stem bends (inflection to inflection) is
  handed over, one stem bend for a contiguous window of 1–4 of that hand's
  own bends. Nothing is averaged. The window:
  - starts turning the way the stem bend turns (mirrored if needed);
  - is drawn at the paper scale the local zoom implies (the hand's R in
    channel widths is measured on this stem, × k);
  - is the best match, in net turn and length, among windows ahead in the
    hand's running chain, so the river's course comes back afterwards;
  - may not cross itself (the "river law"), and may not hold one of the
    belt's piece-end corners.
- **Event.** At the nearest approach only, the tightest stem bend becomes a
  real cut-off bend, whole, at the bend's own scale. Option `oxbow` hangs a
  real oxbow outline at the apex instead.
- **Frame.** 10 candidate lines (start, approach centres, a ±20° turn) are
  built; the one that fits the box at the largest scale, then fills it best,
  is kept. Nothing is clipped. Frame 300 × 160 mm, margin 10: the river runs
  as a band, 300 mm is the AxiDraw's full travel, and 160 mm gives the
  nearest bends room to open without running off.

Two bugs were found and fixed on the way, both seams:

1. `grammar.tokens_to_points` carries a token's end curvature into the next
   token. A window joins segments from different strokes, so the drawn end
   heading disagreed with `end_heading`. The stem resumed at the wrong angle:
   cusps up to 178°. Windows are now integrated token by token
   (`integrate_tokens`), as passage does.
2. The cut-off profiles are 128 samples. At high zoom they showed facets.
   They are now resampled cubically in θ to the line's step.

After both, the largest heading change between 0.1-unit steps is about 7°
(the far stem's own twists) with hands and a cut-off on.

## Answers to the brief's questions

- **Approach or paste?** Approach. With heading, handedness and scale
  inherited at the joint, a handed bend reads as the same river. The stem
  alone (A) already reads as one river approached, and better than I
  expected.
- **Is it the Amazon up close?** Partly, honestly. The three GRWL hands
  were learned from 14 Amazon-basin stems each, one of them this very stem
  (`grwl:SB19:seg1`, its first 60/150/400 w). They are smoothed at the same
  1.5 w as the far stem, so they hold no finer detail than it does. Up close
  they are "a river at that zoom", not this river's hidden detail. The
  cut-off bends are real Amazon-basin necks.
- **Where should the handover sit?** Not gradually across the middle zooms:
  at share 0.95 (C) the hands still disappear into the line. At the peak
  only (D), river_bend's gentle lobes are indistinguishable from the
  magnified stem. The event at the nearest approach (E, F, piece) is the one
  place where something different happens and still reads as the river.
- **Can the joint be invisible?** Yes, after the cusp fix. See image 4.
- **Magnified sampling?** Not a problem at these zooms. The stem is sampled
  at w/4 and smoothed at 1.5 w; θ is a cubic spline, so a 40× bend is a clean
  curve. The flip side: at 40× a single bend becomes one plain arc (the
  long smooth strokes in A and G).
- **Symmetric = ornament?** Each approach has its own rise/fall and peak
  (`skew`, `vary`); the deepest is placed at random. Read: F (one slow
  approach) is the most ornamental; four unequal approaches (piece, G) read
  most like a river.

## Honest read

- Against "dynamics": strong contrast of scale inside one line (far twists
  a few mm long next to bends of 80 mm and more), and the camera rhythm varies from sheet to
  sheet. Density is moderate: one line on a strip leaves blank. It is a
  voice for compositions, not a composition.
- Against "relation between regimes": weak. The far view and the near view
  are one regime at two scales. The hands' handover is technically clean and
  visually silent. The cut-off is the only second regime that shows.
- Would Ian keep it? I think he would keep the zooming river itself, as the
  fresh version of the Amazon found river (the piece, or A from the
  population). I would not sell the hand ladder as a visible feature.

## Proposed core changes (not made)

1. `grammar.tokens_to_points` could take a `per_token=True` flag (integrate
   each token from its own k0), so every voice that splices segments from
   different strokes gets `end_heading`-consistent geometry. Passage and
   rnear both work around it.
2. A second frozen stem at higher resolution (GRWL less smoothed, e.g.
   σ = 0.5 w) for the nearest approach, so zooming in could show detail the
   far view hides. It needs a local re-extraction, not a download.
