# gmorph: one hand's grammar morphed continuously (round 13, thread II-a, 2026-10-18)

**Outcome.** One river_belt line on a 460 × 70 mm strip changes gesture type four
times: long calm bends, then a hairpin meander, a sawtooth, an angular coil, and
loops. It stays one hand all the way, because every segment and corner is a token
from that hand's own chain. Seven of the hand's grammar dials move on their own
schedules. The types come from combining dials, so no single dial makes them.
I think it passes the judging test, and the middle is the strongest part.

## What I tried

- **A new voice, `gmorph`** (`kit/voices_gmorph.py`). It runs the hand's order-2
  token chain in chunks of training-stroke length, as the drift line does. At each
  draw it re-weights the chain's own proposals by dials that change with position
  u along the line:
  - `span`: which of the hand's segment lengths to prefer, as a quantile.
  - `turn`: which of its bends to prefer, from calm to tight, as a quantile.
  - `corners`: how often its corner tokens are chosen.
  - `sharp`: which of its corners, from blunt to hairpin, as a quantile.
  - `sign`: zigzag (each turn against the last) or coil (each turn the same way).
  - `gain`: curvature gain.
  - `pull`: the belt's pull back to the valley axis.
  - `r`: an optional regime schedule.
- Then the voice integrates the tokens along a valley, as `belt` does.
- **Dials are quantiles of each hand's own vocabulary.** So one schedule can drive
  any hand, and selecting tokens never rescales them.
- **Each schedule is a knot list** `[u, value, …]`, cosine-eased between knots.

## What happened

These are the lessons in order. Scratch images are in `.local/gesture/out/gmorph/`.

1. **κ-continuity was undoing the dials.** The chain's continuity step makes an arc
   take the previous curvature. So "calm, long" tokens came out as long tight arcs,
   and the calm start drew big loops. The fix: judge each proposal *as it will be
   drawn* (`_realised`).
2. **Zigzag by flipping only the end curvature made S-wiggles, not a meander.** A
   meander needs whole bends reversing. Now a ruled token is the hand's token
   mirrored whole. The tangent stays continuous and the curvature may jump at the
   joint, which is what a reversing meander does.
3. **Coiling made concentric rings**, which looked mechanical. A coil now lets go
   with a counter-turn after `loop_rad` (7 rad), so the loops travel.
4. **The sign rule now applies to corners too.** Zigzag plus corners gives a
   sawtooth. Coil plus corners gives the *angular coil* (folded box flaps). That
   is a type that neither neighbour has.
5. **Calm types eat horizontal space**, because they progress about 1 mm per mm
   of line while tight types progress about 0.2 mm. So the knots are placed by
   eye, giving each type roughly 90 mm of strip.

## Duds

- **`gmorph-dud.jpg` (out dir only, not one of the four).** Row 1 moves one dial
  alone (`turn` 0.1 to 0.95, one ramp). The result is the hand's usual mix getting
  curlier, with no sequence of types. Row 2 is the morphing_belt study's single
  gain ramp: the bends tighten and the type stays the same. Together they show
  that one dial is a slider, and that the types need combinations.
- **Other hands under the first schedules** (scratch `hands-contact.jpg`). Hands
  with calm vocabularies (matisse, marden) mostly wobbled. The end piled into one
  knot.

## My honest read

- **Judging test (Axis II).**
  - The change starts where the calm bends first fold into hairpins, about 85 mm
    in, and it runs left to right.
  - Start: long calm bends. Middle: a sawtooth coast (corners turning to
    alternate sides, straight runs between them), then the angular coil. End:
    travelling loops.
  - The middle is its own type, not an average. Neither end has corners at all;
    the sawtooth exists only because two dials overlap there.
- **Weak spots.**
  - The angular coil is short: two or three flaps, then a free sweep.
  - The end loops sometimes knot.
  - The calm start is close to plain.
  - The strip is small for its detail. The close-up carries the reading.
- **Would Ian keep it?** I think the close-up (image 2) is more likely to be kept
  than the strip. It shows "the gradual regime morphing of the line" at a size
  where you can see it. The hands image argues that this is a mechanism, not a
  one-off. The risk is that he reads the coast and sawtooth as "river again",
  since the hand is a river hand.
- **Regime r is wired but untried.** I did not use it, to keep the change in
  the line's grammar rather than in width.

## Seeds and rebuild

Seeds are 13101–13108. The piece is 13102, and the variants are listed above.
Every image has its recipe beside it (`*.recipe.json`; for the hands contact it is
`{"rows": [...]}`). The images rebuild with
`.local/gesture/.venv/bin/python tools/gesture/experiments/gmorph.py piece|close|schedule|hands|dud`.
The route is `ROUTE3` in that script.
