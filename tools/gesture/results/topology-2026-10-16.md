# The composition / topology layer: relations measured, a structural generator (2026-10-16)

Files: `gesture/topology.py` (measure + generator), `tests/test_topology.py`,
`experiments/topology_study.py` (`measure`, `compare`, `evaluate`, `sheets`).
Raw outputs: `.local/gesture/out/topology/{compare,evaluate}.{md,json}`, per-artist
models `model_{artist}.json`, generated drawings in `gen/`, sheets in `sheets/`.
Sheets kept here: `topology-trees.jpg`, `topology-in-hand.jpg`, `topology-learned.jpg`.

**Verdict.**

- Topology alone separates the three artists about as well as the geometry panel:
  3-way balanced accuracy 0.70 (logreg) / 0.75 (forest), chance 0.33.
  Most of that comes from a few relations, not from the ink field.
- Grammar v3's composition is closer to real than expected: real-vs-generated AUC
  0.90–0.95 on topology features only. Its errors are specific: too many end-to-end
  joins, and those joins are smooth, where real ones are sharp.
- The learned structural generator is not yet closer than v3 (AUC 0.96–0.99). It
  under-produces ends that stop just short of another line.
- `kind="oehlen_tree"` is by design far from the real figure drawings (AUC 1.0). It
  is the first generator here whose sheets read as winding, forking, sheet-filling
  trees without texture. Ready for Ian to look at; nothing has been plotted.

## 1. Relation taxonomy (what `relation_graph` measures)

Strokes are nodes and relations are edges. Every threshold is in R, the
reference length (the bbox diagonal), with a floor of 1.5 line widths. Thresholds
are in R rather than line widths because w/R differs by 2× between collections
(Hokusai 0.0029, Matisse 0.0015, Ingres 0.0014). That is the known σ/w scan
confound.

| # | relation | definition | recorded |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **T** (end on line) | open end within 0.005 R of another line's interior | host position u, branch angle (folded 0..π/2) |
| R2 | **L** (join) | end within 0.005 R of another line's end (≤ 0.012 R along the host) | deflection: 0 = smooth continuation, π = cusp |
| R3 | **near** | end stops 0.005–0.02 R short of another line | gap, aim (angle between the end direction and its nearest line) |
| R4 | **free** | nothing within 0.02 R | — |
| R5 | **X** | proper crossing ≥ 0.01 R from either line's ends | crossing angle |
| R6 | **P** (parallel run) | ≥ 0.03 R of a line within (touch, 0.06 R] of another's interior, tangents within 20° | offset, overlap length, offset ÷ overlap, offset CV |
| R7 | **K** (kiss) / merge | interiors touch tangentially and part without changing side; ≤ 0.1 R of contact = kiss, longer = merge | contact length |
| R8 | **forms** | closed or end gap ≤ 0.1 × length; containment → nesting depth | area, depth |
| R9 | **cells** | faces of the planar arrangement (ends extended by touch) | count, area ÷ ink hull |
| R10 | **graph** | contact graph (T, L, X) and all-relation graph | degree, isolated share, hubs (≥ 2 T on one host), components, largest component's ink share |
| R11 | **ink field** | 10 × 10 lattice over the ink bbox | coverage, entropy, central quarter mass, radial spread; canvas margins (descriptive only, because scan crops are arbitrary) |

Tests (`test_topology.py`, 11 tests, about 6 s) build scenes with known relations
and check that each one is detected:

- T: u and angle are recovered.
- X: one crossing at 90°.
- L: two joins with 90° deflection.
- Near-meeting: gap 0.01 R, aimed at the other line.
- Nested parallel arcs: 30 px offset recovered to ±4 px.
- Three concentric rings: nesting depth 1/2/3, three cells.
- Two tangent circles: one kiss, no crossing. A loop's seam doesn't split the run.

The tests also cover determinism and purity, and that the generator is
contiguous, keeps no confetti and gives long lines.

## 2. Per-artist topology signatures (real long-stroke subsets)

Inputs: `grammar_v3_frozen.long_drawing` (no hatching), 31 / 27 / 34 drawings.
The table shows the median and IQR per artist, ordered by Kruskal–Wallis H. Full
40-feature table: `.local/gesture/out/topology/compare.md`.

| S2 | ink entropy on the lattice | **.886** | .839 | .819 | 20.0 |
| S3 | parallel-run pairs per stroke | .33 (.18–.45) | **.49** (.32–.70) | .22 (.14–.30) | 19.1 |

Signatures, in words:

1. **Matisse.** Lines spread over the whole sheet: highest coverage and entropy.
   - When a Matisse line stops short of another, it **points at it**: aim 1.13 rad,
     against about 1.4 for the others. These are aimed near-meetings, lines that
     almost arrive.
   - His crossings are near-perpendicular (1.30 rad).
   - The "nested parallel curves" trait is real but **not distinctive** here.
     Hokusai has more parallel runs (S3), and the offsets are the same for all
     three (≈ 0.03 R).
2. **Hokusai.** Long lines that branch.
   - The most T ends, the most hubs (one line carrying several branches) and the
     largest connected components.
   - The most parallel pairs: double contours of the brush.
3. **Ingres.** Short, isolated contour pieces: 59 % of strokes touch nothing.
   - Where they do meet, they meet **tangentially**. He has the only nonzero
     kisses, the shallowest T angles (0.61 rad) and the shallowest crossings.
     Graphite contours graze each other.
4. **Shared by all three.** About 60 % of ends stop 0.005–0.02 R short of
   another line, and only about 18 % are free.
   - In these drawings a line rarely ends in empty space, and rarely ends exactly
     on another line.
   - Joins are sharp, not smooth (≈ 1.2 rad), because the extractor already
     chains smooth continuations into one stroke.

**What distinguishes them** (grouped 5-fold × 5, standard medium projection):

| # | comparison | logreg / forest |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | 3-way, all 40 features (raw → projected) | .64 / .72 → **.70 / .75** |
| D2 | 3-way, without economy features (length, count, ink) | .63 / .68 |
| D3 | 3-way, ends + graph only (16 features) | .62 / .66 |
| D4 | 3-way, ink field only (5 features) | .50 / .40 |
| D5 | Matisse vs Hokusai, pairwise AUC (projected) | .79 / .84 |
| D6 | Matisse vs Ingres, pairwise AUC (projected) | .90 / .88 |
| D7 | Hokusai vs Ingres, pairwise AUC (projected) | .94 / .96 |

Permutation importance (forest):

- stroke length .080;
- offset ÷ overlap .023;
- parallel pairs .017;
- coverage .017;
- near aim .016;
- kisses .013;
- hubs .013.

Caveats:

1. **Stroke length is partly a selection artefact.** The long subset's floor is
   40 w, and w/R is 2× larger for Hokusai, so its floor is about 0.12 R against
   0.06 R. D2 is the conservative number.
2. **The medium is not removed.** Matisse etching vs lithograph is still
   recoverable from topology: held-out AUC 0.68 → 0.65 linear and 0.67 → 0.58
   forest after projection. Part of the topology signal is medium (crayon breaks
   lines into near-meetings).
3. **These are statistics of extracted long subsets, not of the drawings.** The
   extractor splits at junctions, and the subset drops short connectors. A figure's
   contour therefore arrives as fragments that nearly meet. That is the fair
   comparison target for anything learned from these subsets, but it is not what the
   eye sees as one line.

## 3. What grammar v3 gets wrong (composition only)

v3 was run from `grammar_v3_frozen`, using the saved grammars in
`.local/gesture/out/grammar_v2/`, on the real canvases scaled to a long side of
1600 px. Its output was measured with the same topology features.

| # | | Matisse | Hokusai | Ingres |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V0 | real vs v3 AUC, topology only (logreg / forest) | .90 / .95 | .95 / .98 | .90 / .90 |
| V1 | **L joins** (share of ends), real → v3 | .036 → **.117** | 0 → **.167** | .061 → .097 |
| V2 | **join deflection** (rad), real → v3 | 1.25 → **.52** | 1.18 → **.38** | 1.32 → **.50** |
| V3 | kisses per ink, real → v3 | 0 → .18 | 0 → .22 | .25 → 0 |
| V4 | T angle (rad), real → v3 | .91 → 1.04 | — | .61 → .79 |
| V5 | X angle (rad), real → v3 | 1.30 → 1.01 | — | — |
| V6 | isolated strokes, real → v3 | — | .37 → .23 | — |
| V7 | contact components ÷ strokes, real → v3 | — | .59 → .40 | — |
| V8 | parallel ink, real → v3 | — | .22 → .13 | — |

How to read the rows:

1. **V1 + V2 are one error.** v3's "continue from a free end" relation
   (tangent ± N(0, 0.25)) makes smooth end-to-end joins: 3–∞ × too many of them, at
   0.4–0.5 rad deflection against a real 1.2–1.3. The extractor never produces a
   smooth join, because it merges those into one stroke. Real joins are therefore
   corners, and v3's continuation should either merge into the previous line or turn
   sharply.
2. **Hokusai rows (V6–V8).** v3 connects Hokusai too much (fewer isolated strokes,
   fewer components) and draws too few double contours.
3. **V3 and V5.** v3 kisses where Matisse and Hokusai never do, and does not kiss
   where Ingres does. Its crossings are too shallow for Matisse.
4. **What v3 already gets right.** Near/free ends, T rate, crossings per ink,
   coverage and entropy are all within real IQRs. Its composition was never the main
   tell; line texture was (`grammar-v3.md`).

## 4. The structural generator

`generate_structure(model, rng, size, kind=...)` returns a `Drawing` of smooth,
hand-less skeleton polylines.

- Each stroke carries meta: relation, host, order, and how it ended.
- Each stroke is a curvature walk at the path scale: an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck κ(s)
  with the learned sd of κ·R after σ = 0.015 R smoothing, about 7–8, and its
  correlation length.
- Each walk is steered:
  - off the sheet edge;
  - around other ink and itself, through look-ahead probes on an occupancy raster;
  - toward empty space, through the gradient of the coarse ink density, minus an
    optional figure envelope.

Relations are explicit:

- **`kind="learned"`** reads a `TopologyModel`. The model is JSON, learned per
  artist by `learn_model`, and every field can be hand-set.
  - **New lines start** as T (from a host interior at a learned u and angle), L
    (from a free end, with a learned deflection, at half rate because one join makes
    two L ends), P (an offset copy of a host stretch at a learned offset and overlap,
    then free growth), near (at a learned gap) or free (in an emptier part of a smooth
    random "figure envelope").
  - **A line that meets ink** ends on it (T), crosses it (within the learned X rate
    per ink) or steers away. It stops short when it is boxed in.
  - **Near the end of its planned length**, a line homes in on the nearest ink and
    stops at a learned gap, with the learned near/(near + free) probability.
  - **Lines shorter than the learned q10 length** are discarded.
- **`kind="oehlen_tree"`** has hand-set parameters:
  - **Trunks.** 1–3 trunks rise from the bottom edge (sometimes a side), with
    tropism up.
  - **Forks.** Sympodial forks every ~0.25 of a branch, on alternating sides, at
    0.6 rad ±40 %. Children are 0.62 × the remaining length; twigs wind 1.25× more
    per order and fork less.
  - **Fill.** Tips are pulled into empty space, and branches re-sprout toward the
    emptiest lattice cell until the coverage reaches `fill` (0.85).
  - **Meetings.** On meeting ink a tip ends on it (0.2), crosses it (0.15) or
    avoids it.
  - **Against texture.** A clearance of 0.01 R and a minimum branch length of
    0.08 R. Lines leave at the sheet edge instead of running along it.
  - **Complexity knobs:** `depth`, `trunks`, `fill`, `fork_every`, `ratio`, `wind`,
    `tropism`.

### Evaluation against the real subsets

| # | real vs | AUC logreg / forest | main residuals (gen vs real median) |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Matisse learned | .965 / .963 | near ends .45 vs .59; free .31 vs .18; no near-closed forms (0 vs .03); X angle 1.04 vs 1.30 |
| G2 | Hokusai learned | .987 / .962 | near .34 vs .56; free .31 vs .19; L .10 vs 0 |
| G3 | Ingres learned | .957 / .988 | near .43 vs .61; free .40 vs .18; coverage .64 vs .55 |
| G4 | tree vs any artist | 1.0 / 1.0 | one component (ink share 1.0 vs .2–.3); T ends .44 vs .09–.21; cells .9 per ink vs 0; contact degree 2.1 vs .5–.9 |

The first learned run scored 0.99–1.00. Two fixes brought it to G1–G3:

- joins were double-counted;
- ends never sought ink.

Lengths, the T rate, the parallel-run rate and crossings per ink now match. The
remaining tell is the ends. Even with end-seeking, near ends stay at 0.34–0.45
against a real 0.56–0.61, and free ends at about 0.3–0.4 against 0.18.

The tree is not meant to match a figure drawing. Its numbers say what it is: one
connected, branching, cellular structure, which is what Ian asked for. No
long-subset figure drawing looks like that.

## 5. The sheets, honestly

1. **`topology-trees.jpg`** (oehlen_tree; rows depth 3/4/5; columns fill
   0.7/0.85/0.95 with 1/1/2/3 trunks). These read as trees: trunks rise and wind,
   fork, and the forks fork again into a crown.
   - Depth 5 with one trunk (bottom left) is the most tree-like.
   - At fill 0.95 with 2–3 trunks the sheet fills with a network of branching
     lines that close into irregular cells, closer to a cracked glaze or leaded
     glass than to foliage. No hatching and no confetti anywhere.
   - Weak points:
     - The re-sprouts that fill the space make long, calm arcs that read as
       "balloon" outlines rather than branches.
     - Twig tips sometimes bunch into small tufts.
     - Lines that run off the sheet end abruptly at the margin.
     - Hierarchy (thick to thin) is carried only by length and wiggle. Every line
       has the same weight.
2. **`topology-in-hand.jpg`**: two structures, each as a skeleton, in Matisse's
   hand and in Hokusai's hand, via **restyle.py** (`restyle_drawing`, gauss/chain,
   amount 0.5).
   - **The tree in Hokusai's hand** is the best image of the round: a gnarled,
     scalloped tree, with structure and junctions intact.
   - **In Matisse's hand** the tree gets lumps and kinks.
   - **Artefacts:** a few tight spirals at line ends ("@" marks), and some jagged
     stretches.
   - The learned Matisse structure in either hand reads as a dense knot of
     fragments around a mass.
3. **`topology-learned.jpg`**: learned structures per artist, 4 each.
   - **Matisse:** dense knots of medium-length lines around a mass (the envelope),
     with lines nearly meeting, a few crossings and occasional curls. Some sheets
     have small fans of 3–4 short parallel strokes, which border on hatching
     (the P relation at short overlap).
   - **Hokusai:** fewer, longer, wandering connected lines with loops. This is the
     most coherent row.
   - **Ingres:** sparse short pieces. #2 is a dud (scattered fragments).
   - None of these reads as a figure. That is honest about the target: the learned
     topology is the topology of fragmentary extracted subsets.

## 6. How this plugs in

- **Into the grammar (v4).** Use the relation layer as the grammar's composer:
  1. Replace v3's smooth "continue" with a sharp join or a merge (§3 V1–V2).
  2. Use the learned near-aim and end-seeking for stops.
  3. Use the P relation for double contours (Hokusai).
  4. Then score v4 with `topology_features` next to the panel. A topology-only
     AUC is a cheap second test 1.
- **Structure from A, hand from B.** `generate_structure` → `restyle.restyle_drawing`
  works today (§5.2). The skeleton's per-stroke meta (relation, host) is there so a
  later restyle could re-snap T ends after redrawing; today the ends are only pinned.
- **Into axibridge, later.**
  1. **`oehlen_tree` is a generator** in its own right. Its parameters are already
     bounded and dimensionless; mm output means R = the sheet diagonal.
  2. **`TopologyModel` is a small JSON profile.** It would fit the "learned profile
     = project asset" option in `IDEAS-gesture-bank.md` (open decision, not settled
     here).
  3. **A topology-aware composer** (start relations, end behaviour) could also drive
     the existing `continue_strokes` / `freehand` sources.

## 7. Next steps

1. **Ends.** Make end-seeking reliable: retry the planned length when the seek
   fails, and sample the stop gap and aim together, so that near ends reach the
   real 0.6.
2. **The fragment target.** Measure a **chain view**: merge real strokes across
   aimed near-meetings and sharp joins into "perceived lines", then learn the
   topology of those. That is closer to what the eye reads as one line, and the
   generator could then emit one skeleton line per perceived line.
3. **The tree.**
   - Separate re-sprouts (space-filling) from forks visually: shorter, more
     wiggly re-sprouts.
   - Optionally map order to pen or multi-pass for weight.
   - Explore the "computer painting" direction: several trees, each crossing
     others' regions at higher p_cross.
4. **Grammar.** Feed §3 into the grammar rewrite: sharp or merged joins, Hokusai's
   connectivity and double contours.
5. **Confounds.** Run the comparison on scans re-extracted at a common line width
   (the σ/w confound), and add a medium-matched pair before trusting the length and
   near-meeting signals.
