# Morph by accumulation — round 13, thread II-c `accrue` (2026-10-18)

One line changes gesture type because of what it has already done. The river_belt
hand draws free wire loops. Each loop it happens to make the same way tightens the
next, so a habit forms one loop at a time. The tight loops press the pen into a
nib, harden into a pressed loop chain, and choke into a knot. At the knot the
habit breaks and its turning passes to the nib. The nib spins: barbs, then a
twisted rope, then a calm ribbon that dries back to wire. Further on, a new habit
starts, faster this time. Nothing outside the line steers any of this: no driver,
no field, no route that changes the gesture.

## What I built

- **`kit/voices_accrue.py`**, voice **`accrue_walk`**. A learned hand speaks
  every token; each chunk is the best of K proposals, as in the drift line. A
  small body of state travels with the line and bends the hand's tokens:
  - **habit** (`habit="loops"`): a ratchet. The first full turn the hand makes
    sets a sense. Every further loop that way tightens the curl bias
    (logistic: slow, then fast, then a limit). A counter-turn loosens it.
    The curl is a staircase with one step per loop, not a slide. As the curl
    grows, the hand's own curvature is damped, so the loops become regular.
  - **press**: self-density, the share of the newest stretch lying on the
    line's own past.
  - **tip**: when a hardened coil sits on itself for 30 mm, it becomes a knot
    (mass). The curl drops to zero and is handed to the nib as spin
    (Ω = 4·C/R rad/mm, half-life 35 mm). The path runs nearly straight for
    40 mm.
  - **fatigue**: each break makes the next coil tighter, and the next habit
    forms faster (`learn`). After `exhaust` breaks the habit is spent.
  - **paper memory**: every stretch leaves its state where it was drawn.
    Passing within 6 mm of a stretch at least 150 mm older, the line takes on
    80 % of that state, at most once per 150 mm.
  - The left-to-right target and the edge turn are composition only; the edge
    turn is kept out of the habit.
  - The voice writes `acc_*` channels and an event list (loops, relapses,
    knots, recalls) in `meta["accrue"]`.
  - `habit="leaky"` keeps the first design (curl = H·A, runaway) for the duds.
- **`kit/modulators_accrue.py`**, modulator **`accrue_ink`**: maps the line's
  own state to regime channels:
  - tightening curl → the pen presses (wire → nib);
  - knot → mass;
  - nib spin → nib angle integrated along the line;
  - spin below 0.04 rad/mm → wire again.
- **`experiments/accrue_explore.py`**: the working stages (hands, seeds,
  one, chart). **`experiments/accrue_final.py`**: rebuilds the four review
  images.
- **`tests/test_kit_accrue.py`**: 9 tests, all passing. They cover:
  registration, determinism in-process and across PYTHONHASHSEED, ink on the
  paper, the curl as a one-sense staircase until the first break, the break
  handing spin to the nib (which then decays), the exhaust limit and recall
  gap, bounded regimes, and leaky mode. `test_kit.py` passes (41).

**Frame (chosen):** 297 × 105 mm, half an A4 sheet cut lengthwise. It plots in
one pass on the AxiDraw bed. The 2.8 : 1 strip makes the line read left to
right as a process, as the morphing belt did. Seeds 3901–3971; the piece is
seed 3944 with the voice defaults.

## What happened

1. **Leaky habit, first design** (curl = H·A fed back through a gain that grows
   with length). Past H = 1 it runs away. The result was a spiral, then a
   knot, then a spiral, then a knot: a cycle, not a development. At smaller
   scale it became knot after knot with barbs over barbs (dud a).
2. **Spin handed to the nib at the break.** This was right from the first try,
   and it is what dud 3 had: barbs → twist → calm, now caused by the line's
   own coil instead of a mosquito.
3. **A rate cap on the leaky curl** made the forming visible, but the curl
   became a smooth ramp, which is the brief's named risk (dud b).
4. **Paper memory as a continuous bath** kept re-infecting tangles with spin,
   which left heavy ribbon knots at the end. Fix: memory only from stretches
   at least 150 mm older, once per 150 mm. Recalls are now events.
5. **The loop ratchet** is the turn of this thread. The habit forms from the
   hand's own loops, one step per loop. On the paper this shows as wire loops
   that become same-handed, smaller and pressed. It does not always arrive:
   4 of 12 seeds (3901–3971 sweep) reach no knot, and in some the hand never
   repeats a turn often enough for the habit to start (image 3, row 3). It is
   not a parameter slide.
6. With `c_max` 45 a returned habit sometimes became a long pressed coil
   cord. That is a strong new type, but beaded and heavy; `c_max` 32 keeps it
   a chain.

## My honest read

- **Axis II test.** Where the change starts:
  - Loop 1 (109 mm) sets a sense.
  - It first shows at the dark double loop left of centre (346 mm), where the
    pen starts to press.
  - It runs left to right.
  - Before: free river-belt wire with loops both ways.
  - The middle is its own thing: a pressed chain of same-handed loops, at its
    limit from 457 mm. It is a cord, not an average of wire and knot.
  - The tip is the knot (658 mm).
  - After: a twisted rope, a ribbon, then wire.
  - The strip shows the curl stepping with each loop rather than sliding.
- **What it does not do.** Paper memory is in the mechanism and fires (marker
  8), but in the piece it is a small event, not a strong "memory returns".
  The strongest returns happened at `c_max` 45, where they look heavy. The
  barbs are faint at this setting; the release reads mostly as rope.
- **Would Ian keep it?**
  - Likely yes: the process strip, because it is pointable and the habit is
    caused from inside, plus the knot → rope passage as an image.
  - Possibly not: the piece as a finished sheet. The right-hand free hand is
    busy and angular, and the twisted ribbon is near "the aesthetic we already
    achieved". What is new is the forming and breaking, not the ribbon.
  - Most likely he will want this habit put on a line that also meets other
    regimes (Axis I).
