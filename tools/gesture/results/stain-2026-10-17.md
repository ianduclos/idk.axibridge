# Mass from real stains — round 12, thread 3 (`stain`, 2026-10-17)

A line that slows bleeds into the cloth. Where a dancer's hand pauses, the line runs into a stain: once
round the stain's rough edge, then back and forth over part of its ring (the tide line). Each pass lands
in a slightly different place, so the passes cross rather than nest. Then the line goes on through the
stain along its own path. The longer the pause, the bigger the stain and the more of its dark ring
closes. A short hesitation leaves an open crescent; a long stillness leaves a ring that is almost closed.
Everything is one contiguous line per hand. Nothing was plotted or downloaded.

## What I tried, in order

1. **Measured the corpus.** The Fabric Stain Dataset has **no masks, only bounding boxes** (398 stain
   photos and 68 defect-free; 870 boxes). Each box is segmented by its own darkness against a plane fitted
   to the box border, after blurring away the weave at the weave's own period, which is measured from the
   spectrum. 755 stains were measured (`experiments/stain_measure.py`, about 70 s). The statistics
   (no stored shapes) are in library `stain/stain_fabric`:

   | measure | corpus |
   |---|---|
   | darkness profile | 90 % are a "mesa": dark to a steep edge. Iso-darkness fronts crowd at the edge: gap 0.06 R there, 0.17 R inside |
   | tide line (rim darker than core × 1.15) | 10 % of stains. The ring sits at r ≈ 0.7–0.95 R; the core is 0.66 of the ring's darkness |
   | how much of the ring is dark (rimmed stains) | median **63 %** (IQR 56–77 %): rings mostly fail to close |
   | front roughness (radius, modes > 6) | 0.071 R (IQR 0.036–0.12), spectral slope −2.5 |
   | shape | elongation 1.6. Many stains are smeared fingerprints, so this is ignored on purpose (beads stay round) |
   | weave anisotropy (front along threads ÷ diagonals) | median 1.03: **weak**. Thread "arms" show only in a coarse-weave subset (the cross-shaped drops) |
   | satellites | 54 % have at least one; distance 1.1–2.6 R; tiny (median 0.6 % of the main area) |
   | halo (faint wet zone) | reaches 1.16 R |

2. **A woven medium.** Fronts are first-passage times (Dijkstra, 16-neighbour) through warp and weft
   threads with log-normal conductance over fibre noise. One weave serves the whole sheet. Calibration
   against the photos: with fiber 0.45 the fronts measure 0.067 R and slope −2.7 (corpus 0.071, −2.5). The
   piece uses fiber 0.3 (≈ 0.045 R, inside the corpus IQR) for rounder beads.
3. **Where the line slows.** For a dance voice, the slowing comes from the dance's own clock (`dance_t`).
   Otherwise it comes from the 2/3 power law (speed ∝ κ^-1/3). Stain reach ∝ √(time above 1.5 × the
   part's median time per mm), which is Washburn's law. An even clock leaves no stain; this is tested.
4. **Mass grammars, most of them duds** (see below). Spiral laps of the fronts look like a topographic map.
   A particle wandering in its own ink is a scribble. Loose threads give shards. Nested "pinned" laps look
   like tree rings or roses. What worked was the **crossing passes**: the edge drawn once, then the tide
   line drawn as stick-slip arcs. The arcs cover a share of the ring that grows with dwell (0.2 → 0.75),
   and the line runs through the middle.

## The images

1. **`stain-piece.jpg` — the finished piece.** *Levitation*, both hands, 15 s from t = 15 s, seed 6620,
   frame **200 × 200 mm**. I chose the frame on purpose: the stains are round, the two hands have no
   preferred direction, and a square keeps both lines large while staying well inside the AxiDraw bed.
   There are seven stains (4 + 3) at the hands' real pauses. Some are open crescents, others nearly closed rings.
   The long right-hand descent ends in the biggest stain, and the hand's pause jitter shows inside it.
2. **`stain-strip.jpg` — the drawing beside its measurement.** It has four panels:
   - **A:** the corpus darkness profiles (mesa / tide line).
   - **B:** the medium's fronts for one blot.
   - **C:** one stain at three dwell lengths: the ring grows by √t and closes.
   - **D:** each hand's excess time per mm, with ticks where the stains fall.
3. **`stain-closeup.jpg`.** A close-up of the big right-hand stain at 30 px/mm: the crossing passes of the
   tide line, the thin whole edge, the line passing through, and a small neighbouring stain.
4. **`stain-duds.jpg` — the same line drawn three wrong ways.** Each was a real attempt.
   - The front spiral (`schedule=density`) reads as a topographic map or tree rings. This is the risk the
     brief named, and it happened.
   - The particle in its own wet ink (`stain_particle`) is a scribble knot.
   - Loose wicking threads (`arms=4`) break the stains into shards.
5. **`stain-variant.jpg` — a different clock.** This uses the `fly_fru` hand with no recorded clock, so
   the 2/3 power law decides where it lingers. The stains land on the fly's coils, and the coils sit
   inside them. At this scale the weave shows: the fronts come out square or diamond-shaped, like the
   coarse-weave drops in the corpus.

Every image has its recipe or parameters beside it (`stain-*.recipe(s).json`, `stain-strip.params.json`).
Rebuilding the piece from its recipe gives the same points in separate processes with different
`PYTHONHASHSEED` values. Seeds: piece 6620, variant 7311, strip blots 12 and 30–32.

## Honest read

- **Would Ian keep the piece?** Maybe, as a direction rather than a finished study.
- **What works:**
  - The masses are not contour lines and not photo traces.
  - The tide-line crescents that fail to close come straight from the 63 % measurement and read as stain,
    not as a glyph.
  - Pauses come from a dancer's real stillness.
  - The graded crescent → ring is the transition, and it reads.
- **What doesn't:**
  - The bigger rings still lean toward "rose or cabbage" when many passes stack.
  - The thin rough edge is a little frilly. The corpus roughness may partly be fabric texture caught in
    the measurement.
  - The transition is mostly between stains (small → large), not inside one: the line meets the edge and
    the mass begins. I never made a convincing sheath where the line visibly swells before the stain. The
    comet tail (`tail`) barely shows on real dance pauses, which are abrupt.
  - The square fronts in the variant come from the lattice. They are honest to the coarse-weave subset
    but look polygonal.
- **Could not do:**
  - There are no stain masks in the corpus, so the segmentation is my own and unchecked against ground
    truth.
  - The weave orientation measured from the spectra was noisy (twill diagonals), so the "fronts lead along
    the threads" claim stays unproven corpus-wide.
