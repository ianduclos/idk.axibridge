# Round 13, thread II-d: morph across a series (prefix `stages`), 2026-10-18

**Outcome.** One line crosses five A5 sheets, and it changes only where it meets
something. It rides the far river and takes the river's bends. It crosses a red
voice and becomes a broad nib. It crosses its own wet trace and pools beads.
When the ink is spent it dries to a hairline and leaves. Moving the river or the
red voice off the line's path removes exactly the stage it caused. The
counterfactual image shows this, and the tests check it.

## What I built

- **`kit/arrangers_stages.py`**
  - **The line (`stages_line`)** is integrated in curvature space:
    θ' = κ_src − pull·sin(θ − θ_waypoint), plus soft walls.
  - **Where κ comes from.** κ_src is spoken one whole bend at a time (from one
    inflection to the next). It comes from the line's own hand (`ingres`, drift
    removed, as the `belt` voice reads it) or from a grammar the line has caught.
  - **Bends are interleaved whole, never averaged.** The middle of a morph is
    big hand curves with river meanders set into them.
  - **The route** is a list of rough waypoints. It decides where the line goes,
    never what the line is.
  - **`stages_row`** places the other parts as event layers, each in its own box
    and pen, each with an effect:
    - `grammar`: when the line is near and aligned, it rides 0.9 mm beside the
      layer and leaves at a tangent. After that, the layer's own bends come back
      into the line more and more often (p = p_max·(1 − e^(−Δs/τ))). For the
      frozen far-view stem, those are the river's bends *past the fragment's
      end*, downstream or upstream.
    - `nib`: each crossing sets the nib angle to the crossed line's heading and
      adds width. Width and angle then approach the new target over 30 mm.
    - `hand`: each crossing raises the crossed voice's hand share.
  - **Events that come from the line itself:**
    - a wet nib (W ≥ 1.4 mm) that crosses its own trace pools a round,
      line-built bead, each bead bigger than the last;
    - beads and nib width spend an ink budget, and when it is spent the line
      dries.
  - **The control.** `cause: "edges"` applies the same effects at sheet edges
    1, 2, … in layer order, whatever the line meets.
  - **`stages_ink`**:
    - wire everywhere;
    - a line-built broad nib, drawn as parallel lanes joined into one
      boustrophedon path (contiguous, no hatching);
    - beads drawn as `dev_beads` spirals.
  - **`stages_layer`**: the layers, one wire per mark.
  - **The cut.** `run_stages` / `run_stage_sheet` reuse `arrangers_series.cut`,
    so the paper edge splits whatever lies on it.
- **`experiments/stages.py`**: the recipes (`main`, `missed`, `noriver`,
  `control`, `swap`, `hands`). `try NAME [seed]` and `review` rebuild everything.
  Working images are in `.local/gesture/out/stages/`.
- **`tests/test_kit_stages.py`**: 9 tests, all passing. `test_kit.py` passes
  too (41). The tests check:
  - the events come in order;
  - nothing is wet before the first crossing, and no river bends appear before
    the ride;
  - when the red voice is moved, the path is identical and there is no nib and
    no beads;
  - when the river is moved, there are no river bends;
  - the control changes only at edges;
  - a sheet recipe rebuilds its sheet exactly;
  - beads are round;
  - output is the same under PYTHONHASHSEED 1 and 77.

**Frame, chosen.** Five A5 portrait sheets (148 × 210), giving a 740 mm frieze.
Five because there are four causes plus an exit. A5 is narrow enough that the
line must cross an edge every ~15 cm, and each sheet fits the AxiDraw. Sheets
are kept sparse: 250 / 700 / 490 / 990 / 110 mm of line.

Seeds: 5316 (piece), 5344 (variant), and 5311–5320 swept.

## Duds not shown

- **Hand → hand by crossings** (`hands`, ingres → mantis → fly). Reading a hand
  as κ and re-steering it smooths away what made the hand that hand: mantis
  corners and fly loops become generic curves. The change can't be seen.
- **Infection counted by length** (`share: "length"`). The river takes over
  fast and the line tangles. Counting by bends is better: the line's own long
  curves keep coming back, and that makes the middle.
- **The first route** sent 700 mm of line into sheet 1 and never reached sheet 5.
  Route-by-progress steering does not survive a zigzag grammar, so it was
  replaced by waypoints.

## Honest read

**Axis II.**
- The change starts at the ride on sheet 1 and runs left to right.
- The types are: calm hand wire → hand curves with river meanders set into them
  (the middle, its own thing) → river zigzag → zigzag broad nib → bead cluster
  → dry hairline.

**Axis I.** You can point at each cause:
- the double line beside the blue (borrow), then the tangent exit;
- the red hook where the nib begins;
- the line's own crossings where the beads sit;
- the ink running out.

The causes also compound: the river's grammar causes the self-crossings that
make the beads (counterfactual a).

**Weak points.**
- In the piece, the red crossings fall 19–27 mm from the sheet 3|4 edge. At
  row scale the nib can look edge-caused; the counterfactuals are needed to be
  sure. Variant 3 shows the cause more plainly.
- The paper edge is only a cutter here, not a cause.
- Sheet 4 is the densest, and the beads may read as blobby at row scale.
- The nib's turned thick/thin does not show on a tight zigzag. It does show on
  big loops (variant).

**Would Ian keep it?** I think sheets 1–3 of the piece will land: the ride and
the gradual river takeover are what he praised in Metamorphosis 1–4, now with a
visible cause. I'd also expect him to like the variant's calligraphic sheet 4
and counterfactual row a, which works as a drawing in its own right. Sheet 4's
bead cluster is the risk.
