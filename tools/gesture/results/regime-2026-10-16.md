# Regime metamorphosis along a stroke — 2026-10-16

Direction: `docs/IDEAS-gesture-bank.md`, "three regimes that become one
another in space". This thread covers **one stroke changing regime along its
length**: line → nib → shape and back, plus the transition moves Ian pointed
at in his reference image.

Code:
- `gesture/regime.py`: the model, the renderer, the operators. The interface
  for `signs.py` and `regime_field.py` is documented at the top of the file.
- `experiments/regime_study.py`, stages `fit catalogue grammar tree cost`.
- `tests/test_regime.py`: 20 tests in about 4.5 s.

Raw outputs: `.local/gesture/out/regime/` (`fit.json`, `*_costs.json`,
`cost.json`, `ref_overlay.jpg`, `ref_extract.json`).
Sheets: `regime-catalogue.jpg`, `regime-grammar.jpg`, `regime-tree.jpg`.
Pen: 0.35 mm. All units are mm.

## 1. Model

`RegimeStroke` has these fields:
- `points` (N, 2): the centreline.
- `r` (N,): the regime value, in [0, 2].
- `phi` (N,): the nib-edge page angle, using broadnib's convention.
- `W` (N,): the nib edge length.
- `mass` (N,): the swelling diameter at r = 2.
- `w_min`, `closed`, `fill` (an optional (N,) weight) and `meta`.

At each sample the ink footprint is a **stadium**: a segment of half-length
a along the nib edge, dilated by a disc of radius ρ. This is broadnib's
segment ⊕ disc. Its width across the path is 2a·|sin(θ−φ)| + 2ρ.

| r | a | ρ | reads as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | pen/2 | the line, drawn exactly as given |
| 0→1 | smoothstep(r)·W/2 | → max(pen, w_min)/2 | the line thickens under the nib law |
| 1 | W/2 | w_min/2 | broad nib: W·\|sin(θ−φ)\| + w_min |
| 1→2 | W/2 | grows toward mass/2 | the disc outgrows the nib, so width stops depending on direction and the stroke balloons |

Two properties hold, and both are tested:
- Width is **non-decreasing in r** in every direction.
- Width is **C¹ across regime changes**: smoothstep has zero slope at 0, 1
  and 2.

**Rendering.** The pen has a diameter, so the drawn contour is the boundary
of the *pen-centre* region. That region is the same sweep with ρ − pen/2,
built as the union of convex hulls of consecutive stadia (the exact
Minkowski sweep for piecewise-linear motion).
- At r = 0 the stadium is a point, so the sweep *is* the centreline.
- As r rises, a ribbon opens out of the line's end. The renderer splices the
  line run into that outline, so **line → nib is one pen path**.
- A counter whose inradius is below pen/2 is not drawn, because the pen would
  ink it anyway. That is the moment a loop "closes".
- **Fill** is a set of concentric insets every pen width, with a final
  half-step so no white spine is left. It is **graded**: an inset at depth d
  is kept where d ≥ (1 − f)·h. Here f = smoothstep((r − 1.1)/0.4) and h is
  the local half-extent.
- As a result the ink pools from the centre outward and reaches the outline
  only at f = 1. A first version clipped the fill at f ≥ 0.5, which left a
  straight seam across the ribbon where the fill began. That was the most
  "glued-on" artefact. Graded fill removed it.
- Output is plotter polylines only (closed rings repeat their first point):
  no hatching.
- `render([s1, s2, …])` unions the ribbons of a group first. A mass and the
  drips it sheds therefore share one outline.

`from_field(points, field_fn)` samples r = field_fn(x, y) along the points,
clips it, and smooths it at σ = W. A field therefore cannot put a kink into
the width.

## 2. Operators (each returns new strokes and keeps them continuous)

| # | operator | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `thicken(at, ramp, to, until)` | Line → nib (and back) with a smoothstep r ramp. Default ramp length is fitted (§3). |
| 2 | `swell(kind="bead")` | A raised-cosine r bump to 2: a bead on a line, or a nib swelling into a mass and back. |
| 3 | `swell(kind="vessel")` | The stem flares toward the end. The nib turns across the path and lengthens while r rises, giving a goblet or flask. |
| 4 | `close_fill(amount)` | A tangent loop at the end whose r climbs to 2·amount. Amount 0 is a line loop, 0.5 a nib hook, 1 a counter that closes into a filled dot. |
| 5 | `eye` | One thin loop, then an inward spiral that swells to a pupil. Loop and dot are **one** stroke. |
| 6 | `terminal_drop("lozenge")` | Nuqta: a short tangent hook into the nib's thick direction, then a press of about one nib length, filled. |
| 7 | `terminal_drop("drop")` | r → 2 at the end: a teardrop. |
| 8 | `shed("drips" \| "hairs")` | Child strokes rooted *inside* the mass, starting at r ≈ 1.8 and thinning to a line, with optional end drops. Hairs are dry-brush trails past a heavy stroke's end. |

## 3. Profiles from data (`fit`)

**Width ramps** are measured along extracted strokes with σ = 0.75 w. The
ramp is the arc length for width to climb from 25 % to 75 % of the way
between the stroke's q10 and q90 widths, in units of its thick (q90) width.
Only strokes with contrast ≥ 1.6 are counted.

| # | source | strokes / ramps | ramp median (IQR) | contrast q50 | terminal bulb share (end > 1.3× interior) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | shikasta, generative sheets only (wc-12/13/15) | 493 / 644 | **1.46** (1.04–2.37) | 1.78 | 20 % |
| 2 | nasta'liq (measure only) | 1090 / 1364 | 1.22 (0.88–1.90) | 2.14 | 32 % |
| 3 | Baghdadi (measure only) | 282 / 382 | 1.36 (1.02–1.87) | 1.93 | 24 % |
| 4 | **Ian's reference image** | 169 / 202 | **1.49** (1.11–2.88) | 1.89 | 37 % |

1. **Thin becomes thick over about 1.5 of its own thick widths**, in the
   calligraphy and in Ian's image alike. The reference has a longer tail.
   `thicken` reaches 25→75 % in 0.235 of its ramp, so the default is
   **ramp_w = 6.2 W**. `sample_profile` draws from a log-normal fitted
   through the quartiles.
2. **Reference image** (extracted with the calligraphy settings in black-ink
   mode, so the colour areas drop out; overlay in `ref_overlay.jpg`):
   - Thin line w = 5.3 px.
   - Thick strokes are 3.0–4.1× thin (median 3.35) and take 17 % of stroke
     length.
   - 19 masses with inradius > 3 thin widths; diameter median 8.6 thin
     widths, which is **2.6 thick widths**. That is the default `mass_w`.
   - **84 % of masses have ≥ 3 line exits**, so masses sit *on* the line
     network (junctions and crossings), almost never as isolated blobs. This
     is a placement rule for `signs` / `regime_field`.
   - Terminal bulbs are more common than in calligraphy (37 %); tapers are
     rare (6 %).
3. Caveat: the extractor's skeleton ends inside terminal blobs, so end widths
   are under-read. Bulb shares are a lower bound.
4. Policy: the defaults come only from the generative shikasta sheets and
   Ian's own image. The other genres appear only as comparison statistics.
   The nuqta size (≈ 1 nib) is not measured here (†).

## 4. Pen cost per regime (`cost`; pen-down mm per mm of stroke)

| # | W | r = 0 | 0.5 | 1 | 1.5 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.5 | 1.0 | 2.0 | 2.0 | 10 (5–10 paths) | 15–17 (8–12 paths) |
| 2 | 2.5 | 1.0 | 2.0 | 2.1 | 16 | 25–28 (13–19 paths) |
| 3 | 4.0 | 1.0 | 2.0 | 2.1 | 26–28 | 42–45 (20–30 paths) |

**Reading:**
- A nib ribbon costs a flat ×2: two edges, one path.
- A mass costs roughly (mass diameter / pen) per mm, with one pen lift per
  inset ring. Fill dominates every sheet.
- On the grammar sheet, a rise-and-fall line is 1–2 paths and about 170 mm.
  A line passing through a mass is about 13 paths and 630 mm.
- On the trees, the mass part is 26–30 mm of pen per mm of trunk.
- Masses are where the time goes. Any composition should ration them; the
  reference does too, with 17 % thick and 19 masses.

## 5. The sheets, looked at honestly

1. **`regime-catalogue.jpg`**
   - **One mark becoming another** (works):
     - line → nib → line (#1): the ribbon opens out of the line with the
       nib's slanted tip;
     - r rising 0 → 2 (#2): ink pools from the centre with a pointed front,
       then fills to the outline;
     - the **vessel** (#5), which reads as a flask or goblet, the clearest
       metamorphosis;
     - **eye** (#8): a loop holding a pupil, convincingly one gesture, and
       the most "sign-like";
     - **close_fill 1** (#7) and **drop** (#10): a line ending in a dot.
   - **Reads as glued:**
     - Bead (#3) and nib-into-mass (#4) show a white rim between the outline
       and the pooled core. It reads as a swollen outline with ink inside,
       an egg or yolk, rather than a solid bead. Interesting as a sign, not
       as the reference's solid swelling.
     - Drips (#11) are thin double ribbons with tiny end blobs: mechanical
       and too regular.
     - close_fill 0.5 (#6) is a lop-sided hook, weak.
     - The nuqta (#9) is correct but small and polite.
2. **`regime-grammar.jpg`** (v6 Matisse/Hokusai lines + tremor, r(s)
   profiles):
   - "Through mass" with r-scaled mass diameter gives lens-shaped swellings
     that grow out of the hand's line and taper back. This is the closest to
     the reference's swelling strokes.
   - "Pulses" become capsules on a string: too regular, the "beads"
     operator's look.
   - Hokusai's hairpins under a mass make **boot / club / comma shapes**.
     These are the less obvious sign-like forms the direction asks for, and
     they come for free when a mass lands on a corner.
   - Rise-fall alone (r ≤ 1) mostly reads as a **double line**, not a nib
     stroke. At 0.35 mm pen and W ≈ 2 mm, the nib outline is visually
     indistinguishable from the composite's offset returns. A nib ribbon
     needs either W ≥ 3 mm or partial pooling (r ≈ 1.2) to read as a broad
     stroke.
3. **`regime-tree.jpg`** (oehlen_tree; trunk = mass tapering into a ribbon,
   branches = nib, twigs = line, branch roots inherit the host's r):
   - The **left tree's trunk is the best metamorphosis on any sheet**: a
     black cone streams up into a ribbon and then into the branches.
   - Branch roots inheriting the host's r make small ink pools at forks,
     like knots or buds. That is good, and it matches the "masses sit on
     junctions" measurement.
   - The right tree's second trunk enters from the sheet edge as a heavy
     black bar with a pointed fill front: closer to "glued on", because its
     taper runs the wrong way for its visible part.
   - Order-1/2 ribbons again read as doubled lines.
4. **Against the reference:**
   - Present: the reference's swell-and-pool transitions, the
     loop-with-a-dot, and the line-into-mass.
   - Missing:
     - its wet, irregular mass edges;
     - masses that *exceed* the line by a large factor at a point (the
       black ovals);
     - variety: every operator is smooth and symmetric, while the reference
       is lumpy.

Nothing has been verified by Ian's eye or on paper.

## 6. Open problems

1. **Nib vs double line.** At plotter scale, an outline-only nib ribbon is
   ambiguous. Options:
   - a minimum W relative to the pen;
   - a light centre pool at r ≈ 1;
   - option (b) solid fill only where W·|sin| is large.
2. **Mass irregularity.** Mass outlines are perfect sweeps. They need
   low-frequency radial noise (tremor-like, from the reference's mass
   outlines) and asymmetric bulges.
3. **Rim artefact.** The graded fill leaves a white rim inside swellings
   that never reach r = 2. Tie f to the ribbon's absolute width as well as
   to r?
4. **Drips and hairs** are hand-tuned (length 6 W, 4 drips). The reference's
   drips and dry-brush ends could be measured: count and length per mass.
5. **Masses on junctions** (84 % in the reference) is a placement rule. It
   belongs to `regime_field` / `signs`; trees already get it through
   inherited roots.
6. **Cost.** Masses are 15–45× line cost. A cheaper "shape" rendering is an
   open choice: spiral fill as one path instead of ring-per-lift; or outline
   plus 2–3 insets ("hollow mass").
7. Self-overlapping wide ribbons union correctly, but the nib law does not
   model ink pooling at sharp turns. Hokusai corners can produce knots that
   read heavier than intended.
