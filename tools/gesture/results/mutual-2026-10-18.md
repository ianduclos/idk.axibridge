# mutual: four regimes in turns, each one's events cueing another (round 13, thread I-b, 2026-10-18)

**Outcome.** The piece is one sheet of 300 × 200 mm. Four regimes draw it together in interleaved turns:
- a far-view river (blue);
- a seismograph (red);
- a bold hand (black, 1.0 mm);
- a slow hairline (black, 0.25 mm).

Every turn reads the paper. Awareness is a short table of discrete events, not a field.
- The clearest call and response: bold stops in a knot, so the river turns and wraps the knot. When the river arrives, bold rides it from the knot and leaves at a tangent.
- The second time, bold is tired and refuses. It turns its back on the river.
- 16 events in 150 turns.
- The dud turns every relation up and gets 42 events. It is soup, as the brief warned.

## What was built

- `kit/voices_mutual.py`, voice `mutual` (use with the `single` arranger and the `oehlen_found_ink` renderer).
  - **Turn engine.** In each tick the regimes take turns in a fixed order: graph, river, bold, wire.
    - Each regime posts events to a board.
    - Each regime reads only the events it listens to (`RELATIONS`).
    - Every answer, refusal and "ignored" is logged.
    - Every point carries `tick` and `neg` (own / riding).
  - **river.** The frozen GRWL SB19 stem is held in its own valley frame (s, u, n).
    - It is re-laid along a course that the regime steers.
    - On its own, the course keeps the stem's valley bends and its general direction of flow.
    - Called, it turns its course, speeds up, wraps the knot and leaves when it faces downstream.
    - The meander itself is never altered: rebuilt on its own axis it is exact (tested).
  - **graph.** x is the clock. It writes real pre-event seismic noise.
    - On a cue it plays a real arrival: Tōhoku, Maule, Sumatra, Nepal, in that order.
    - It then ignores cues while ringing.
    - While the wire touches it, it lifts its needle, which leaves a gap.
  - **bold** (`dance_thrusting`) and **wire** (`trail_slime`). Every line is a whole line of the hand.
    - Awareness only chooses where a line is placed and turned, when it stops, and what comes next.
    - That was the lesson of my first build. Steering the hand chunk by chunk turned it into notched, MS-Paint-like strokes.
- `experiments/mutual.py`: recipes `v1`–`v4` and `soup`, plus `sweep`.
  - `strip` renders the same run at chosen turns: faded old ink, numbered events, cause > answer arrows.
  - `final` rebuilds the four images: `.local/gesture/.venv/bin/python tools/gesture/experiments/mutual.py final`.
- `tests/test_kit_mutual.py`: 9 tests, about 8 s. They cover:
  - call and response both ways, plus the refusal, asserted as events;
  - few events;
  - the ride sitting beside the river;
  - one needle gap per wire ride;
  - the valley frame being exact;
  - no stray chords in the river;
  - `upto` being a prefix of the same run;
  - determinism across `PYTHONHASHSEED`.
- `tools/gesture/tests/test_kit.py` still passes (41).

**Frame.** 300 × 200 mm is the AxiDraw's whole bed at 3:2.
- The long side is the seismograph's clock, so time runs across the sheet.
- The right third is a reserved blank. Only the wire may enter it.

Seeds 1311–1326 were swept. The piece is v4, seed 1313; the dud is seed 1313 of `soup`.

## What happened (about 60 runs)

1. **v1: hands steered chunk by chunk toward targets.** The bold hand became straight runs with notches, a regime with no character. I rebuilt both hands as whole planned lines; only their placement listens.
2. **The river never arrived.**
   - The edge-avoidance outranked the call, and the stem's own curvature was added unclamped.
   - Fix: a called river lets the edge go, and its turning is bounded by its meander width.
3. **Stray chords in the river.** Nearest-point projection jumped across loop midlines. A monotone s plus both axis components (u, n) made it exact.
4. **Composition.** Everything piled up at the left. Two rules spread it:
   - the river remembers its direction of flow;
   - bold prefers to stop where the river can reach, off the edge (bold reading the river).
5. **Wire riding a seismogram.** An offset along the normals boxed the spikes in. A shifted second print reads as the wire copying the graph.
6. **Refusal.** One-way relations alone looked like obedience.
   - A tired bold (appetite) refuses the second call.
   - The wire's own refusal turns the rest of its line away from bold ink.
   - In the piece the wire refuses twice, at the tall bold stroke.

## Honest read

- **Judging test.** It passes in the piece, with the strip for timing.
  - Event in A, answer in B: bold's knot 1, then the river's ring around it.
  - Event in B, answer in A: the river's arrival, then bold riding it from the knot for 50 mm and leaving at a tangent.
  - Refusal: knot 2 is wrapped too, and bold walks away.
  - Indifference: the river crosses the wire's hairline everywhere.
  - Without the strip, bold turn > arrival and arrival > wire are only legible as timing. The arrival sits at the clock, not at the turn.
- **Would Ian keep it?** Maybe the procedure. Probably not this sheet as a finished drawing.
  - **For:** relation over shape, regimes kept whole, and "the line in front becomes the river" happening because the river came to it. The process strip is the kind of image he liked in conduction.
  - **Against:**
    - The river's wraps read as blue clouds rather than a calm far view.
    - The wire's approach-and-bridge junctions make a few kinks.
    - The right third is empty apart from the trace.
    - The knot-1 detail (knot, bridge, ride) can read as a head in profile with an eye: a figurative risk.
    - The seismograph strip is close to the "systematic structure" he accepts only as an accessory.
- **Strongest next step.** Keep the event table and drop to three regimes (river, bold, wire) so the piece carries the relation without the strip. Or give the river a second stem, so it can answer the third call and bold must choose.
