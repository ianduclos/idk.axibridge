# Sign-like shapes and how they relate to lines — 2026-10-16

Research round on Ian's clarification of the regimes direction
(`docs/IDEAS-gesture-bank.md`): the small forms that "seem to communicate
something" — beads, an eye holding a dot, a nib stroke ending in a lozenge,
a line swelling into a vessel, hooks, commas, a knot that fills in — placed
*in relation to* lines, like diacritics. Code: `gesture/signs.py`
(vocabulary, placement grammar, measurement), `experiments/signs_study.py`
(stages `reference dots fonts grammar catalogue sheets pairs`),
`tests/test_signs.py` (22 tests, about 1 s). Per-run outputs:
`.local/gesture/out/signs/`.

Figures: `signs-catalogue.jpg` (12 families × 5 draws, each with a host
stub), `signs-v6.jpg` (grammar-v6 drawings, learned | random placement),
`signs-tree.jpg` (oehlen_tree, learned | random).

## 1. Inventory of the reference image

`.local/gesture/ref/ian-regimes-sd-2024.png` (1080 × 1920, not committed;
the annotated overlay stays at `.local/gesture/out/signs/reference_overlay.jpg`).
Ink: chroma-suppressing `black` gray (spray colours go to paper), threshold
0.38, open 1 + close 2 px. Line width **w ≈ 6.3 px** (median skeleton width).

**Automated pass** (`signs.inventory`: masses = ink surviving an opening by a
disc of 0.8 w; relation from the number of same-component thin branches
touching the mass, a hole-fill test and the gap to other ink):

| # | relation | count | size / w (median, IQR) | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | junction (≥ 3 branches) | 62 | 2.8 (2.2–5.1) | thick pieces *inside* nib letters, not signs |
| 2 | interior (2 branches) | 24 | 3.1 (2.6–3.9) | swellings / beads on a line |
| 3 | terminal (1 branch) | 6 | 4.8 (2.7–9.6) | drops, blocks at line ends |
| 4 | adjacent (detached, gap ≤ 4 w) | 10 | 5.8 (4.8–6.7) | gap 0.72 w (0.52–1.29) |
| 5 | enclosed | 1 | 1.6 | the eye's pupil, 2.3 w from its loop, off-centre |
| 6 | free | 0 | — | — |
| 7 | large masses (> 12 w) | 7 | — | the big nib/vessel bodies |

The automated pass is noisy on this image: SD spray, grey washes and broken
thin lines fragment, and ~60 % of what it finds are pieces of nib letters.
So the relation mix of the grammar comes from a **reading by eye** of 26
clear forms (`MANUAL_REF` in the study, boxes in px):

| # | relation | n | forms | extent / w |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | terminal | 8 | drops closing lines (×4), nib wedge/lozenge, block hanging on a line end, hook ends, the line swelling into a goblet | drops 3.5–7, block 12, goblet 63 |
| 2 | adjacent | 9 | filled knot with a white slit, small block under it, comma beside a tangle, black oval beside the goblet, ring under the block, big comma/hook, three ticks (comb), ring + hook pair, two specks | 5–21 |
| 3 | enclosed | 3 | eye (loop + off-centre dot), small loop with a dot, crossing loop with a dot | loops 10–17 |
| 4 | interior | 3 | knot filling in on a thin line, beads on the mid lines, bead on the goblet stem | 7–11 |
| 5 | free | 3 | free dot, a lone thick crescent, a negative (white) eye inside the big mass | 2.5–22 |

What the reading adds, beyond counts:

1. **Constellations.** 13 of the 26 forms sit in 4 small *heterogeneous*
   groups: block + ring + comma + comb (bottom right); knot + block; loop +
   dot + drop + specks; goblet + oval + bead. The signs talk to *each
   other* as much as to the lines — a phrase, not a sprinkle.
2. **Sizes are heavy-tailed**: many forms at 3–7 w, a few at 10–20 w.
   Calligraphic dots are 1.3–2.7 w; the reference's signs are bigger.
3. **Terminals continue the line's direction**; adjacent forms sit at a
   small gap (≈ 0.5–1.3 w) and align with or across the local tangent
   (automated: 28° median, broad).
4. Density: 26 forms per 4 560 w of skeleton = **0.0057 signs per w**.

## 2. Placement grammar from calligraphy (policy-gated)

`signs.measure_dots` on ink masks (`calligraphy.ink_mask`, Otsu, rule
removal): dots = compact components (0.35–7 w², solidity ≥ 0.82, extent ≤
4.5 w) within 6 w of a body (≥ 10 w²) and ≥ 0.8 w off the local ink centre
line (small letter pieces on the writing line are excluded). Local baseline
= PCA axis of body ink within 14 w.

**Sources: 11 sheets, all passing `require_generative`** (exercise /
poetry / document): met-716305, met-716307, met-716304, wal-W668-66b,
wal-W668-74b, cma-1983.1115.a, wc-12, wc-13, wc-15 (siyah mashq,
exercise/poetry, petition), wc-05 (diwani document), wc-30 (maghribi
treaty). wal-W672-5b/6a (karalama tangle) and wc-06 (ferman fill
ornament) are allowed but unusable. **299 dots**, of which wc-05 80 and
wc-15 72 dominate.

| # | quantity | value (p10 / p25 / median / p75 / p90) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | clear gap to the host / w | 0.57 / 0.84 / **1.81** / 3.36 / 4.71 |
| 2 | dot size (equiv. diameter) / w | 1.30 / 1.57 / **1.81** / 2.16 / 2.73 |
| 3 | side: above the local baseline | **0.52** (above and below equally) |
| 4 | dot long axis vs baseline (deg) | −77 / −56 / −13 / 33 / 67 (broad; the nib angle is not recoverable at these resolutions) |
| 5 | clustering 1 / 2 / 3 dots | **0.88 / 0.08 / 0.03** (pairs written as one dash count as 1) |
| 6 | pair axis vs baseline (deg) | 7 / 10 / **25** / 45 / 77 (pairs mostly side by side) |
| 7 | pair centre spacing / dot size | 1.17 / 1.23 / **1.41** / 2.24 / 2.56 |
| 8 | along-host position u | 0 / 0.15 / 0.55 / 0.89 / 1 (dots sit over the whole body, ends included) |

**Fonts (shape only, measure-only material):** dots of isolated letters
(neutral string, `neutral_string_ok`) in Amiri / Aref Ruqaa: 11 dots,
Amiri aspect 0.94 (a nuqta is a near-square diamond), Aref Ruqaa 0.34 (the
ruq'a pair-dash). This only sets the lozenge aspect range (0.55–0.95).

**Comparison with the reference.** Calligraphic diacritics are smaller
(1.8 w vs 3–7 w) and sit further off (1.8 w vs ~0.7 w) than the
reference's adjacent forms, and they are single far more often (88 %); the
reference prefers mixed *groups*. Calligraphy gives the *adjacent*
relation's metric (gap, size, side, pair geometry); the reference gives the
relation mix, sizes of the bigger forms and the constellation habit.

**The grammar** (`PlacementGrammar`, `.local/gesture/out/signs/grammar.json`;
fallback deciles in `signs.DEFAULT_GRAMMAR`): relation p = terminal 0.30,
adjacent 0.33, interior / enclosed / free 0.12 each (manual counts + 0.5);
kind given relation is a **design prior** (`KIND_GIVEN_RELATION`, not
learned); adjacent gap, size, side, axis, cluster and pair values are
resampled from the 299 dots; plus three design settings flagged as such:
constellation p 0.6 within 16 w of the previous sign, lognormal size spread
σ 0.4 (non-dot kinds), and density 2 × the measured 0.0057 / w (at the
measured density a v6 drawing carried ~10 signs that read as stray specks).

## 3. Vocabulary (`signs.vocabulary()`, `make_sign`)

All parametric (sizes in w; local frame anchor = origin, +x = host
tangent), never from letterforms or faces. Swelling kinds (drop, comma,
bead, rimless vessel) render through `regime.render` as r = 2 masses with
mass = local diameter (regime.py landed during this round); other fills
are concentric insets at pen spacing; never hatching.

| # | kind | relation(s) | parameters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | lozenge | adjacent, terminal, enclosed | size 2.2–4.2, aspect 0.55–0.95, skew, rounding, nib angle (sampled from the dots' axis) |
| 2 | drop | terminal, adjacent, free | size 2.5–5.5, tail 1.2–2.6, bend |
| 3 | comma | adjacent, terminal | head 2.5–5, tail 2.5–5.5, curl (rising curvature) |
| 4 | bead | interior | size 1.8–3.4, length 2.5–5, 1–4 beads, spacing |
| 5 | eye | adjacent, free | almond loop 5–10 long, height 0.45–0.8, dot 0.7–1.4 off-centre, wobble |
| 6 | vessel | terminal | length 6–14, belly 2–4.5, neck, concave rim |
| 7 | knot | interior, free | one curl (prolate trochoid), loop partly or wholly filled |
| 8 | crescent | adjacent | size 4–8, thickness, cupped toward / away from the line |
| 9 | stack | adjacent | 2–3 lozenges: pair (axis from the learned pair angle) / triangle / row |
| 10 | tick | terminal, adjacent | 1–3 parallel strokes, angle to the tangent 50–125° |
| 11 | pendant | terminal | irregular rounded block 4–10 on a stem |
| 12 | ring | adjacent, free | size 4–9, open gap 0–0.9 rad |

**Reader-check notes** (policy E3, by my eye — not a substitute for a
reader of Arabic/Persian): the **comma** with a curling tail can read as a
hamza (ء) or a "9"; a **stack** of three dots in a triangle over a curve
evokes the ش/ث dot clusters; **ring + tick** combinations can read as
Latin "o"/"e"/"≡". No generated sheet shows a word. A single **eye** reads as
an eye — intended (Ian's "eye-like loop holding a dot"); `place_signs`
never puts two eyes side by side, but a constellation can still drift
toward a face (eye + comma under it) — not seen in these sheets, not
guarded.

## 5. Honest reading of the sheets

1. **Attached relations carry the effect.** Where the grammar threads beads
   on a line, ends a line in a block, a drop or a comb of ticks, the marks
   clearly belong — the random panel's same shapes float as debris. A
   string of beads moved off its line reads as a caterpillar; on the line
   it reads as the line *doing* something. This matches the reference,
   where most communicative forms are attached or nearly so.
2. **Adjacent dots are too quiet.** Calligraphic diacritics at 1.8 w are
   specks at sheet scale; a dot 1.8 w above a line and a dot at a random
   free place look alike in a dense drawing (random places in a dense v6
   drawing are near some line anyway). The reference's adjacent forms are
   bigger and closer (3–7 w at ~0.7 w).
3. **Still decoration more than speech.** Even with constellations, the
   sheets read as a line drawing *with* marks, not as the reference's mixed
   field where marks and lines are one script. The reference's forms grow
   out of the line's own regime (the goblet is the line, the knot is the
   line filling in); here most signs are separate objects placed next to
   the line. The fix is upstream: signs should be regime transitions of
   the line (`regime.terminal_drop`, `swell`, `close_fill`, `eye`), with
   this grammar deciding *where*, not just stamps.
4. **Trees**: on oehlen_tree the free ends are twig tips, so terminals
   collect there (combs and drops at tips read like buds — the best moment
   on that sheet); the big empty cells get adjacent/free signs that look
   like confetti.
5. Vocabulary quality: comma, pendant, bead strings, tick combs and the eye
   are convincing; the knot reads as a small filled bulb rising off the
   line (a knot "filling in", though not yet a crossing knot); the vessel
   reads more like an arrowhead / claw than a goblet; the lozenge and crescent are small at catalogue scale; small
   white specks inside some regime-rendered drops are rendering artefacts.

## 6. Policy compliance

- `learn_grammar` calls `calligraphy.require_generative` on every dot
  source before learning; `stage_dots` and the calibration crops gate their
  sheet lists before reading a pixel. Tests: Qur'anic, devotional
  (verify), the flagged Baghdadi book, album pages and unknown ids all
  raise `PolicyError`; the study's sheet list passes the real manifest.
- Fonts contribute dot *shape* only (aspect), from neutral isolated
  letters through `shape_text`'s `neutral_string_ok` check.
- Generation uses no letterform: every sign is a parametric shape. Calligraphy
  crops appear only in the judge's calibration pairs (in `.local/`), as
  research documentation.

## 7. Open problems

1. **Signs as transitions, not stamps** (§5.3): drive `regime.py`'s
   operators from `place_signs` decisions so a terminal sign *is* the
   line's end swelling/closing.
2. Adjacent scale: fit the adjacent gap/size to the reference (bigger,
   closer) and keep the calligraphy pair/cluster geometry; or make it a
   per-zone choice (script-like zones use calligraphy metrics).
3. Constellations are a single "near the previous sign" rule; the
   reference's groups mix kinds deliberately (block + ring + comma + comb).
   A small kind-to-kind transition table would say more.
4. Better reference inventory: a manual annotation of all forms (not 26),
   or a cleaner reference; the automated pass is dominated by nib letters.
5. Dot recall in dense nasta'liq exercise sheets is low (dots merge with
   bodies); the nib angle of dots is not recoverable at 2000 px.
6. No automated letter/face detector; the E3 reader check stays required
   before any public showing.

## Blind judge on the placement pairs (run by the lead after this report)

Sonnet judge, images only, key outside the repo; 19 pairs. Question: in
which panel do the small shapes seem to *belong* to the lines and say
something?

| # | condition | judge picks the learned placement | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | test: grammar vs random placement on v6 lines / trees | **11/12** (confidence 3–4) | .006 |
| 2 | calibration: real calligraphy dots vs displaced | 5/7, all at confidence 1 | .45 |

- The learned placement reads as belonging, decisively. The judge's
  cues: marks touching or ending a line, bead chains following a line,
  and random specks reading as scattered. That is exactly the "attached"
  relation the sheets showed carrying the effect. The judge flagged that
  this might be a layout artefact; it is in fact the design.
- The calibration is uninformative rather than failed: the displaced-dot
  crops look nearly identical at that scale, so the judge guessed.
