## Instrument and attempts

The script grows one whole learned hand with the frozen kit, resamples it to
3,600 equal-arc points, and splits its motion into two instructions. A slow
version moves a pivot. The source's unwrapped heading rotates a telescopic
arm. Its radius changes with smoothed absolute turning activity. Only the arm
end draws. Heading changes pass through a gear ratio; this is a synthetic
geometric mechanism, not a claim about insect anatomy or physical dynamics.

The finished piece uses `trail_miner`: the library records six extracted
strokes and 76 records from two leaf-miner images, CC BY-SA 4.0. These are
learned turning statistics, not copied galleries. The little blunt steps
and reversals survive the new mechanism, while the large spaces change.
The script uses the library policy gate; no calligraphy or print source enters
generation. I do not see legible text or a specifically Arabic appearance in
the chosen drawing. This is my visual reading, not a human language review.

I made 48 candidate renders, including repeated controls: 12 fly seeds with the initial long arm;
12 hand/arm-side tests; 12 hand/gear tests; 12 leaf-miner seeds. I also tested
two rotations, inspected isolated drivers and made the final framing pass.
All working images remain under `.local/gesture/out/outsider/`.

The initial fly series was the dud: the arm magnified every turn into a
similar circle. More crossings did not give those circles a reason to matter.
Reducing the gear to 0.37 and using the leaf-miner hand produced broader,
unequal spaces, with fewer decorative returns. Many seeds still looked like
generic cut-paper animals or fluent ornaments; I did not treat that as proof
of a successful new grammar. There is no kit registration or library addition.

## Images, in review order

1. **`outsider-01-finished.jpg` — The unfinished rooms.**
   180 × 205 mm portrait, black 0.5 mm pen; one continuous open polyline,
   3,600 points and 723.23 mm of pen travel; no fills, hatching or simulated
   width changes. The two attempted enclosures
   share a narrow passage without becoming a neatly joined symbol. I would
   protect the blunt little step halfway down and the uneven scale of the
   upper loop and lower pointed return. The portrait puts these incidents
   above and below one another without making a readable row. Its generous

2. **`outsider-02-process.jpg` — driver / pivot / drawn end.**
   Left to right: the isolated generated leaf-miner driver; the slow pivot;
   the final endpoint trace. Each cell is fitted independently so this is a
   shape comparison, not a common-scale trajectory measurement. Three
   continuous pen paths, no connecting lines between panels. The right panel
   reproduces the finished geometry, including its physical pen width.

3. **`outsider-03-dud.jpg` — the arm becomes a compass.**
   The initial fly-derived failure, seed 19331, unreduced rotation. One
   continuous 3.47 m line. Its circular repetition is the problem. I am not
   presenting the dense part as a mass or a fill technique.

4. **`outsider-04-alternative.jpg` — a more occupied vacancy.**
   The river-derived alternative, seed 21713, gear 0.37. Its enclosed space
   changes when followed around the crossing blades. It is an honest
   competing choice, with more interaction and less quiet than the finished

## Second eye and honest read

A bounded Sol (`gpt-5.6-sol`) second eye inspected the whole neutral-labelled
hand/gear population and close views before knowing the sources or my choice.
It found the repeated circles weak, preferred the river alternative's shaped
central vacancy, and questioned whether the portrait's separated upper and
lower episodes were consequential or simply insufficiently related. I retain
that disagreement: the interruption is why I chose the portrait.

Sol also compared two rotations. One became readily animal-like, the other
resembled an ornamental heading. We both preferred the portrait's greater
uncertainty. After source reveal it reviewed the entire 12-seed leaf-miner
population and the isolated driver. It found substantial changes of spatial
organization across seeds, alongside failures that settle into quadrupeds,
biomorphic emblems or knots. The process strip changed its reading of the
selected piece: the endpoint retains the driver's two-zone disposition but
turns closure into two incompatible attempts to enclose. It supported the
portrait selection while retaining its preference for the river piece as
the richer isolated drawing. Its strongest caution was to preserve the tiny
upper loop, open inner arc and blunt lower notch rather than smoothing them.

I stand behind image 1 in its chosen frame. It has an awkward interval I want
to return to, rather than an impressive accumulation of events. Ian might
keep it as a spare, pointed element: the pen can be followed through several
changes of role and the empty spaces have different shapes. He might reject
it because the cut-paper / unknown-glyph associations arrive too quickly, or
because the two halves never quite need each other. Both objections would
be fair. I agree with the brief's taste direction; my disagreement is with
Sol's choice of the more elaborately occupied alternative. This is a selected
piece, not evidence that every seed deserves keeping. Paper behaviour remains
unverified.
