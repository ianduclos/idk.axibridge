# Round 14 b1 `cproc`: composer as procedure (2026-10-18)

**Outcome.** One arranger, `cproc`, decides a whole composition from the seed and then plays the kit's praised
techniques into it. Twelve consecutive seeds give twelve sheets that differ in frame, density, core, blank,
dominant register, layer order and reversal. They do not read as one layout with a varying line. They do share
a vocabulary (black hand knots, a coloured found pen, a heavy pen), which is what a series keeps. The weakest
point: the dense sheets resemble each other more than the sparse ones do.

## What it is

- Code: `tools/gesture/gesture/kit/arrangers_cproc.py`. It registers the arranger `cproc`, the renderer
  `cproc_ink` (wire at each mark's pen and ink; limb signs as a nib) and the placeholder voice `cproc_score`.
- Use: `cp.recipe(seed)` decides the frame on purpose and returns a recipe. `kit.run(recipe)` decides the rest
  from `ctx.rng("plan")` and executes it. The decisions are in `marks[0].meta["cproc"]`.
- Script: `tools/gesture/experiments/cproc.py` (`one`, `pop`, `final`). Tests: `tests/test_kit_cproc.py` (13 pass;
  `test_kit.py` 41 pass).
- Runtime: 0.6 to 19 s a sheet. The 12-sheet population renders in about 80 s.

## The procedure, per sheet

1. **Frame**: one of seven, chosen on purpose and named in the recipe's `notes`. They are strip 300×80,
   landscape 300×200, square 210×200, portrait 200×290, small 150×105, column 105×290 and band 300×140. All fit
   the AxiDraw bed.
2. **Density dial**: drawn from a U-shaped Beta(0.45, 0.45), so the extremes come up often. Labels: sparse
   (< 0.12), open, dense, all-over (> 0.88).
3. **Core and blank**: an off-centre core, sometimes a smaller counterweight core. The blank kind is side,
   corner, band (density on both sides), island or none.
4. **Scale ladder**: small, mid and large rungs, plus a spread dial. Each hand line takes a rung: a small knot,
   a middle hand, or a large fast sweep.
5. **Dominant register**: found, structure, traverse, hand or soup. The dominant one gets size and weight, and
   the others get fewer lines.
6. **Acts in order**: found, then structure, then hands, then heavy. A per-sheet disorder dial perturbs that
   habit. Found matter that arrives after the hands is pasted over them and cuts them. A reversal comes after at
   least 60% of the acts, and a crossing line follows it.

The players, each running the existing technique:

| Role | Players |
|---|---|
| Found | `oehlen_found_river` at a per-sheet zoom (span 260–3200 widths), `river_braid`, `river_loops`, `diagram` (kappa or phase), `trail_sheet` |
| Structure | handover specimen rows (Cohen key, bowed key, coiled, waves) at their own proportion; a chart |
| Hands | `negotiate` (ride, grow, ignore), `catch` (`contagion_walk` carrying river, chart, specimen or an earlier line's hand), `answer` (grows out of the last line's end), `passage`, the free hand of accrue seed 3966 quoted whole at any scale and angle, `limb` (limb signs), `soup` (`mutual`) |
| Traverse | `belt` with gain morphing, `passage` (one row), `waves`, `arc` (one hand at sheet scale), or a river strip, laid along a spine through the core and the blank |
| Heavy | a re-ink of a stretch at the core's edge (1.3–2.4 mm), a heavy gesture, or a heavy limb |
| Reversal | a cut cover, a tint, a torn band (erasure) or a pasted patch of found matter, followed by a line that crosses it (it may ride the cover's edge) |

Rules' R1 is a per-sheet dial (`weight_follows_ground`): a line is heavy while it rides the ground and a
hairline elsewhere.

## The three principles and where they land

- **T1**, "Density is a dial set per sheet, and the dial has extremes." The population runs from 0.01 (60101,
  a few lines on the full bed) and 0.016 (56069, one small knot high in a 290 mm column) to 0.967 (53039).
  The blank is decided per sheet: an island in 50011 and 55057, a band in 52027, a corner in 60101 and none in
  the all-over sheets.
- **T2**, "the long traverse … passes through the core and the blank alike, and it belongs to neither." The
  traverse is a strip laid along a spine that runs from behind the core, through it, through the blank, to an
  edge. Two examples: the belt in 55057 (gain 2.19 → 0.69), and in 60101 an arc plus a passage row
  56 mm tall. Three rungs at once show in 50011: specimen rows at 12 mm, middle hokusai knots, and an arc and
  large sweeps at 275 mm.
- **T5**, "at least one late act cuts back into what is already there … then a line crosses the cover." Every
  sheet has a reversal followed by a crossing, and the crossing crossed in 12 of 12. Kinds and places vary:
  - a band at the blank's edge cuts 890 mm of line in 55057;
  - a pasted chart cuts 221 mm of hands in 50011, which is found matter arriving late;
  - a tint recolours the core red in 60101.

## The dud, and what it taught

59093 is a small sheet with structure dominant. Six specimen rows were meant to carry it. Fly circles, a
passage and a braid outvote them, and the sheet becomes an even tangle with no core, no blank and no contrast.
An earlier build, now fixed, packed the specimen grammar into 150 mm rows at 297 mm settings. That made black
bricks that read as hatching. The specimens now scale R and band with the row width.

The lesson: on a small sheet, dominance has to take weight and size away from the other registers, not only
lines. Density without a scale contrast is not the soup Ian liked; it is grey.

Two other fixes came from working images:
- A chart turned 90° read as hatching, so charts now turn at most ±15°. The tempo chart is dropped as found
  matter for the same reason.
- Drawn cover edges in 40% of sheets became a signature, so they are now 30%, and never on a band.

## Honest read

- **Different every time: mostly yes.** Frame, density, blank and dominant register change the sheet at a
  glance. Look at 60101 next to 53039, 56069 next to 51013, and 52027 next to 61109. The order and reversal vary
  per sheet.
- **Sameness that remains:**
  - The middle register is nearly always black hand knots fitted into rectangles. In the dense sheets
    (50011, 52027, 53039, 55057) that register sets the look, so those four feel like siblings.
  - Several portrait frames came up in this run.
  - The heavy intruder is a curved gesture, never geometric. T3 belongs to another thread, and I left it alone.
- **Dynamics:**
  - Weight contrast is real (0.25 mm hairlines against 2.4 mm), and so is scale contrast in the open and
    sparse sheets.
  - Speed contrast comes mostly from the chart ruling, the large fast sweeps and the arcs.
  - The densest sheets have the least internal contrast.
- **Would Ian keep it?**
  - I think he would keep 50011 and 55057 as drawings. They carry several relations at once (found pasted over
    hands, a caught seismograph, lines that ride and answer), and that is what he rewarded in round 13.
  - 60101 is the sparse extreme. It may read as too little to him.
  - I expect him to keep the procedure as a way of working rather than every sheet.
  - The risk is that he sees the dense half as one style.
- **What I cut for runtime:** nothing that changes a mechanism. Specimen rows run their own route without a
  length cap. Passage is capped at 400–2500 mm, and mutual runs 90–160 ticks.
