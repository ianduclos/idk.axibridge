# Tremor layer — 2026-10-16

Question: the visual judge's cue #2 was tremor and wavering in real lines,
which the generators lack. The grammar works at σ = 2 line widths (w) and
cannot express anything finer. Is that fine-scale character real, or is it
extraction noise? Can a residual layer model it and add it to any smooth
drawing?

Code: `gesture/tremor.py`, `experiments/tremor_study.py` (stages `floor stats
model eval pairs sheets`), `tests/test_tremor.py`. Raw outputs are in
`.local/gesture/out/tremor/` (`stats.json`, `models/*.json`, `eval.json`,
`sheets/`).

## Measurement

- **Residual.** Each stroke is resampled at 0.25 w and smoothed twice:
  1. at 0.3 w, giving P;
  2. at 2 w **with Tukey twicing**, giving the centreline C.

  r(s) = (P − C)·n / w is the lateral offset in line widths.
- **Why twicing.** A plain Gaussian pulls a curve toward its centre by
  κσ²/2. At σ = 2–3 w that bias was as large as the tremor itself, and the
  first measurement only showed curvature. Twicing removes it; a circle now
  measures < 0.01 w.
- **Corners are excluded.** Points with |κ_C|·w > 0.18 are dropped, dilated
  by 3 w.
- **What the band contains.** A σ = 2 w residual contains wavelengths λ ≲
  10 w. Most of its energy is at λ ≈ 3–10 w. The extractor's own 1-px
  smoothing (≈ 0.25–0.4 w) removes everything below λ ≈ 1.5 w: the
  0.3–1 w scale is **not recoverable** from these scans.
- **The scale is right.** A wider band (σ = 8 w) shows that v4 already
  matches real energy at λ 8–16 w (0.12 vs 0.12 for Matisse). The gap is
  only below ≈ 8 w, which is the complement of the grammar.
- **Noise floor**, per scan (45 scans):
  1. Measure the scan's high-pass paper noise ÷ ink contrast.
  2. Take its own long strokes, smoothed at 4 w, as known-smooth ground
     truth.
  3. Render them at the scan's working resolution, with its widths and the
     matched paper noise (`synth.render`).
  4. Re-extract them with `extract_hybrid`.
  5. Measure the residual the same way as on the real strokes.

  The ground truth's own leakage into the band is 0.011–0.016 w and is part
  of the floor. Matisse lithographs also got a "grain" render, with ragged
  crayon edges.

## 1. Noise floor vs real tremor

Residual rms in line widths, corner-free, long strokes.

| # | group | scans | w (working px) | noise ÷ contrast | real rms | floor rms | real ÷ floor | excess rms | corr. length real / floor (w) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Matisse (all) | 25 | 3.3 | 0.006 | **0.048** | 0.015 | 3.3 | 0.046 | 0.95 / 1.44 |
| 2 | Matisse etching | 15 | 2.6 | 0.008 | 0.043 | 0.015 | 2.8 | 0.040 | 1.01 / 1.44 |
| 3 | Matisse lithograph | 10 | 4.2 | 0.006 | 0.067 | 0.013 (grain 0.022) | 5.1 | 0.065 | 0.86 / 1.45 |
| 4 | Hokusai (brush) | 10 | 7.1 | 0.045 | **0.071** | 0.022 | 3.2 | 0.067 | 0.91 / 1.23 |
| 5 | Ingres (graphite) | 10 | 3.5 | 0.052 | **0.071** | 0.017 | 4.2 | 0.069 | 0.93 / 1.33 |

1. **Real tremor is 3–5× the extraction floor in every group, including
   Matisse's etched line.** It is not extraction noise. The floor differs in
   kind too: it is spiky (kurtosis 46–71: rare glitches at crossings),
   while real tremor is a continuous wobble (kurtosis 5–9).
2. **Shape is shared across hands.** Correlation length is ≈ 0.9 w
   everywhere, with energy concentrated at λ ≈ 4–8 w.
3. **Amplitude rises with curvature.** By |κ|·w bin, real rms goes 0.03 →
   0.10 (Matisse) and 0.05 → 0.14 (Hokusai, Ingres). The floor also rises,
   but less: 0.01 → 0.05.
4. **Ends wobble more.** In the first 3 w, rms is 1.2–1.5× the interior
   for Matisse; flatter for the others.
5. **Longer strokes are slightly smoother.** Spearman ρ between stroke
   length and rms is −0.27 for Matisse, −0.14 for Ingres and ≈ 0 for
   Hokusai.
6. **In absolute terms the tremor is tiny:** 0.05–0.07 w rms measured.
   That is about 0.2 px at the 1200-px judge renders.

## 2. Medium check (within Matisse)

- **Raw numbers.** Lithographs measure 0.067 against 0.043 for etchings,
  1.5×. The grain bracket explains little of that gap: ragged edges add only
  ≈ 0.018 w to the floor.
- **Resolution confounds the raw comparison.** Etchings are thinner at
  working resolution (2.6 px vs 4.2 px per line), and the extractor's 1-px
  smoothing attenuates them more.
- **At matched resolution the gap shrinks.** Over sheets with w = 2.6–4.3
  px, the medians are 0.053 (etching, n = 8) and 0.064 (lithograph, n = 6),
  about **1.2×**. Etching sheets spread widely (0.03–0.09).
- **Reading:** tremor is mostly hand-or-scale. Lithographic crayon adds a
  modest amount, and that excess is medium, not hand. Hokusai and Ingres sit
  at ≈ 0.07, which is Matisse-lithograph level.
- **No within-hand pair exists for brush or graphite,** so whether 0.07 is
  "hand" or "soft medium" cannot be separated for them.

## 3. Model (`TremorModel`)

- **Process.** Per artist, a stationary Gaussian process on the lateral
  offset:
  1. take the Welch PSD of real residuals over corner-free runs (16-w
     segments);
  2. subtract the floor PSD;
  3. **deconvolve the measurement band-pass** H(f) = G_lo − twice(G_hi),
     with power gain capped at 5.
- **Envelope.**
  - Gain per |κ|·w bin: 0.6 → 2.1–2.5.
  - Gain per distance-from-end bin: Matisse 1.35 at the ends, others
    ≈ 1.0–1.2.
  - Per-stroke log-normal gain: sd 0.29 (Ingres), 0.42 (Hokusai), 0.50
    (Matisse).
- **Calibration.** A round-trip calibration on the artist's own sheets,
  with their tremor removed (`detremor`) and modelled tremor added, matches
  the measured rms. The correction factor was ×0.93–1.16.
- **Synthesis** is FFT filtering of white noise. Nothing is resampled from
  real strokes, so nothing can be memorised.
- **Result** (displacement rms / q99, in w):

  | artist | rms (w) | q99 (w) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Matisse | 0.063 | 0.16 |
  | Hokusai | 0.112 | 0.29 |
  | Ingres | 0.119 | 0.31 |

  Separate models were also saved for Matisse etching and Matisse
  lithograph: 0.061 and 0.105.
- **`apply_tremor(drawing, model, rng, amount)`.**
  - Adds the offset along the 1-w-smoothed normal.
  - Pins open ends exactly, with a 1-w smoothstep.
  - Re-snaps every end that touched another stroke within 2 w: the end
    follows the host's displacement, ramped over 3 w.
  - Closed strokes get a periodic residual.
  - amount = 0 is the identity.
- **Tests** (9 tests, < 10 s): determinism, purity, amount = 0 identity,
  pinned ends, T-junction contact kept, amplitude linear in amount,
  `detremor` removes ≥ 65 % of the residual, JSON round trip, and AR(2)
  statistics recovered through fit → apply → re-measure (rms and
  correlation length within 20 %).

## 4. Evaluation

Real long-stroke drawings (31 / 27 / 34) were compared with 16 v4 drawings
per artist, without and with tremor at amount 1.0. AUC is 5×5-fold logistic
regression; the per-feature column is mean |AUC − 0.5|.

| # | artist | feature set | real vs v4 | real vs v4+tremor | v4 vs v4+tremor | per-feature real/v4 → real/v4+t |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Matisse | tremor stats (9) | 1.00 | 0.93 | 1.00 | 0.50 → 0.20 |
| 2 | Hokusai | tremor stats | 1.00 | 0.92 | 1.00 | 0.50 → 0.15 |
| 3 | Ingres | tremor stats | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.49 → 0.23 |
| 4 | Matisse | panel σ = 0.75 w (40, no cl_/w_/end_) | 1.00 | 0.97 | 1.00 | 0.26 → 0.13 |
| 5 | Hokusai | panel σ = 0.75 w | 1.00 | 0.99 | 1.00 | 0.28 → 0.14 |
| 6 | Ingres | panel σ = 0.75 w | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.26 → 0.14 |
| 7 | Matisse | panel σ ≥ 7 w | 0.80 | 0.84 | 0.09* | 0.092 → 0.095 |
| 8 | Hokusai | panel σ ≥ 7 w | 0.82 | 0.82 | 0.34* | 0.105 → 0.109 |
| 9 | Ingres | panel σ ≥ 7 w | 0.87 | 0.92 | 0.25* | 0.123 → 0.129 |

\* below 0.5 means CV on indistinguishable pairs. v4 vs v4+tremor at 7 w
has a per-feature mean |AUC − 0.5| of 0.02–0.04.

1. **Without tremor, v4 is trivially separable at fine scale.** Every
   tremor statistic is 1.00. At σ = 0.75 w, v4 has about 10× fewer
   curvature extrema and inflections than real: Matisse `ext_rate` is 24
   against 279.
2. **Tremor closes most of the per-feature gap.**
   - `ext_rate`, `infl_rate`, `ext_turn_q50`, `ext_w_q50`, rms, q90 and
     the band powers land at real medians (per-feature AUC 0.3–0.6).
   - Correlation length is 0.88 against 0.91–0.94 real; the model is
     slightly too fine.
   - Some features remain off: `ext_spacing_cv` (extrema are too regular)
     and `k_cv` for Matisse.
   - Multivariate, fine-scale real vs v4+tremor is still 0.92–1.00
     separable. The layer matches marginals, not the joint structure; real
     wobble is less stationary than a Gaussian process.
3. **The 7 w standard results are not broken.** v4 and v4+tremor are
   indistinguishable at σ ≥ 7 w. Real vs v4 moves by +0.00 to +0.05, which
   is within the CV noise at n = 16. This run used no INLP and no `cl_`
   group, to save CPU.

## 5. Sheets

Files: `tremor-hokusai.jpg` and `tremor-matisse.jpg`. Ingres is in `out/`.

Each sheet has five columns: real, v4, then v4 + tremor at 0.5, 1.0 and
3.0. The top row shows each whole drawing; the bottom row shows a 1:1
detail, rendered at 1200 px with 1.5-px lines and 1-px smoothing.

1. **At 0.5 the tremor is invisible** at sheet scale.
2. **At 1.0 there is a faint, even wiriness.** It shows in the detail on
   long contours and loops: Hokusai's loop gets a slightly knobbly outline.
   It reads as "less vector-perfect", not as hand.
3. **The real lines look different.** Hokusai's brush contour wavers at
   longer wavelength (≈ 5–20 w) with occasional kinks. Matisse's etched
   contours wobble gently and unevenly.
4. **Model tremor is too uniform.** It wobbles everywhere, at the same rate.
5. **At 3.0 it becomes a crinkled, coastline texture,** clearly a filter.
   This is a useful awkward look, but not the real look.

Honest summary: amount 1.0 is statistically right and visually subtle. Real
tremor's character is in its *intermittency*: bursts and calm stretches, and
kinks. A stationary process does not have that.

## 7. Recommendation

1. **Keep the layer as an optional post-pass.** It is cheap, safe for
   structure (ends and contacts held, 7 w panel unchanged) and moves the
   fine-scale marginals to real.
2. **Don't expect it to move the judge.** At honest amplitude it is near
   the visibility limit.
3. **Next, if pursued:** an intermittent model. Options are a regime-switch
   or Markov-modulated amplitude (calm ↔ shaky), or a heavy-tailed kink
   process. Fit it to the real residual's local-rms autocorrelation, which
   is not yet measured. Also lengthen the band to ≈ 4 w, where the sheets
   suggest the visible wavering lives.
4. **Plotter.** These figures assume a 1200-px canvas plotted at 200 mm,
   with the hand's line width mapped to plot scale: w ≈ 0.40 mm (Matisse),
   0.75 mm (Hokusai) and 0.36 mm (Ingres).

   | artist | rms (mm) | q99 (mm) |
   |---|---|---|
   | Matisse | 0.025 | 0.065 |
   | Hokusai | 0.084 | 0.22 |
   | Ingres | 0.043 | 0.11 |

   Only Hokusai is clearly above the AxiDraw's ≈ 0.1 mm mechanical jitter.
   Matisse and Ingres tremor at true amplitude is **below the machine's own
   noise**; the plotter will supply comparable wobble for free. On paper,
   amount ≈ 2–3 is the minimum for the layer to be a deliberate,
   visible choice. Plotting larger, or with a pen much thinner than the
   modelled w, raises this.
