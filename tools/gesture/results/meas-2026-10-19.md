# Round 15, thread D: process-correlated measuring devices (`meas`, 2026-10-19)

## Outcome

Seven devices in the `process` family. Each one reads something that really happened in a drawing: the pen's order, who drew each line, the source's own clock, and the relations the score (or the arranger's log) records. None of them draws a rule that measures nothing. They all lay on any finished sheet through `apply_devices`.

The two strongest are `meas_air` (the pen's flight between lines) and `meas_stations` (river km along a river_zoom line). `meas_inventory` is the round-10 numbered atlas taken to sheet scale. `meas_turning` is the new version of the κ chart. `meas_ledger` and `meas_arcs` are true, but they read as tables. Use them beside a drawing, never on it. The weak one is `meas_roll` (see Duds).

Round-14 sheets carry no score, so `process_score` builds one from the arranger's own log. On 4507 that is the ccolour events (carry, spill). On 14007 it is the cimpro moves (thicken, cover, cross, counterweight, trail). Every derived score is marked `"derived"`. A recorded `notes["score"]` always wins.

## The catalogue

| # | device | reads | key params | sits best |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `meas_stations` | the source's clock on the line: `u_km` (river_zoom) or `dance_t` (dance). It ticks round values across the line; they crowd where the zoom is far. Beside, it lays the same line out straight as a scale bar whose scale changes, with drawn mm, km of river and the zoom range. It never invents a clock: no channel, no ink. | `clock` auto/km/seconds, `stations`, `label_every`, `tick`, `on_line` | beside bottom of a river_zoom strip; over a dance (the ticks show the limb's speed) |
| 2 | `meas_roll` | the drawing as a piano roll. x is the pen's order, each line as wide as the ink it laid; there is one lane per corpus family (or hand). Each note is the line itself, turned to its chord and stretched. | `by` line/step, `lanes` family/hand, `shape` stretched/whole, `gap` | beside bottom, 35–45 mm |
| 3 | `meas_inventory` | the sheet taken apart into its steps (ccolour object, cimpro move, score layer, part), in drawing order, at one common scale. Each item is numbered and named by verb and/or hand. A ring with the same number marks the pen's first touch on the drawing. Items shrunk below the common scale say so (`/2`). | `by` step/line, `name` hand/role/both, `scale`, `index` | beside right, 80–120 mm |
| 4 | `meas_air` | the pen-up route from each line's end to the next line's start: straight hairlines, rings at pen-downs, numbered with the same numbers as the inventory and ledger. Beside, it draws the route alone, as the drawing's negative, with the total air distance and the number of lifts. | `level` ink/line/step, `min_gap`, `ring`, `number_every`, `ink` (red) | over, at step level |
| 5 | `meas_arcs` | the score's relations. Beside: an arc diagram, with layers in laid order and each relation an arc from actor to acted-on, its kind at the crown. Over: each relation where it happened, as a ring at `at` and a hairline running 1.4 mm beside the stretch `s` it names. | `style` auto/arcs/places, `kinds`, `hug`, `ink` | beside bottom; `places` over, filtered to 2–3 kinds |
| 6 | `meas_ledger` | the books of the sheet's making: one row per step. Columns: what it did, who drew it, from which corpus, ink mm (plus a bar in the step's own ink), pen range. Matisse draws the inner rules; the frame and the lettering stay straight (Ian's ledger note, round 11). With `index`, the row numbers are ringed on the drawing. | `by`, `drawn_by`, `straight`, `index`, `bars` | beside right, 100–120 mm |
| 7 | `meas_turning` | a new κ version. Every line starts from one origin and plots its accumulated whole turns against arc length, each in its own ink, the longest named with their final count. A river wanders on the axis, a fly coils steadily, a stair steps, a bead shoots up. Found charts are left out by default. | `min_len`, `max_lines`, `name`, `exclude`, `x` mm/share, `smooth` | beside bottom, 60–70 mm |

Shared reader: `kit/devices_meas_read.py` provides `units(sheet, by)`, `process_score`, `layer_marks` and `family`. Units come in the pen's order. Their hands come from the `voice` channel weighted by arc length, the ccolour plan, the library ids the cimpro log names, river_zoom's stem, or the recipe part, in that order.

Test bed: the `meas_boxes` arranger puts parts in boxes and records a score of what the geometry did, `cross` (with `at` and `s`) or `near`. Its relations are measured, not composed. The `meas_ink` renderer draws one named ink per part at constant width.

Specs beside each image (`.recipe.json`) hold the drawing's full recipe plus the device specs. `experiments/meas_review.py` rebuilds all five; `experiments/meas_try.py` lays any spec on any test drawing.

## Duds

- `meas_roll`. It is true, but the stretched notes of closed or looping lines turn to scribble. On 14007 two long steps take most of the width. It reads as a graph more than a drawing. Keep it as an option; don't lead with it.
- The 14007 panel of image 5. The arc labels are too small at sheet scale. The `places` view with COUNTERWEIGHT and COVER written on the drawing is the "legend on the sheet" risk the brief named.

## Honest read against the bar

- **Would Ian keep it?** He would keep `meas_air` over 14007 and the river km pairing, I think. Both make a weird drawing readable without making it dull. They are thin and exact, they add one straight-line regime the drawing lacks, and they only make sense for *that* drawing. That is the round-10 quality.
- `meas_inventory` is the numbered atlas he called "amazing" at round-10 scale. At sheet scale it works but is busier. It is best with `by: step`.
- `meas_turning` is new and clear. But it is a chart, and it could become the next cliché if it gets into compositions. It belongs beside a drawing only.
- `meas_ledger` and `meas_arcs` are the most "process" of the set: they literally print what the composer did. That is valuable for the composition bench, as a readout of the substructure. As art they are tables, so use them beside only.
- 4507 was already at the edge of density. Any over-layer on it costs the void in the middle he liked. Lay devices beside it, never over it.
- I did not judge these against Ian's eye. Like every round, the lead's ranking should come after his notes.

## Proposed core changes (not made)

- `core.render_image` should honour `Ink.meta["colour"]`, so `kit.preview` shows devices in their inks. `experiments/meas_common.render` does it for now and accepts names and hex.
- The score contract could add one mark-level link, `Mark.meta["score_layer"] = <layer id>`. `meas_boxes` writes it; without it, `layer_marks` falls back to `part`, which is ambiguous for single-part composers like ccolour and cimpro.
- `revisit_nib` writes hex into `Ink.meta["colour"]`; `ccolour.render_pens` expects names and draws hex as black.
