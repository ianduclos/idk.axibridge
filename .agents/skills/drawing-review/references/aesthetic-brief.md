# Ian's direction

Potent abstraction, with the appearance of deliberation. Associations may be
barely nameable or a stretch; figuration is not required. Oehlen and early,
prefigurative AARON are contextual references, not techniques to reverse engineer
or standards a tiny plotter generator is expected to equal immediately.

Ian finds Oehlen's “Bad Paintings” an appealing name/concept but is unconvinced
that badness makes the paintings work. What reads as “bad” can prompt the desire
to invest thought. Something more intuitive holds the work together and sustains
that attention. He sees early AARON as an example of that coherence. Treat this
as Ian's aesthetic framing, not a settled art-historical account.

Working hypotheses: a line can become several things along its course; empty
space can have a particular shape; passages can have unequal visual authority;
local agreements can coexist with unresolved incompatibilities. None is a
required feature. Do not force every mark to justify itself or everything to
cohere in one story. Awkwardness may be necessary or merely weak: look closely.

For this instrument, perceptible novelty in the exchange is central. A response
should offer another move; explanatory sophistication is not enough. The machine
can respond to a human stroke, older material or a relation between them, or
ignore the latest intervention. Not every turn or drawing needs to be interesting.
Sheets of iterations, including duds and almost-promising stages, are useful
food for judgement rather than a failure rate to minimise.

The bench makes elements for later composition on the main canvas. Judge the
element's possibilities as well as the whole preview. A hard rectangular crop
may be an incidental failure of the work surface. Do not mistake that for a
chosen compositional border. The physical medium uses plotter paths; weight
requires physical marks. Texture/colour/opacity in reference paintings exceed
what this slice offers, but can inform a reading of relationships and authority.
